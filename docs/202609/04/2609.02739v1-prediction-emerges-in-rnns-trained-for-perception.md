## 摘要
大脑非常善于理解嘈杂而模糊的感觉输入。预测加工假说认为，这种能力依赖于预测。然而，目前尚不清楚大脑为何会进化出预测感觉世界这一计算代价高昂的过程来辅助知觉。在此，我们通过模拟论证：在为知觉而优化的系统中，预测会自然涌现。我们训练循环神经网络（RNN）在不同噪声水平下对巴赫作品的标记化版本进行去噪。随后，我们探究网络的状态是否包含关于下一标记的预测信息。我们通过冻结 RNN 的权重并训练一个从其状态进行预测的线性读出层来检验这一点。我们将该线性读出层的性能与独立训练的线性基准模型进行比较。结果表明，在中等噪声水平下，来自 RNN 的线性读出层优于基准模型，这表明这些网络依赖预测机制来支持知觉。我们进一步表明，RNN 对感觉输入的响应与预测误差成比例。总之，这些结果表明，预测加工的神经特征是在知觉优化过程中涌现的，无需任何额外的训练约束。

## Abstract
The brain is highly proficient at making sense of noisy and ambiguous sensory inputs. Predictive processing hypothesises that this ability relies on prediction. However, it is unclear why the brain would have evolved to predict the sensory world, a computationally expensive process, in order to aid perception. Here we use simulations to argue that prediction naturally emerges in systems optimised for perception. We train recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to denoise a tokenised version of Bach's compositions at a range of noise levels. Afterwards, we enquire whether the states of the networks contain predictive information about the next token. We test this by freezing the RNN weights and training a linear readout from its states on prediction. We compare the performance of the linear readout with that of an independently trained linear benchmark model. The results show that the linear readout from the RNNs outperforms the benchmark model at moderate levels of noise, indicating that the networks rely on a predictive mechanism to support perception. We further show that the responses of the RNNs to sensory inputs are proportional to prediction error. Together, the results demonstrate that neural signatures of predictive processing emerge, without any further training constraints, from optimisation of perception.
