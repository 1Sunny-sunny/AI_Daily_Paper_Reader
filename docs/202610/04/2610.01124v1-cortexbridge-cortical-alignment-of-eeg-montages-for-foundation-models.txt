# CortexBridge: Cortical Alignment of EEG Montages for Foundation Models 

Jiazhen Hong 1 Xiaotian Zhou 1 Zihao Ding 1 Kailong Wang 1 Yu Wu 11Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Rutgers University Piscataway, NJ 08854, USA 

## Abstract 

Electroencephalography (EEG) foundation models are often pretrained with a fixed channel vocabulary or a limited set of montages, making transfer difficult when electrode layouts change. We propose CortexBridge, a lightweight adapter that combines EEG features with electrode and atlas coordinates to map arbitrary montages into a shared cortical latent space. Evaluated with three frozen foundation models on five brain-computer interface (BCI) datasets from the Mother of All BCI Benchmarks (MOABB), CortexBridge improves performance in 13 of 15 evaluations. The gains in balanced accuracy average 0.80% for EEGPT, 0.70% for LaBraM, and 3.26% for CBraMod, with a maximum gain of 13.02% on 12-class steady-state visual evoked potential (SSVEP) classification. Visualizations of the learned atlas representations reveal task-dependent spatial patterns, with SSVEP showing a more concentrated representation in the Yeo Visual network than auditory P300. These results establish cortical alignment as a learnable and anatomically grounded routing mechanism from heterogeneous EEG montages to pretrained foundation models. 

## 1 Introduction 

Recent advances in EEG representation learning have shifted the field from task-specific decoders toward self-supervised pretraining and large-scale foundation models [8, 1, 12, 7, 19, 20]. Despite this shift, most models still represent EEG in scalp-electrode space. Their spatial interfaces are therefore tied to the acquisition montage rather than to a shared anatomical representation. A key challenge is montage dependence: electrode number and placement vary across recordings, while many pretrained models rely on fixed or predefined channel interfaces [7, 19, 20]. Coordinate-based interfaces relax channel identity constraints but still operate in scalp space [4, 3, 2]. Classical source imaging maps scalp measurements to anatomy, but relies on explicit forward and inverse modeling [11, 14]. This motivates cortical alignment as a shared representation across heterogeneous montages for foundation models, distinct from biophysical source estimation. We propose CortexBridge, a lightweight adapter that combines EEG features with electrode and atlas coordinates to map arbitrary montages into a shared cortical latent space. CortexBridge learns sparse electrode-to-ROI routing, models multi-scale interactions among cortical regions, and injects the resulting representation into a frozen foundation model through a residual interface. Evaluated with three frozen foundation models on five MOABB datasets, CortexBridge improves performance in 13 of 15 evaluations. Our contributions are: • We introduce cortical alignment as a learnable interface for adapting heterogeneous EEG montages to pretrained foundation models.

## 2 Related Work 

EEG representation learning. Early EEG models such as EEGNet and EEG Conformer learn task-specific spatial and temporal representations [9, 18]. Self-supervised methods including BENDR, MAEEG, and EEG2Rep instead learn reusable representations through contrastive or masked objec-tives [8, 1, 12]. More recent foundation models broaden pretraining scale and architecture: MMM incorporates geometric structure [23], BIOT uses frequency-aware biosignal tokens [21], LaBraM predicts masked neural codes [7], EEGPT aligns spatial and temporal representations [19], and CBraMod models spatial and temporal dependencies in parallel [20]. 

Montage interfaces. Existing models handle electrode layouts in several ways (Figure 1). Fixed-layout methods assume consistent channel slots across recordings, while vocabulary-driven methods identify electrodes from a predefined channel set. Coordinate-driven models such as NeurIPT and REVE instead encode electrode positions directly and can therefore accommodate previously unseen locations [3, 2]. However, all three approaches ultimately retain a scalp-indexed representation. CortexBridge differs by mapping each montage to the same atlas-indexed cortical space before interfacing with the pretrained backbone. 

Cortical representations. Channel selection and interpolation can reconcile input dimensions across montages but remain entirely in sensor space. EEG source imaging instead estimates cortical activity using a forward model and regularized inverse operator [11, 14]. CortexBridge serves a different purpose: it uses cortical anatomy as a representation prior and learns electrode-to-ROI routing directly for downstream adaptation. The Schaefer atlas and Yeo networks provide a common cortical organization [16, 22], while the resulting representation remains a learned latent space rather than a biophysical source estimate.

## 4 Experiments 

Datasets. We evaluate on five public datasets from MOABB [6], spanning motor imagery, P300, and SSVEP paradigms. Liu2024 contains left- and right-hand motor imagery from 50 acute stroke patients [10]. BNCI2014-008 contains visual P300 data from eight participants with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis [15], while BNCI2015-003 uses an auditory P300 spelling task [17]. Nakanishi2015 is a 12-class SSVEP dataset [13], and Huebner2017 contains visual P300 spelling data [5]. None of these datasets appears in the published pretraining sets of EEGPT, LaBraM, or CBraMod [7, 19, 20]. 

Preprocessing and protocol. We use the event-specific windows defined by MOABB, apply a 0.5– 45 Hz band-pass filter, and resample all datasets to 200 Hz. Additional model-specific resampling and padding are applied when required by each backbone’s patch structure. Normalization statistics are computed from training subjects only. We use subject-grouped three-fold cross-validation, with test subjects held out from both training and validation. Balanced accuracy is reported for all experiments. Dataset details are provided in Appendix A. 

Baselines and training. We evaluate EEGPT, CBraMod, and LaBraM on all five datasets. The raw baseline uses a learned channel projection, while CortexBridge augments this interface with the cortical path. Paired runs use identical subject folds, initialization, classifier, and training schedule. CortexBridge adds approximately 0.10M trainable parameters. Full training and implementation details are provided in Appendix B.

Transfer across EEG paradigms. The largest gain occurs on Nakanishi2015, where CortexBridge improves CBraMod by 13.02% and EEGPT by 3.40% on 12-class SSVEP. This result suggests that mapping sparse scalp measurements to a structured cortical representation is particularly useful for its eight-channel montage. All three models improve on BNCI2015-003 auditory P300, and smaller gains extend to Liu2024 motor imagery. The two decreases are limited to 0.96% for EEGPT on BNCI2014-008 and 0.11% for LaBraM on Huebner2017. The gains across motor imagery, P300, and SSVEP show that the learned routing is not limited to one EEG paradigm. 

Task-dependent cortical representations. Figure 3 compares atlas representations learned from two eight-channel datasets using the same backbone and adapter. SSVEP is elicited by periodic visual stimulation, and its representation is more concentrated in regions associated with the Yeo Visual network. This network captures the locally coupled organization of visual cortex in the seven-network parcellation [16, 22]. This correspondence suggests that CortexBridge retains task-relevant cortical organization. The maps show learned ROI representations rather than biophysical source estimates. 

## 6 Conclusion 

We introduced CortexBridge, a lightweight adapter that maps heterogeneous scalp EEG montages to an atlas-indexed cortical representation through signal- and coordinate-aware electrode-to-ROI routing. Across EEGPT, LaBraM, and CBraMod, CortexBridge improves 13 of 15 evaluations on five unseen datasets, with a maximum gain of 13.02% on 12-class SSVEP. The learned ROI representations retain task-dependent spatial organization, with SSVEP concentrated in the Yeo Visual network. These results show that CortexBridge improves adaptation to unseen montages and provides a common, anatomically indexed interface across EEG foundation models. 5References

## C Limitations 

Our evaluation covers five public datasets, three EEG paradigms, and three foundation models. Future work will extend CortexBridge to more recording systems, participant populations, and model architectures. Evaluation on larger mixed-montage collections and different atlas resolutions will further establish its generality. Theoretical analysis of the learned cortical mapping is another direction for future work. 8
