## 摘要
表征时间信息的能力常被假设为生物神经网络效率和学​​习能力的基础。这推动了脉冲神经网络的发展。然而，时间信息究竟如何在神经网络中被表示，以及不同形式的时间表示如何影响性能和计算效率，仍不清楚。我们引入神经网络时间表示程度的概念，由其组成函数（激活、学习、编码和解码）是否显式地将时间作为计算变量来操纵而定义。这一视角产生了一个连续谱，从非时间表示（时间不作为网络函数中的显式计算变量出现）到完全时间表示（计算显式地作用于时间变量）。使用固定的深度架构，我们比较了具有递增时间表达力的不同编码、激活机制和解码：通常人工神经网络中使用的非时间加权和激活、脉冲神经网络中使用的非时间二值脉冲激活，以及延迟神经网络中使用的基于脉冲时间的激活。在事件数据集上的实验表明，提高时间表示程度可以同时改善性能和计算效率，在 Google Speech Commands 数据集上达到了最先进性能。我们进一步分析了时间表示如何诱导不同的前向和后向计算轨迹，导致梯度显式依赖于事件时间的历史。

## Abstract
The ability to represent temporal information is often hypothesized to underlie the efficiency and learning capabilities of biological neural networks. This motivates the development of spiking neural networks. However, it remains unclear how temporal information is actually represented in neural networks and how different forms of temporal representation affect performance and computational efficiency. We introduce the notion of the degree of temporal representation of a neural network, defined by whether its constituent functions (activation, learning, encoding, and decoding) explicitly manipulate time as a computational variable. This perspective yields a continuum ranging from non-temporal representations, where time does not explicitly appear as a computational variable in the network functions, to fully temporal representations, where computations explicitly operate on temporal variables. Using a fixed deep architecture, we compare different encoding, activation mechanisms and decoding with increasing temporal expressiveness: non-temporal weighted-sum activations used in usual artificial neural networks, non-temporal binary spiking activations used in spiking neural networks, and spike-time-based activations used in delay neural networks. Experiments on event-based datasets show that increasing the degree of temporal representation improves both performance and computational efficiency, allowing state-of-the-art performances on the Google Speech Commands dataset. We further analyze how temporal representations induce distinct forward and backward computational trajectories, leading to gradients that depend explicitly on the history of event times.
