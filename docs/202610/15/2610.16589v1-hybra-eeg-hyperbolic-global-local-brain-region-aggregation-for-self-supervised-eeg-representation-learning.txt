# HyBRA-EEG: Hyperbolic Global-Local Brain-Region Aggregation for Self-Supervised EEG Representation Learning 

# Xin Liu ∗, Feng Lu ∗, Wei Li ∗

Abstract 

Electroencephalography (EEG) representation learning is challenged by noisy signals, inter-subject variability, and heterogeneous electrode montages in different datasets and recording settings. Recent self-supervised EEG pretraining methods improve transferability by converting heterogeneous recordings into unified token or sequence representations, but channel-aware designs may still encode montage- or dataset-specific regularities. We propose HyBRA, a self-supervised framework based on hyperbolic global-local brain-region ag-gregation. HyBRA maps heterogeneous channel observations into a fixed regional token space by combining electrode coor-dinates, region priors, and feature-dependent hyperbolic dis-tances. A channel-evidence injection branch preserves fine-grained discriminative cues during aggregation. The result-ing tokens are processed by a Region-Time Transformer and pretrained with masked reconstruction of latent features and region-level signals. HyBRA outperforms the compared meth-ods in average balanced accuracy on six benchmark datasets. Under a controlled six-fold evaluation on OpenBMI-MI, Hy-BRA matches CSBrain in balanced accuracy, improves AU-ROC by 1.84 percentage points and AUPRC by 5.23 per-centage points, and uses 96.8% fewer parameters. Matched comparisons on TUAB and TUEV show higher balanced ac-curacy for hyperbolic matching than for Euclidean distance or cosine similarity. 

# Introduction 

Electroencephalography (EEG) provides a non-invasive view of brain activity and supports applications in clinical EEG analysis and brain–computer interfaces (Daly and Wolpaw 2008; Cohen 2017). Transfer between datasets remains diffi-cult under subject and acquisition variability, local wave-form distortion, and montage-dependent changes in the spatial sampling of scalp potentials (Jurcak, Tsuzuki, and Dan 2007; Wu, Xu, and Lu 2022). Recent self-supervised models, including BIOT (Yang, Westover, and Sun 2023), EEGPT (Wang et al. 2024), LaBraM (Jiang, Zhao, and Lu 2024), CBraMod (Wang et al. 2025a), and CSBrain (Zhou et al. 2025), learn transferable representations from large EEG corpora while accommodating heterogeneous record-ing formats. A channel token from one dataset may have no direct counterpart in another, and similarly named electrodes may exhibit different signal rela-tionships under different reference schemes or montage de-signs (Qin, Xu, and Yao 2010; Chella et al. 2016). This mis-match also affects task transfer. Motor imagery emphasizes sensorimotor rhythm modulation (Pfurtscheller and Lopes da Silva 1999), mental arithmetic involves workload-related os-cillatory patterns (Chikhi, Matton, and Blanchet 2022), and clinical detection tasks focus on abnormal waveforms, event-specific patterns, or ictal activity (Gemein et al. 2020; Obeid and Picone 2016; Fisher, Scharfman, and deCurtis 2014). A representation tied to the source channels may entangle task-relevant neural patterns with dataset-specific spatial regu-larities, limiting transfer to different EEG paradigms (Lotte et al. 2018; Wu, Xu, and Lu 2022). A fixed region-token space provides a more consistent rep-resentation under montage variation. Individual electrodes measure local scalp potentials and remain sensitive to dis-arXiv:2610.16589v1 [cs.LG] 12 Oct 2026 placement and local noise (Nielsen et al. 2023; Miljevic et al. 2022). Task-related EEG patterns often involve several elec-trodes (Schaworonkow and Nikulin 2022), and region to-kens consolidate this distributed evidence without changing the representation structure when the channel configuration changes. This regional organization still requires selective aggregation. Uniform pooling cannot represent non-uniform channel contributions to the same region (Figure 1, Top), and many-to-one aggregation may attenuate local waveforms that support downstream prediction. HyBRA addresses these lim-itations with feature-dependent channel-to-region matching and a direct channel-evidence pathway (Figure 1, Bottom). We propose HyBRA, a self-supervised EEG framework based on hyperbolic global-local brain-region aggregation. HyBRA combines short-term channel features with electrode coordinates and maps the resulting channel tokens into the fixed region-token space with the Hyperbolic Region Mixer. A brain-region prior defines admissible channel-region asso-ciations, and feature-dependent hyperbolic distance adjusts the relative weight of each candidate channel. The resulting region-token space forms the global component of HyBRA. The complementary channel-evidence pathway forms the local component by drawing on the pre-aggregation channel features and injecting weighted channel-level evidence into each region token. The fused region tokens enter a Region-Time Transformer that models temporal dynamics within regions and interactions among regions. The self-supervised objective reconstructs latent features and region-level signals at masked region-time positions. HyBRA introduces a region-centered pretraining scheme in which the regional pathway constructs a fixed region-token space and the channel-evidence pathway preserves channel-level evidence. Hyperbolic matching adapts channel contri-butions to each region, and evidence injection limits infor-mation loss during aggregation. HyBRA achieves the highest mean balanced accuracy on each of six downstream datasets. Controlled comparisons on TUAB and TUEV show higher mean balanced accuracy for hyperbolic matching than for parameter-matched Euclidean and cosine variants. 

# Related Work 

Recent self-supervised EEG models, including BIOT (Yang, Westover, and Sun 2023), EEGPT (Wang et al. 2024), LaBraM (Jiang, Zhao, and Lu 2024), CBraMod (Wang et al. 2025a), and CSBrain (Zhou et al. 2025), learn transfer-able representations from large EEG corpora and support downstream adaptation. These models handle montage vari-ation using architecture-specific input adaptation, flexible to-kenization, or learned channel embeddings. A recent bench-mark reports that channel adaptation remains sensitive to the model architecture and training regime (Kokate et al. 2026). HyBRA differs at the representation level by mapping the observed channels into a fixed region-token space before region-time modeling. Region-aware EEG models have established regional or-ganization as a useful spatial representation. LGGNet (Ding et al. 2024) aggregates electrodes within local regions be-fore modeling inter-region relations. R2G-STLT (Cheng et al. 2024) constructs regional patches for spatiotemporal learning. LAG (Gao et al. 2024) derives higher-level topol-ogy from local functional connectivity. ALGGNet (Wang et al. 2025b) studies dataset-specific regional partitions us-ing adaptive clustering. These studies support the value of regional modeling. HyBRA differs by refining prior-defined channel–region associations from the current channel fea-tures of each sample. Regional aggregation may also atten-uate channel-level evidence, which HyBRA retains with a channel-evidence pathway. Hyperbolic representation learning offers a non-Euclidean geometry for structured and non-uniform relations (Nickel and Kiela 2017; Ganea, Bécigneul, and Hofmann 2018). HEEGNet (Li et al. 2026) reports geometric properties of EEG signals and learned representations that are compatible with hyperbolic modeling. Other hyperbolic EEG studies op-erate on complete sample representations or preconstructed connectivity networks (Chang et al. 2025; Guillemaud et al. 2025). HyBRA uses feature-dependent hyperbolic distance for channel-to-region matching within a brain-region prior. Its channel-evidence pathway preserves channel-level evi-dence during aggregation.

HyBRA performs masked region-time modeling by sampling a binary mask M ∈ { 0, 1}B×K×P over the position-aware tokens Z0 from Eq. 9. Each masked token is replaced by a learnable token m ∈ RD :

Predictions are evaluated only at masked positions. At each masked position, Lfeat is the cosine loss between the predicted feature and the pre-mask token at that posi-tion. The target is detached after LayerNorm and then ℓ2-normalized. Lraw is the reconstruction loss for a normalized region-level signal derived from the input patches using the channel-to-region prior. # Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

Datasets. Table 1 summarizes the seven public subsets used for pretraining. They cover steady-state visual evoked po-tential (SSVEP) (Liu et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2017), motor imagery (MI) (Cho et al. 2017; Schalk 2009), and emotion recognition (EMO) (Chen et al. 2023; Li et al. 2019). The montage and segment length vary across datasets. All sig-nals are resampled to 200 Hz. The six downstream datasets cover motor imagery, clinical EEG analysis, and mental workload. OpenBMI-MI (Lee et al. 2019) uses six subject-group folds, and BCIC-IV 2a (Tangermann et al. 2012) fol-lows a nine-fold leave-one-subject-out protocol. TUAB and TUEV (Obeid and Picone 2016) use their official test sets. Siena (Detti 2020) uses 14 patient-level folds, while Menta-lArithmetic (Zyma et al. 2019) uses six subject-group folds. All held-out test sets are subject- or patient-disjoint from the corresponding training data. Appendix E describes the preprocessing and split construction. 

Pretraining Setup. HyBRA is pretrained for 200 epochs on eight Huawei Ascend 910B3 NPUs with a batch size of 32 per device. AdamW uses a peak learning rate of 3 × 10 −4 and a weight decay of 1×10 −4. The learning rate increases linearly for five epochs and follows a cosine schedule to 1 × 10 −6.The mask ratio is 0.4, and λraw is 0.5. Appendix F lists the software versions and remaining settings.

Main Results 

Baselines and Evaluation Metrics. We compare HyBRA with four pretrained EEG models: BIOT, EEGPT, LaBraM, and CSBrain. TUAB and TUEV also include task-specific baselines. Balanced accuracy (BAcc) is the primary metric as it averages recall across classes. Secondary metrics follow each dataset’s evaluation setting and are listed in Table 2. 

Performance Comparison. Table 2 shows that HyBRA achieves the highest mean BAcc on all six downstream datasets. Its macro-averaged BAcc is 0.7278, giving rela-tive improvements of 5.03%, 8.48%, 15.29%, and 18.29% over the corresponding macro averages of CSBrain, LaBraM, BIOT, and EEGPT. Against the strongest pretrained base-line on each dataset, the BAcc gains are 2.37, 1.79, and 6.99 percentage points on TUEV, BCIC-IV 2a, and Men-talArithmetic. The margins on TUAB, OpenBMI-MI, and Siena are smaller but remain positive. On OpenBMI-MI, HyBRA obtains a comparable BAcc to CSBrain ( 81 .17% 

versus 81 .04% ) and improves AUROC and AUPRC by 1.84 and 5.23 percentage points. HyBRA also leads every re-ported metric on TUEV, BCIC-IV 2a, and MentalArithmetic. LaBraM retains the highest AUROC on TUAB and Siena. The BAcc results show gains across both clinical and BCI datasets without implying uniform improvements on every secondary metric.

Ablation Study 

We evaluate the components of HyBRA on TUAB and TUEV using the same data splits, random seeds, and training proto-cols. Table 5 shows that every variant has a lower mean BAcc than the full model. The larger reductions on TUEV suggest greater sensitivity to spatial modeling and the pretraining objective under this event-classification setting. 

Coordinate-Aware Patch Encoding. Removing coordinate encoding lowers BAcc by 1.67 percentage points on TUAB and 5.85 points on TUEV. Electrode coordinates provide use-ful spatial information before channel-to-region matching. 

Hyperbolic Region Mixer. Replacing hyperbolic matching with parameter-matched Euclidean or cosine variants low-ers mean BAcc on both datasets. The variant without brain-region tokenization is a structural tokenization ablation and is not parameter-matched to HyBRA. It removes region aggre-gation, channel-evidence injection, and region embeddings, and applies the same axial Transformer and SSL objectives to the channel-time grid with per-channel reconstruction. Interpretability Analysis 

Region Attribution and Perturbation. We examine whether the region tokens produced by the Hyperbolic Re-gion Mixer carry differentiated evidence for downstream pre-dictions. Under the channel-to-region prior, the left-central (LC) and right-central (RC) tokens cover central scalp ar-eas (Pfurtscheller and Neuper 2001). For right- and left-hand imagery, LC and RC jointly account for 30.27% and 29.74% of the normalized absolute Integrated Gradients at-tribution (Sundararajan, Taly, and Yan 2017), respectively, as shown in Figure 3(a). This non-uniform pattern shows that the region tokens contribute differently to the predic-tion. The concentration of attribution in central scalp regions is spatially consistent with established sensorimotor patterns in hand motor imagery. As shown in Figure 3(b), we further test attribution faith-fulness through token perturbation (Samek et al. 2017). The model is frozen, and the selected region tokens in each trial are replaced with their fold-specific training-set means. Replacing the highest-attributed token reduces mean subject-level BAcc from 0.8117 to 0.7248. Random and lowest-attributed replacement retain BAcc values of 0.7923 and 0.8102, respectively. The decrease caused by highest-attributed replacement is 6.75 percentage points larger than that caused by random replacement. Replacing the five highest-attributed tokens further reduces BAcc to 0.6211, while replacing the five lowest-attributed tokens retains 0.7949. These results show that model predictions are more sensitive to highly attributed region tokens, supporting the faithfulness of the attribution ranking. Results under alterna-tive attribution targets are reported in Appendix H. 

Representation Space Visualization. We use t-SNE (van der Maaten and Hinton 2008) to compare the representations learned by the pretrained EEG models on BCIC-IV 2a. Figure 4 shows that the HyBRA embedding forms more compact within-class groups and clearer separation between motor-imagery classes. The other embeddings exhibit greater class mixing, although some clustering remains visible for BIOT. HyBRA also obtains the highest Silhouette coefficient of 0.200 and the lowest Davies–Bouldin index of 1.621 among the displayed repre-sentations. These visualization-based results are consistent with the stronger downstream performance of HyBRA on BCIC-IV 2a, while not serving as independent evidence of class separability in the original high-dimensional space. 

# Conclusion 

We presented HyBRA, a self-supervised EEG framework that maps montage-dependent channel observations to a fixed brain-region token space. Its hyperbolic global-local brain-region aggregation combines a montage-specific region prior with feature-dependent hyperbolic distance, while channel-evidence injection preserves information from the original channel tokens. HyBRA achieves the highest mean BAcc across six downstream datasets. The Euclidean and cosine variants on TUAB and TUEV further support the contri-bution of hyperbolic channel-to-region matching under the evaluated settings. Future work will examine adaptive re-gion construction and broader pretraining across centers and electrode configurations.
