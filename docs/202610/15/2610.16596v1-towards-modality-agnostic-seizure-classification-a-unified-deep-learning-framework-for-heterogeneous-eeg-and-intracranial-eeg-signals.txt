# Towards Modality-Agnostic Seizure Classification: A Unified Deep Learning Framework for Heterogeneous EEG and Intracranial EEG Signals 

## Ramya Sundaram, Member, IEEE , Muhammad Aslam Jarwar, Senior Member, IEEE , M Uma , Sunder Ali Khowaja, Senior Member, IEEE and Qurat-Ul-Ain Mastoi 

Abstract — Seizure prediction and detection, essential for clini-cal treatment of epilepsy, remain the crucial task of this neurolog-ical disease. EEG and iEEG are widely used to analyze epilepsy due to its capability of capturing discriminative brain activities at high time resolution. However, existing works generally analyze either EEG or iEEG seizure activity separately, where EEG analysis has been well-researched, while more and more works consider iEEG for its higher specificity at the spatial level and signal quality. Currently, most of the existing seizure prediction systems are single-modal in nature, which limits its application to a variety of clinical conditions. Therefore, a common framework to analyze seizures with both EEG and iEEG signals needs to be developed. This paper presents a new framework of multimodal seizure clas-sification integrating the heterogeneous EEG and iEEG recordings from different subjects and sources. A specific framework, called Unified Multimodal Signal Harmonization (UMSH), is developed for harmonizing signals with varied sampling rates and recording con-ditions. Moreover, to handle the extremely class-imbalanced prob-lem in the data sets, we use a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (cGAN) based data augmentation technique to generate synthetic samples to balance the class distribution. The proposed framework uses the EEGNet-Vision Transformer (EEGNet-ViT) as the feature extraction and classification network to learn the local discriminative patterns as well as global contextual information in the multimodal seizure data. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework for robust seizure classification, emphasizing the potential of integrated EEG-iEEG multimodal learning in advancing next-generation epilepsy moni-toring and clinical decision-support systems. 

Index Terms — CGAN, EEGNet-Transformer model, In-tracranial EEG, ictal, Non-Seizure, preictal, Synthetic data.

II. R ELATED WORKS 

The automatic detection and classification of seizures has attracted a lot of attention in recent years. Many researchers used different machine learning and deep learning approaches to classify brain signals related to seizures. The most part of the research used EEG signals for seizure analysis, while the amount of research related to iEEG was relatively small. To gain knowledge about these methods, it is necessary to understand the recent trends and limitations. Hence, in this section, the existing work related to automated seizure detection and classification using both EEG and iEEG is reviewed, especially the machine learning and deep learning approaches utilized. 

A. EEG-Based Seizure Detection and Classification 

EEG-based seizure detection and classification have been widely studied using various machine learning and deep learning techniques [13]. Among the publicly available datasets, the CHB-MIT [11] and University of Bonn datasets [14] are the most commonly used due to their availability and well-annotated seizure recordings. Additionally, the Temple University Hospital EEG Seizure Corpus (TUSZ) [15], one of the largest clinical EEG datasets, is frequently utilized for large-scale and cross-subject seizure classification studies. In machine learning-based seizure detection and classification studies, hand-crafted features extracted from EEG signals are commonly employed for model development. Researchers have extensively investigated time-domain, frequency-domain, and time–frequency-domain feature extraction techniques [5] to capture seizure-related characteristics and improve classification performance. While some studies have focused on single-channel EEG [9] analysis to reduce computational complex-ity, others have explored channel selection strategies [16] to identify the most informative EEG channels and enhance classification ac-curacy. Notably, using GWO-based optimization of features along with hybrid SVM-Fuzzy classification enhanced the performance for the diagnosis of seizures [17], and the use of Mutual information and Random Forest-based channel selection using DWT and SVM allowed the detection of seizures in a computationally efficient way [16]. Additionally, CNN-enhanced feature learning and epileptic state classification were combined with dynamic channel selection, proving the efficacy of optimal channel selection techniques in seizure detection [18]. Many studies have developed hybrid frameworks that use feature selection [19], feature fusion [20], and sophisticated classification methods to enhance seizure detection performance [21]. Particularly, a CNN-LSTM-based [22] model was created to extract spatial and temporal information from DWT-derived EEG sub-bands, with excellent detection performance across the TUSZ, Bonn, and CHB-MIT datasets. A multiple-input deep feature learning network (MDFLN) [23] learning both the time and the time-frequency domain features was presented, and BLSTM was employed for seizure classification, where the proposed network performance surpassed that achieved using a single view feature representation. Moreover, EfficientNet-B0-based [24] CNN combined with the ensemble SVM voting method trained with STFT and channel correlation features demonstrates an effective approach for reliable prediction, achieving comparable accuracy to the existing research with CHB-MIT data. Besides these binary detection approaches, several works have inves-tigated multiclass seizure classification to distinguish among different seizure types and thus improve the clinical utility of the TUSZ dataset [25]. 

B. iEEG-Based Seizure Detection and Classification 

Besides methods based on scalp EEG, a number of research groups have explored iEEG signals for the purpose of seizure detection and prediction due to their better signal quality and spatial resolution. Recent surveys indicated that machine learning and deep learning methods for the detection of epileptogenic zones using iEEG signals are receiving wide attention [26]. In order to achieve the improvement of seizure detection accuracy on long-term iEEG data, Laelaps (a hyperdimensional computing-based approach) was proposed [27], and a high detection rate with the lowest energy and no false alarms was achieved. To develop a patient-independent seizure detection system, transformer-based and multi-task learning models have been studied [28], and the proposed models showed high generalization ability on the patients and datasets. For the issue of low-resource devices, a lightweight CNN model with an attention mechanism has been designed for seizure detection in iEEG signals [29]. For epilepsy state classification using iEEG, CNN, CNN-SVM, and hybrid CNN-based models have been developed [30]. Moreover, a convolutional-transformer-based model has been studied for effectively utilizing inter-channel information and to obtain an interpretable model in seizure detection based on continuous long-term iEEG recordings [8]. According to the current literature, most seizure detection and prediction investigations have used either scalp EEG or iEEG data independently. Despite substantial advances in both fields, no study has extensively studied a single system capable of managing both RAMYA et al. : TOWARDS MODALITY-AGNOSTIC SEIZURE CLASSIFICATION: A UNIFIED DEEP LEARNING FRAMEWORK FOR HETEROGENEOUS EEG AND INTRACRANIAL EEG SIGNALS 3

EEG and iEEG inputs concurrently. This restriction is mostly due to fundamental differences between EEG and iEEG recordings, such as variances in signal properties, electrode topologies, spatial resolution, noise levels, and sampling frequencies. Hence, it is difficult to create a generalized model that is efficient to learn both modalities of signal. This research gap is addressed in this study by developing a unified seizure classification framework for the EEG and iEEG signals. The proposed approach attempts to increase the generalizability and flexibility of seizure detection systems, allowing a single model to function across several recording modalities and assisting in the creation of more flexible epilepsy monitoring systems. 

III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY 

In this work, a single framework for seizure classification using both scalp EEG and iEEG signals is presented. The proposed method integrates the EEG and iEEG datasets and forms a generalized seizure classification model that can accommodate both recording modalities. Since EEG and iEEG signals have distinct differences in their characteristics, channel setup, and sampling frequencies, the proposed methodology first uses a specialized pre-processing tech-nique, namely, Unified Multimodal Signal Harmonization (UMSH), to convert EEG and iEEG signals into similar representations for downstream tasks. The merger of these two datasets also forms a strongly imbalanced classification problem, which is dealt with using an adequate balancing method. After preprocessing and balancing the data, features are extracted and classified using the hybrid EEGNet and ViT models to exploit discriminative patterns relevant to seizures. B. Unified Multimodel Signal Harmonization 

The proposed UMSH framework proposed here consists of two main stages, iEEG Signal Harmonization and EEG Signal Harmo-nization, for the two modalities to standardize the heterogeneous natures of both and provide a uniform representation for multimodal seizure classification. 

1) iEEG Signal Harmonization: The multicentre Clinic iEEG dataset is a set of 3-second-length signals with a sample frequency of 5000 Hz, whereas the CHB-MIT EEG dataset is sampled with 256 Hz. It would be impossible to fuse both datasets directly, as the temporal resolution difference is massive. First, the signals from the iEEG dataset were transformed to be compatible with EEG data. The original 5000 Hz signals were resampled to 256 Hz with Resample Poly (upsampling 32 and downsampling 625) to maintain the signal characteristics. C. Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (CGAN)-Based Data Balancing 

As is clear from Table III, the class distribution in the joint EEG-iEEG dataset is highly imbalanced, with a significantly larger number of samples in the non-Seizure class than the Ictal and Preictal classes. This imbalance in distribution may cause the learner to be biased towards the majority class, and lead to a low detection performance with bad generalization abilities to the seizure-related classes. It is necessary to apply a suitable data balancing method to equalize this class distribution before the classification phase. A Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (CGAN) is utilized to produce synthetic samples for the minority classes as a solution to the problem described. Instead of standard oversampling techniques, the CGAN produces class-specific synthetic samples by conditioning the network using the class labels in the training. Thus, the CGAN learns the distribution of each class and generates authentic synthetic EEG and iEEG samples and preserves characteristics of each class. The CGAN framework includes two adversarial networks: the generator (G) and the discriminator (D). The generator takes as input the noise vector z and class label y and generates a fake sample, whereas the discriminator takes as input the combination of real and generated samples along with their class labels, and tries to discriminate between real and fake samples. By using adversarial learning, the generator can learn to generate better fake minority class samples. The objective function of CGAN is:

In the proposed framework, we employ CGAN to over-sample the minority classes, i.e., Ictal and Preictal classes, until a balanced class distribution is obtained. By introducing artificial samples, we enhance the data variability and decrease the data skew due to class imbalance. As a result, the class-balanced data allows the subsequent classifier to learn more distinctive seizure-related representations, which ul-timately contribute to improving the robustness and generalization ability of the classification model. D. Improved EEGNet-ViT architecture for multimodal classification 

The harmonized and class-balanced standard EEG and iEEG sig-nals are given to the EEGNet architecture as input feature extraction after being normalized. The reasons EEGNet was chosen as the first feature extraction module were its lightweight structure, computation-efficient design, and its ability to learn spatial-temporal features from electrophysiological signals effectively. Compared to a normal convolution operation, it greatly reduces the number of trainable parameters. Batch Normalization and ELU activation are applied once more. Then, to reduce the number of feature dimensions and remove the redundant information, an Average Pooling layer with a pooling size (4,1) is applied, so that the dimension of the feature representation is decreased from 3072 samples to 768 samples in the temporal domain. After that, a dropout layer with a dropout rate of 0.4 is implemented to overcome overfitting and enhance model generalization performance. Finally, the compressed feature maps are then fed to a Separable Convolution layer with F2=32 filters and the size (16,1). Separable convolution separately does the depth-wise and pointwise convolu-tions, thus making it computationally more efficient with preserving discriminative information from feature maps. This layer extracts and updates the temporal-spatial information that are learned from EEG and iEEG signals. These feature maps are normalized and activated with Batch Normalization and ELU activation functions. Subsequently, a second Average Pooling layer is introduced with a pooling size of (8,1), reducing the feature dimension from 768 to 96 temporal tokens. A dropout layer is then applied to enhance robustness and avoid overfitting of the model. The resultant feature dimension at this point is 9632, and the features have concise yet highly informative spatio-temporal features of the seizure. The extracted features are subsequently flattened into a sequential representation, where a Lambda layer reshapes the feature maps, and a TimeDistributed Dense layer projects each feature vector into a 64-dimensional embedding space ( embed dim = 64 ). This embedding projection converts the EEGNet-derived feature maps into a compact token sequence while preserving the inherent temporal and spatial information. The generated token embeddings are then fed into the Vision Transformer (ViT) to capture global contextual information and long-range dependencies effectively. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we introduced a new framework to classify seizure based on multimodal EEG and iEEG signals, thereby to overcome the shortcomings of single modality seizure detection systems. Developed a UMSH framework to normalize heterogeneous EEG/iEEG data recorded with different sampling rates and properties. To overcome the class imbalance issue, a cGAN-based data augmentation was applied for diversifying the number of classes involved. Besides that, a hybrid EEGNet-Vision Transformer (EEGNet-ViT) model was used for learning local and global patterns associated with seizure. According to the experimental results, the proposed framework was successful to classify seizures robustly. Also, the ablation study revealed the merit of the multimodal data fusion approach. For EEG Training-iEEG Testing and iEEG Training-EEG Testing setup, the classification accuracy was found to be 95.45% and 94.67% respectively. But the model trained and tested using both EEG and iEEG signals gave the highest accuracy of 98.63%. This result showed that the multimodal EEG and iEEG signals could represent more information related to seizure, thus facilitating the model to learn more sophisticated and discriminative representations compared to single-modality settings. The proposed method provided superiority to current methods and increased classification accuracy and reli-ability. It proved the feasibility of joint EEG-iEEG learning for future epilepsy monitoring and clinical decision making, indicating a favourable prospect in future multimodal seizure analysis stud-ies.
