Abstract 

EEG foundation models (EFMs) have improved EEG representation learning by transferring knowledge from large-scale multi-subject pretraining to downstream tasks. However, their pretrained embeddings often retain substantial subject-related variation, placing an additional burden on the downstream classifier, especially in cross-subject settings. To address this issue, we propose DISENTANGLED EFM 

FINE -TUNING (DEFT ), a plug-and-play adapter for EFM fine-tuning. DEFT 

encourages branch disentanglement of pretrained tokens into task-oriented content and subject-consistent style, then performs downstream prediction from the content branch. It instantiates this factorize-before-classify design with recursive Gaussian refinement, temporal-variance regularization, subject-aware contrastive learning, and reconstruction. Experiments across multiple EEG datasets and four EFM backbones show that DEFT improves matched fine-tuning baselines. Further ablations and analyses support the intended content/style branch organization. 

1 Introduction 

Electroencephalography (EEG) provides high-temporal-resolution signals for a wide range of neural decoding tasks, such as emotion recognition [56, 4, 35], motor imagery [51, 48, 32], and sleep staging [22, 64, 62]. Recent EEG foundation models (EFMs) improve scalability by pretraining on large multi-subject corpora and learning reusable representations that transfer across downstream settings [66, 25, 43]. This paradigm has substantially improved the transferability and generalizability of EEG models [25, 43], and is reshaping standard downstream EEG fine-tuning practice. Probing reveals a consistent pattern across the evaluated settings: pretrained EFM embeddings make subject identity more linearly accessible. As shown in Figure 1, a probing classifier recovers subject identity more easily from pretrained EFM embeddings than from raw EEG features, while class-label probing improves only modestly after EFM encoding. EFM encoding can therefore make subject-related structure easier to separate in the pretrained token space. We use the compact factorization Z → (C, S) to summarize this mixed space, where Z denotes mixed pretrained tokens, 

C task-content tokens, and S subject-style tokens. Raw EEG already carries implicit subject traces, and EFM pretraining can sharpen these directions inside the mixed representation. EEG recordings are influenced by stable individual factors such as anatomy, functional organization, electrode-skin impedance, and recording habits [57, 47, 41, 2]. Current EFM embeddings consequently form mixed representations containing both task and subject structure.

This amplification exposes a structural limitation of current EFM fine-tuning: the strongest signal in a pretrained token may be the wrong signal for the downstream task. Figure 2 (left) illustrates this burden. If pretraining sharpens subject structure more than class structure, the downstream classifier inherits a representation that is rich but misaligned. With only task labels as supervision, the prediction head must first discount easily accessible subject structure and then recover class-relevant content from the remaining mixed representation. This coupling is especially undesirable in cross-subject settings , where subject cues are stable, easy to exploit, and poorly aligned with the desired decision rule. As a result, standard fine-tuning may spend part of its capacity correcting the pretrained representation before learning the classifier, which can obscure class-discriminative cues and weaken robustness in cross-subject EEG decoding [57, 49]. This failure mode raises a critical question: 

How can we prevent amplified subject-related variation in pretrained EFM embeddings from burdening downstream classification? 

To address this problem, we propose DISENTANGLED EFM FINE -TUNING (DEFT ), a post-backbone disentanglement adapter for EFM fine-tuning. 2 Preliminary 

2.1 EEG Foundation Model 

Let x ∈ RT ×Nch denote an EEG trial with T time steps and Nch channels. A pretrained EEG foundation model (EFM) backbone B(·) maps x to a token tensor 

Z = B(x) = {zm,w }M,W m=1 ,w =1 ∈ RM ×W ×D , (1) where M is the latent channel-group dimension, W is the number of temporal windows, and D is the token width. We denote this mixed pretrained representation by Z. EFMs are pretrained on large multi-subject EEG corpora to learn transferable token representations for downstream adaptation. 

2.2 EFM Fine-Tuning 

Let y ∈ Y denote the task label and u ∈ U denote the subject identity. Standard fine-tuning attaches a downstream classification head hcls (·) to the pretrained tokens and predicts 

ˆy = hcls (Z), Lcls = CE(ˆ y, y ). (2) In practice, hcls (·) is a lightweight prediction head that operates on pretrained tokens, typically through token aggregation followed by a linear or shallow MLP classifier. In this work, Z is treated as a mixed representation space. Each token may encode both class-relevant neural dynamics and subject-related stable patterns, which forces standard fine-tuning to optimize 

hcls (·) on a representation where the two factors remain coupled. We therefore use content and 

style as functional branch roles in the pretrained token space. Content tokens C retain label-relevant brain-state structure and its temporal variation for subject-disjoint prediction. Style tokens S collect trial-stable residual structure organized primarily by subject identity. The prediction head operates on the content branch, while temporal-stability and subject-aware constraints shape the style branch.

This section presents DISENTANGLED EFM FINE -TUNING (DEFT ), a post-backbone disentangle-ment adapter for EEG foundation model (EFM) fine-tuning.

3.2 Recursive Gaussian Disentangler 

Intuition: The Recursive Gaussian Disentangler performs iterative two-branch inference from counterpart-subtracted observations and temporal priors. It adapts the precision-weighted linear-Gaussian aggregation used in Xi-vector and the prior–observation fusion principle of Kalman filtering to pretrained EFM tokens [31, 26]. Learned diagonal precision controls the contribution of token dimensions whose reliability varies with artifacts, electrode contact, and nonstationary brain activity. Alternating counterpart subtraction and asymmetric static/dynamic transitions then refine content and style estimates. For each compact window ¯zw, we derive observation statistics and remove the current counterpart estimate rw:

ew = fμ(¯ zw), log Λ w = fλ(¯ zw), ˜ew = ew − γwrw, (6) 4where γw ∈ (0 , 1) is a learnable gate controlling counterpart removal. 

Observation 3.1. Let ew be the observation for window w, and let rw be the current estimate of the counterpart branch. If the branch update uses the counterpart-subtracted observation ˜ew = ew −γwrw

with γw ∈ (0 , 1) , then its inner product with the counterpart estimate is reduced by γw∥rw∥22.Alternating this update for the two branches therefore makes each branch estimate depend on evidence after subtracting the current counterpart estimate. The full Gaussian update equations are given in Appendix D.1. 

Derivation. For a branch update with counterpart estimate rw, direct substitution gives ⟨˜ew, r w⟩ =

⟨ew, r w⟩ − γw∥rw∥22. Thus, the observation used by the current branch has a smaller inner product with the counterpart estimate whenever rw̸ = 0 and γw > 0. Alternating the updates gives sw ←Gsta (ew − γwcw) and cw ← G dyn (ew − γwsw), so each branch is estimated after subtracting the current estimate of the other branch. Model Setting: We use the public released checkpoints of CodeBrain, CBraMod, LaBraM, and CSBrain and do not re-train the EFM pretraining stage. Following previous EEG foundation-model fine-tuning protocols, the pretrained EFM backbone, the downstream head, and any added adaptation modules are trainable during downstream training. The vanilla baseline optimizes the backbone and prediction head, while DEFT follows the same backbone update policy and jointly optimizes the disentanglement adapter. This keeps the comparison within the same checkpoint, split, and trainable encoder fine-tuning setting. The adapter adds only a small parameter overhead relative to the original fine-tuning model, including the pretrained backbone and downstream prediction head, as summarized in Appendix D.4. 5 Related Work 

EEG Foundation Models. Encoder-based EFMs such as BIOT, EEGPT, LaBraM, CBraMod, CodeBrain, and CSBrain pretrain reusable EEG encoders with masked reconstruction, neural token prediction, or structured spatiotemporal modeling objectives [58, 52, 25, 53, 43, 66]. Decoder-based or language-connected EFMs provide a complementary direction: NeuroLM connects EEG tokens to language-model instruction tuning, and ECHO casts EEG modeling as sequence-to-sequence prediction with support examples for contextual adaptation [24, 34]. For encoder-based EFMs used in downstream classification, the common adaptation recipe remains vanilla fine-tuning: attach a task head and update the pretrained backbone under task supervision. DEFT instead studies a tailored fine-tuning adapter for this encoder-based setting, factorizing mixed pretrained tokens before prediction while remaining applicable to EFMs that expose token embeddings. 

Disentanglement Learning. Disentangled representation learning separates factors such as content and style, stable and dynamic components, or identity and task information, with applications in speech and time-series representations [20, 33, 39, 45]. In EEG, patient-independent seizure detection has used adversarial or patient-invariant objectives to reduce patient-specific interference [16, 60, 61]. Existing approaches learn subject-invariant representations through adversarial, alignment, or sequential factorization objectives. DEFT applies factorization after a pretrained EFM backbone and assigns task prediction and subject consistency to separate token branches. 

6 Conclusion 

We introduce DEFT , a disentanglement-enhanced fine-tuning framework for EEG foundation models that targets a practical failure mode of EFM adaptation: pretrained embeddings can make subject-related structure highly accessible to the downstream classifier, even when that structure is not the intended target of cross-subject decoding. DEFT inserts a post-backbone factorize-before-classify adapter that decomposes the mixed representation into prediction-oriented content and subject-consistent style, then drives downstream prediction from the content branch. Experiments across multiple EEG task families and four EFM backbones show that DEFT improves matched fine-tuning baselines, while ablation, representation analysis, and content/style probing support the intended branch organization. Appendix checks cover one additional EFM, one independent MDD dataset, and one MOMENT/UCI HAR setting. Overall, these results support disentangling pretrained EFM embeddings for content-driven cross-subject EEG decoding. 9References

A.1 Scalp EEG Foundation Models 

EEG modeling has moved from task-specific supervised decoders toward reusable pretrained back-bones. Earlier deep pipelines, including compact CNNs, graph-based sleep staging, convolutional Transformers, dense Transformer variants, and variational spatiotemporal graph models, encode useful task-level inductive biases [30, 22, 50, 11, 36]. Self-supervised EEG representation learning further reduced annotation dependence through contrastive prediction, masked reconstruction, and informative masked modeling [28, 8, 44], setting the stage for larger-scale EEG pretraining. Recent EEG foundation models (EFMs) scale this paradigm across heterogeneous EEG corpora and task families [65, 59, 38]. Encoder-based EFMs such as BIOT, EEGPT, LaBraM, CBraMod, CodeBrain, and CSBrain learn transferable token representations with cross-data biosignal model-ing, masked signal reconstruction, neural token prediction, criss-cross spatial-temporal attention, decoupled tokenization, or cross-scale spatiotemporal modeling [58, 52, 25, 53, 43, 66]. Recent variants broaden this space with state-space backbones and mixture-of-experts routing [54, 19, 7, 15], large-scale or topology-agnostic pretraining [14, 12, 13], neuroscience-inspired priors [10, 55], and richer token interfaces such as vector quantization, time-frequency motifs, and residual quantization [18, 46, 9]. A related branch connects EEG representations with language or multimodal systems, including EEG-language foundation modeling, contextual sequence-to-sequence EEG modeling, instruction-style brain decoding, EEG-language alignment, and general-purpose sleep-staging interfaces [24, 34, 42, 23, 63]. These studies expand EFM capacity and interfaces, while downstream classification still commonly feeds pretrained EFM embeddings directly to a task head and optimizes in the mixed representation space. DEFT targets this adaptation stage by inserting a representation-level adapter between the EFM and prediction head, factorizing the mixed representation into content and style before classification. 

A.2 Disentanglement and Subject-Related EEG Variation 

Disentangled representation learning organizes latent variables according to factors of variation, with representative methods based on information-theoretic generative modeling, variational objectives, total-correlation penalties, and factorial latent distributions [6, 29, 5, 27]. Later analyses show that useful disentanglement depends on inductive biases, supervision, and downstream utility [40]. In sequential domains, FHVAE and DSAE separate sequence-level factors from segment-level dynamics, and later work isolates content/style factors in speech and time-series representations [20, 33, 39, 45]. 

DEFT extends factorized representation learning to downstream adaptation of pretrained EFM tokens. Subject-related variation is a central source of difficulty in EEG. Stable individual differences, intra-and inter-subject variability, and leakage from subject-specific patterns can strongly affect apparent generalization [47, 41, 2]. Existing EEG methods often seek invariant or aligned representations through transfer learning, domain-adversarial training, patient-adversarial objectives, subject-invariant contrastive learning, variational graph modeling, or coarse-to-fine cross-subject alignment [57, 16, 60, 61, 49, 37, 21]. 

DEFT operates on pretrained EFM embeddings and treats them as a mixed representation that may already amplify subject-related information. The content branch is optimized for downstream prediction and encouraged to preserve task-relevant temporal variation, while the style branch collects subject-consistent structure that remains stable within a trial. This yields a factorize-before-classify adaptation interface for EFMs. 

B Limitations, Ethics, and Reproducibility 

Limitations. DEFT introduces additional loss weights and an inductive temporal-variance prior, which are selected under the downstream validation protocol. For trait-level or demographic targets, validation selects the temporal-prior strength so stable label-relevant evidence remains in the content branch. A dataset with one sample per training subject provides no same-subject contrastive pairs; the classification, variance, and reconstruction objectives remain applicable. The downstream experiments reuse the processed EEG inputs from the corresponding foundation-model pipelines without adding a separate raw-signal preprocessing stage for DEFT . Across datasets, recordings are converted into fixed-duration EEG tensors with labels, subject identifiers, and split keys. Most samples are resampled to 200 Hz and represented as one-second temporal patches; dataset-appropriate filtering and notch removal follow the original dataset or backbone pipeline when raw signals are available. Backbone-specific preprocessing is limited to the model input interface. CodeBrain and CBraMod consume the shared LMDB or sequence-folder tensors after common amplitude scaling. CSBrain uses the same processed samples but reshapes them to match its tokenization convention; for BCIC-Speech, it uses the corrected 60-channel, three-window sample format. LaBraM additionally maps dataset channel names to its standard channel-index convention for channel embeddings. Thus, the comparisons use the same signal-level preprocessing whenever possible, while differences are limited to tensor shape, channel metadata, resampling rate, or tokenizer requirements.
