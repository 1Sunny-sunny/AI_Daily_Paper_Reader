# Modulation and integration in a local processor having input from two distinct sources

Abstract 

By making use of partial information decomposition, we analyse information transmission through a local processor which receives two streams of integrated inputs. We revise previously stated conditions for contextual modulation that are based on the components of partial infor-mation decomposition, and introduce a condition for the presence of signal integration. The conditional probability for the occurrence of an event, such as a spike or burst, is defined based on a general activation function of two distinct integrated inputs to the local processor. Exact results are obtained for information-theoretic measures and the components of partial informa-tion decomposition using the Ibroja method. These results are applied with several activation functions, of additive, multiplicative, subtractive and divisive forms with graphical illustration. We find that transfer functions based on multiplicative activation provide modulatory behaviour, but when the activation function is a simple product of the inputs this is not the case, and the behaviour is integrative. When the activation function is a simple addition of the two inputs we find by considering restricted domains that modulatory behaviour of two types is apparent. Fur-thermore, when both inputs are large and increasing then signal integration occurs in the form of increasing synergy and shared information. Similar signal integration occurs when the activa-tion function is a simple subtraction of the two inputs, and modulatory behaviour occurs in one of the restricted domains but not the other. A form of divisive normalisation transfer function is also considered and it turns out to be purely modulatory, with integrated input from neigh-bouring cells providing the modulation of the driving input. The revised information-theoretic conditions are also applied to noisy data obtained from a realistic simulation of a model layer 5b pyramidal cell in three distinct regimes defined by experimental design and characterised by the values of the input basal and apical amplitudes. Evidence for driving basal input with modulating apical input, driving apical input with modulating basal input, and of apical-basal signal integration is presented. 

Keywords: contextual modulation, signal integration, partial information decomposition, transfer function, layer 5b pyramidal cell, apical amplification, apical drive, synergy, subtractive inhibition, divisive normalisation 

# Introduction 

We consider a local processor which receives input from two distinct sites of integration, namely (a) a receptive field (RF) and (b) a contextual field (CF). Such a processor can be viewed as being part of an artificial neural network (Phillips et al., 1995) or as being a layer 5b pyramidal cell in 1arXiv:2610.05360v1 [cs.IT] 4 Oct 2026 which the sites of integration lie in the basal and apical dendrites, respectively (Larkum, 2013). In earlier work (Kay et al., 2017; Kay and Phillips, 2020), partial information decomposition (PID) (Williams and Beer, 2010) was used to separate out di ff erent components of the transmitted infor-mation. These components were employed in the definition of contextual modulation. The focus of the earlier work was decidedly asymmetric in that the integrated receptive field input was assumed to be driving and the only role for the contextual input was to amplify or attenuate the transmission of information. The possibility that the integrated contextual field input could be driving, and even be modulated by the integrated receptive field input, was ruled out. This stipulation was enforced by a specially-crafted modulatory activation function (Phillips et al., 1995). Use of partial information decomposition demonstrated that this and other modulatory activation functions enable particular forms of information processing that are very di ff erent from those obtained by employing activation functions that involve simple addition, subtraction, multiplication or divisive combinations of the two inputs to the processor (Kay and Phillips, 2020). For reviews of the occurrence of contextual modulation in neuroscience, see Silver (2010), Phillips et al. (2015) and Phillips (2017). Recent work (Graham et al., 2025), in which a reduced, but realistic, model of a layer 5b pyramidal cell was used to generate data regarding the occurrence of a burst, given basal and apical step currents of varying amplitudes, has included the possibility that a burst could be generated by (a) basal input alone, (b) apical input alone (c) a combination of basal and apical input – or not at all. These four distinct regimes were implemented by careful experimental design. In the sequel, we speak of drive and context rather than RF and CF and take a more neutral approach in which either of the two inputs, or both, can be driving and the other modulating. When we speak of “inputs” in the sequel we actually mean “integrated” inputs. Given two distinct binary inputs and a binary output, we define a class of transfer functions, in which each function maps the strengths of the two inputs to the probability of an event at the output. For a given transfer function and a fixed combination of signal strengths, a trivariate binary probability distribution is defined. Partial information decomposition is then applied to this probability distribution. This is repeated for many combinations of the signal strengths of the two inputs, and the PID components provide the pattern of information transmission in each case. We provide exact results for the Ibroja PID (Gri ffi th and Koch, 2014; Bertschinger et al., 2014) in which the PID components are obtained as continuous functions of the signal strengths of the two inputs. We also define a transfer function which implements a form of divisive normalisation (Carandini and Heeger, 2012), and we derive exact results for the Ibroja PID components also for this form of transfer function. We also revise the previous conditions for contextual modulation and define a condition for signal integration. By per-forming tests based on comparing various combinations of input signal strengths, these conditions are checked by employing a number of transfer functions of modulatory and integrative types. We also consider data on the occurrence of a burst for given levels of basal and apical amplitude using simulations of a model pyramidal layer 5b cell (Graham et al., 2025) and reframe the earlier work on contextual modulation in terms of integrated fields of basal and apical inputs, respectively. These conditions for contextual modulation are then checked for the regimes where basal input am-plitude is high while apical input amplitude is low, and vice versa, by using a numerical algorithm to obtain the Ibroja PID (James et al., 2018) for combinations of input basal and apical amplitudes. For the regime in which both basal and apical input amplitudes are high, we demonstrate the integration of the basal and apical inputs in producing a burst. 2Methods 

Notation 

We consider the trivariate probability distribution involving binary random variables, Y, X1, X2, with probability mass function (p.m.f.) p(y, x1, x2). The variable Y is an output variable with levels 0 and 1 , where level 1 denotes the occurrence of an event, e.g. a spike or a burst within a time interval of given length. The input, X1, has two levels: 0 and s1, where s1 is strictly positive, and it denotes the strength of signal X1 when present. The input, X2, has two levels: 0 and s2, where s2 is strictly positive, and it denotes the strength of signal X2 when present. We will make use of standard information measures, such as entropy, mutual information and conditional mutual information etc. (Cover and Thomas, 1991). See Wibral et al. (2017) for a description of these measures and also partial information decomposition. 

Partial information decomposition (PID) 

We can write the Shannon entropy H(Y) as 

H(Y) = I(Y; X1, X2) + H(Y)res ,

where H(Y)res is the conditional output entropy H(Y|X1, X2). Then the joint mutual information (JMI) I(Y; X1, X2) may be decomposed into four non-negative information components (Williams and Beer, 2010) as. 

I(Y; X1, X2) = Unq X1 + Unq X2 + Shd + Syn ,

where these terms describe parts of the information transmitted by the output Y. They are the unique information Unq X1 that input, X1, possesses regarding the output, that is not available from input 

X2; the unique information Unq X2 that input, X2, possesses regarding the output, that is not avail-able from input X1; the shared information Shd that both inputs possess regarding the output and the synergistic information Syn that the inputs in combination have about the output. In the following we will use the Ibroja PID (Gri ffi th and Koch, 2014; Bertschinger et al., 2014). Typically, applica-tion of this PID involves numerical optimisation, but for the class of transfer functions considered here we show that there are exact formulae for each of the PID components and that they can be written as functions of the two signal strengths ( s1, s2). Since we will be comparing many di ff erent 2 by 2 by 2 PIDs, defined by combinations of the signal strengths, we normalise the components in each PID by dividing by the output entropy, H(Y). The value of H(Y) gives the total bandwith – the total amount of information that can be transmitted – while the value of the JMI is the amount of information that is actually transmitted. In particular scenarios the transmitted information can use up all of the available bandwidth, while in others only a small proportion of the available bandwidth is utilised. The PID component, Shd, can be split into two non-negative parts, Shd = ShdS + ShdM, where ShdS and ShdM are called the source shared and mechanistic shared information, respec-tively (Harder et al., 2013; Pica et al., 2017). It is not possible to provide exact formulae for these components but Pica et al. (2017) provide expressions from which they can be determined numeri-cally. In any application involving independent inputs, with I(X1; X2) =0, we can deduce that ShdS 

=0 and so ShdM = Shd (Pica et al., 2017, Eq. (9)), so that the exact Shd component provides the exact mechanistic shared information transmitted by the system. 

Conditions for contextual modulation 

The conditions for contextual modulation are based on Kay and Phillips (2020), with modification. They are chosen to test for the key properties of contextual modulation. In applications, one of the 3two inputs will provide the drive and the other the context . The conditions are: CM1: The drive is su ffi cient for the transmission of the information that the inputs possess about the output, so the context is not necessary. CM2: The drive is necessary for the transmission of the information that the inputs possess about the output, so the context is not su ffi cient. CM3: The output can transmit unique information provided by the drive but little or no unique information provided by the context. CM4: When the drive is weak, the context can strengthen the transmission of the summed informa-tion components: unique information provided by the drive, shared information and synergis-tic information. Four combinations of signal strengths are used to check these conditions: S1 Scenario 1: Strong drive, with context near to 0, or absent S2 Scenario 2: Drive near to zero, or absent, with strong context but not strong enough to generate an event by itself S3 Scenario 3: Weak drive, with context near to 0, or absent S4 Scenario 4: Weak drive, with moderate context The conditions CM1 and CM2 assume implicitly that the possible values for the drive and the context can be varied independently. This is certainly the case when the set of ( s1, s2) values of interest is a rectangular region. When the set of ( s1, s2) values is triangular, e.g. s1 > s2, then these variables can’t be varied independently, and so conditions CM1 and CM2 cannot both be checked. If s1 > s2 only CM1 can be checked, whereas if s1 < s2 only CM2 can be checked – similarly for the corresponding scenarios S 1 and S 2.Overall, we state that contextual modulation occurs because the context can contribute to the transmission of information via the unique component provided by the drive along with the shared and synergistic components when the drive is weak, while little or no unique information provided by the context itself is transmitted. 

Condition for the integration of inputs 

We define a condition for integration of the inputs in the case where both inputs are high: when both inputs are su ffi ciently high then most information is transmitted via the shared and synergistic components, with little or no unique information provided by either input being transmitted.

# Conclusions 

The conditions for contextual modulation, together with four testing scenarios which make use of PID components, have been updated from their version in Kay and Phillips (2020). They have been applied with several di ff erent transfer functions. A simple transfer function has been defined; the 23 two inputs are fed into a general non-negative activation function, a threshold subtracted, and the result passed through a sigmoidal function to give the conditional probability of an event for given values of the two inputs. Exact results have been obtained for the information measures as well as the components of the Ibroja PID as continous functions of the signal strengths of the two inputs. Several general transfer functions have been defined based on additive, multiplicative, subtractive and divisive combinations of the two inputs, and deductions made regarding whether either or both of the unique informations are equal to zero. In the divisive case a particular form of a transfer function used for divisive normalisation (Carandini and Heeger, 2012) has been defined and exact results are available for it. Several specific examples are discussed. Activation functions based on two di ff erent multi-plicative forms are shown to provide purely modulatory transfer functions in which the driving input is modulated by the contextual input. In another illustration in which the activation function is a simple product of the two inputs, it is shown that the corresponding transfer function is purely integrative – and not modulatory, as also found in Kay and Phillips (2020). The transfer function that is based on a simple addition of the two inputs provides interesting results. The Ibroja results reveal that di ff erent findings are obtained for the unique informations provided by drive and by context, depending on whether or not the driving signal strength is greater than the contextual signal strength. Hence the restricted domains – s1 > s2 and s1 < s2 – are discussed separately. This approach was also motivated by the definition of di ff erent input regions in Graham et al. (2025). Evidence is provided that X1 is modulated by X2 when s1 > s2, whereas the reverse is true when s1 < s2. Also, when s1 and s2 are both high there is evidence of the integration of the two inputs. These conclusions are particularly rich especially when compared with the conclusions for this transfer function in the earlier paper (Kay and Phillips, 2020). In this earlier work, the transfer function was considered only for the whole rectangular region for ( s1, s2)and the richness of these conclusions was missed. The same restricted ( s1, s2) domains were also considered when the transfer function was based on a simple subtraction of the two input signals. When the strength of the driving signal s1 is greater than the strength of the inhibitory contextual signal s2 the transfer function produces modulatory be-haviour by depressing the probability of an event. When these relative signal strengths are reversed the conditions for contextual modulation are not all satisfied. As in the case of simple addition it is found that signal integration occurs when the signal strengths are both large. The transfer function based on a version of divisive modulation revealed purely modulatory behaviour in that the integrated input from neighbouring cells reduced the probability of an event. Finally, the information methodology was applied to the data from Graham et al. (2025). Ev-idence for apical amplification of the basal input in Regime HL, basal amplification of the apical input in regime LH, and basal-apical integration in regime HH was presented. These conclusions are qualitatively similar to those for the activation function based on simple addition; the rectan-gular regimes HL and LH correspond to the triangular restricted domains s1 > s2 and s1 < s2,respectively, and the rectangular regime HH corresponds to the unrestricted rectangular domain 

s1 > 0, s2 > 0.
