Abstract — Large-scale EEG foundation models have demonstrated promising transferability across neurolog-ical disorders, but often require millions of parameters and substantial computational resources. In this paper, we present the Universal Semantic EEG Foundation Model (USE-FM), a lightweight EEG foundation model that learns transferable neural representations through self-supervised signal reconstruction on the Temple University Hospital EEG Corpus (TUEG). After pretraining, the encoder is frozen and evaluated on two clinically dis-tinct downstream tasks, abnormal EEG detection (TUAB) and epileptic seizure recognition (TUEP), using a unified frozen-transfer protocol against recent EEG foundation models, including LUNA-Base and CBraMod. With only 1.46 million parameters, approximately one-fifth the size of existing models, USE-FM achieves competitive overall performance, including strong sensitivity and F1-score on TUEP (SEN 75 .00 ± 14 .14 , F1 70 .37 ± 4.01 ), while maintaining competitive performance on TUAB (AUC 

85 .24 ± 5.61 ). Beyond downstream classification, latent representation analysis using k-means clustering together with PCA and t-SNE demonstrates that USE-FM learns organized semantic EEG representations comparable to substantially larger foundation models. These results sug-gest that large-scale self-supervised pretraining enables lightweight architectures to learn transferable semantic EEG representations, providing a computationally efficient foundation for cross-disorder analysis and future clinical decision support in neurological disorders. 

Keywords — electroencephalogram, representation learning, foundation models, semantic feature extraction, transformer. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Electroencephalography (EEG) is a widely used non-invasive neurophysiological modality for investigating brain function and supporting the diagnosis of neurolog-ical disorders because of its high temporal resolution, portability, and relatively low acquisition cost [1, 2]. With the rapid development of self-supervised learning, EEG foundation models have emerged to learn transfer-able representations from large collections of unlabeled EEG recordings [3, 4]. Existing approaches commonly employ masked signal modeling [5, 6], contrastive learning [7, 8], or hybrid pretraining objectives [9, 10] to develop reusable EEG encoders for downstream clinical applications [11–13]. However, recent EEG foundation models have generally evolved toward increasingly large architectures to improve downstream transfer perfor-mance, such as Neuro-GPT (79.53M parameters) [14], Brant (500M parameters) [15], and LaBraM (5.8M, 46M, and 369M parameters) [16]. Although these large and high-capacity models achieve strong performance, their computational complexity and memory require-ments may limit practical deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments. Those requirements can limit computational efficiency, reproducibility, and practical deployment in clinical settings [17, 18]. More-over, clinical EEG presents additional challenges due to substantial inter-subject variability, low signal-to-noise ratio, heterogeneous acquisition protocols, and non-stationary temporal dynamics, making it difficult to learn representations that generalize across neurological disorders [19, 20]. These challenges raise an important question: Can a lightweight EEG foundation model learn universal representations that remain transferable across disorders without encoder fine-tuning? To address this question, we develop the Universal Semantic EEG Foundation Model (USE-FM) , alightweight, parameter-efficient CNN–Transformer au-toencoder for self-supervised EEG representation learn-ing, adapted from a prior semantic EEG representation framework [21]. Instead of scaling model size, USE-FM is designed to learn transferable semantic represen-tations through efficient latent representation learning under a frozen-encoder protocol. The pretrained encoder is directly transferred to downstream tasks without en-coder fine-tuning, enabling fair and computationally ef-ficient cross-disorder evaluation while reducing the need for task-specific retraining. Furthermore, the learned latent space provides a unified representation that can be quantitatively characterized through clustering and visualization analyses, offering insight into the semantic organization of EEG representations. We first pretrained the USE-FM on the large-scale Temple University Hospital EEG Corpus (TUEG) [22] and evaluated without encoder fine-tuning on the Tem-ple University Abnormal EEG Corpus (TUAB) [23] and the Temple University Epilepsy Corpus (TUEP) [24]. Beyond downstream classification performance, we further investigate the learned latent representations using clustering metrics and low-dimensional visualiza-tion to assess representation compactness, separability, and cross-disorder transferability. Experimental results demonstrate that USE-FM learns robust universal EEG representations while maintaining competitive transfer performance with substantially fewer parameters and a

II. M ETHODS 

II-A. Model Architecture 

The proposed Model, USE-FM, adopts the lightweight CNN–Transformer autoencoder architecture introduced in [21]. Rather than developing a more complex architecture with substantially more parameters (Ta-ble 1), this work investigates whether large-scale self-supervised pretraining on TUEG enables a compact encoder to learn transferable semantic EEG represen-tations under a frozen-encoder protocol. USE-FM con-tains only 1.46 million trainable parameters, approxi-mately one-quarter that of CBraMod and one-fifth that of LUNA-Base (Table 1), while maintaining competitive cross-disorder transfer performance.

II-B. Experimental Setup 

Following previous EEG foundation model studies [16, 26, 27], all EEG recordings were band-pass filtered between 0.3 and 75 Hz, notch filtered at 60 Hz, mapped to the standard 19-channel international 10– 20 montage, resampled to 200 Hz, and segmented into non-overlapping 30-second windows. Signal amplitudes were normalized to approximately ±100 μV to reduce inter-recording variability. The encoder was pretrained on the TUEG, comprising 2,259,998 EEG segments (approximately 18,800 hours of recordings). Transfer performance was subsequently evaluated on two downstream datasets: the TUAB, for binary abnormal-versus-normal classification, and the TUEP, for epilepsy detection. These datasets evaluate whether a single pretrained representation can general-ize across clinically distinct neurological disorders. For a fair comparison with LUNA-Base and CBraMod, all downstream experiments followed an identical frozen-encoder protocol. The pretrained encoder was kept fixed, and only a MLP classifier was optimized for each downstream task. 

II-C. Pretraining Configuration 

The model was pretrained using Python 3.12.12 and PyTorch 2.5.1 with CUDA 12.4 on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs using Distributed Data Parallel (DDP). We used the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1 ×

10 −4, a batch size of 64, and MSE reconstruction loss for self-supervised pretraining. Training was performed for up to 100 epochs with early stopping (patience = 5). The total pretraining time was approximately 100 hours.

Results are reported as the mean ± standard deviation over the independent runs. IV. S UMMARY 

This paper presented USE-FM, a lightweight universal semantic EEG foundation model pretrained on over 2.2 million unlabeled EEG segments from the TUEG. Without task-specific fine-tuning, the pretrained encoder demonstrated competitive frozen-transfer performance on both abnormal EEG detection (TUAB) and epilep-tic seizure recognition (TUEP), achieving performance comparable to substantially larger pretrained EEG foun-dation models while requiring only 1.46 million param-eters. As for latent representation analysis, USE-FM revealed a consistent four-state organization of the learned EEG embeddings. Clustering analysis together with PCA and t-SNE visualizations demonstrated that USE-FM learns well-structured latent representations comparable to those of substantially larger foundation models despite its lightweight architecture. These analyses complement the downstream classification results by showing that the learned embeddings exhibit consistent semantic or-ganization beyond conventional performance metrics. Furthermore, the latent space provides a compact rep-resentation for exploring similarities and differences among EEG recordings, offering a useful framework for investigating neurological disorders and improving the understanding of learned EEG representations in future clinical research.
