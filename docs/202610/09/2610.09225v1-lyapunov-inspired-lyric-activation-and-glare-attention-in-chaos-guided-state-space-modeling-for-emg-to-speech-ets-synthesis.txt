# Lyapunov-Inspired LyRIC Activation and GLARE Attention in Chaos-Guided State Space Modeling for EMG-To-Speech (ETS) Synthesis 

Sajid Fardin Dipto Tarikul Islam Tamiti Luke Baja-Ricketts David Vergano Anomadarshi Barua 

Department of Cyber Security Engineering George Mason University Fairfax, VA, USA 

Abstract 

Electromyography-to-Speech (ETS) synthesis is typically a non-linear, chaotic dynamical system. However, no prior work has stud-ied the chaotic behavior of ETS synthesis to date. Yet, prior works strictly rely on stan-dard reconstruction metrics with parameter-heavy transformers that systematically over-smooth natural acoustic dynamics. To close this gap, for the first time, we propose achaos-inspired Lyapunov-derived new activa-tion function (LyRIC) with two novel chaotic loss functions – Lyapunov Exponent Regu-larization and Multi-Scale Detrended Fluctu-ation Analysis – to explicitly capture the de-terministic chaos of human phonation. In addi-tion, we introduce a compressed novel encoder – GLAME, which synergizes global Mamba state-space modeling with localized GLARE attention. We comprehensively do frame-level acoustic evaluation in a multi-language and multi-speaker setup on two datasets - English and Mandarin. It outperforms the established baseline with a 4.69 × increase in objective intelligibility (STOI: 0.61 vs. 0.13) and a 2.08 × improvement in spectral reconstruction (LSD: 1.08 vs. 2.25). Importantly, this improve-ment is achieved with 73.49% fewer parame-ters (14.34M vs. 54.10M), establishing a new baseline in the ETS synthesis task. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work proving that integrating non-linear chaotic physics into neural networks yields superior but compact in-ductive biases for real-time ETS synthesis task. 

1 Introduction and Related Work 

Silent speech interfaces (SSIs) synthesize unvocal-ized speech by decoding articulatory biosignals, as-sisting speech-impaired users. Electromyography (EMG) captures the muscle activity needed for this synthesis (Dong et al., 2023; Satterlee et al., 2025). 

What current ETS systems miss: Despite steady improvements in Word Error Rate (WER), recent ETS models published in ACL and EMNLP (Gaddy and Klein, 2020, 2021) and (Scheck et al., 2023; Ren et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2025) suffer from the following three fundamental limitations. 

First , approaching speech synthesis as asmoothed regression task optimizes for frame-level losses while neglecting the non-linear chaotic dynamics of human phonation. Penalizing natural chaotic variations inevitably forces models to produce muffled, robotic prosody. Second ,recent models (Gaddy and Klein, 2020, 2021) rely on parameter-heavy Transformers (exceeding 44M parameters). This architectural bloat fails to exploit the localized, temporal shift-invariance of EMG signals, violating the low-latency constraints required for SSIs. Third , prior evaluations rely overwhelmingly on WER or CER, leaving the true perceptual fidelity, spectral distortion, and objective intelligibility of previous systems uncharacterized. 

To address these gaps, we propose CGS-ETS (Chaos-Guided State Space Modeling for EMG-To-Speech) , which is the first to unify non-linear chaos theory with efficient State Space Model (SSM), achieving better acoustic fidelity with a drastically reduced parameter footprint. Our core contributions are manifold: (1) We invent and inte-grate Grouped Local Attention with Relative Po-sitional Embeddings (GLARE) to the ETS do-main. GLARE captures temporal shift-invariance while accelerating inference for long-context EMG sequences, providing an efficient alternative to standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA) for ETS synthesis. (2) We propose the GLARE-fused Mamba Encoder (GLAME) , a parameter-efficient 1arXiv:2610.09225v1 [cs.SD] 6 Oct 2026 Figure 1: Detailed architecture of CGS-ETS with chaos-driven LyRIC activation and GLARE attention. 

SSM architecture, where standard SiLU activations are replaced with our newly designed Lyapunov Regularized Implicit Curve (LyRIC) activation function to inject chaotic awareness into the state-space trajectory, achieving a 77.63% reduced com-plexity. (3) We introduce two novel optimization objectives to constrain the network to the deter-ministic chaotic invariants of speech: LER (Lya-punov Exponent Regularization) loss penalizes mismatches in local divergence rates by aligning Lyapunov Exponents (LE) across varying temporal resolutions; and MSDFA (Multi-Scale Detrended Fluctuation Analysis) quantifies the fractal-like, long-range temporal chaotic correlations strictly overlooked by standard spectrogram losses. 2 Overall Architecture 

We show CGS-ETS in Fig. 1. It takes 8-channel EMG data as input, preprocesses it, and gener-ates melspectrogram (mel) bins, which are con-verted to audio at the last stage by a HiFi-GAN vocoder (Kong et al., 2020). Our previously men-tioned novel four innovations in CGS-ETS – an efficient alternative GLARE to standard MHA, a first chaos-derived activation function LyRIC , two chaos-guided loss components LER and MSDFA ,and a very lightweight encoder GLAME are ex-plained below sequentially in the rest of the paper. 

2.1 Design of the GLARE 

Please note that the Grouped Query Attention (GQA) (Ainslie et al., 2023) is a generalization of Multi-Query Attention (MQA) (Shazeer, 2019), and Sliding Window Attention (SWA) captures fine-grained local context by sliding over the in-put sequence (Beltagy et al., 2020) through a slid-ing window mask (SWM). We adopt GQA within the SWA module rather than employing standard MHA to reduce memory bandwidth overhead dur-ing inference (see Table 8 for ablation on differ-ent attention mechanisms) . Furthermore, we delib-erately integrate Relative Positional Embeddings (RPE) from (Gaddy and Klein, 2021) to ensure the attention remains highly robust to natural tempo-ral variations and the precise local alignments re-quired for high-fidelity phonetic decoding. Thus, we propose GLARE, a unified attention mecha-nism that integrates SWM and RPE with GQA, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Like optical glare, which achieves maximum perceptual signal through a di-rected, concentrated beam rather than diffuse ambi-ent light, our GLARE concentrates attention within a sliding local window, groups query computations for efficiency, and encodes positional relationships through relative embeddings rather than attending globally across all token positions, yielding com-petitive performance at substantially reduced com-putational cost compared to full MHA.

2.3 Convolutional Feature Extraction (CFE) 

The CFE block (Fig. 1) comprises 3 residual blocks. Each contains two 1D convolutional lay-ers with ReLU and a parameterized skip connec-tion ensuring dimensional congruency (Appendix A.1). A uniform channel capacity of CD = 512 is maintained across all layers. This design achieves superior EMG synthesis with a reduced parameter footprint (see Table 15 for channel ablation).

3 Comprehensive Analysis 

3.1 Evaluation Metrics 

After applying post-vocoder alignment (see Ap-pendix A.15 and Fig. 1 for details) , we use ten evaluation metrics – Log Spectral Distance (LSD), Short-Time Objective Intelligibility (STOI), Per-ceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ), Scale-Invariant Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (SI-SDR), Non-Intrusive Speech Quality Assessment Mean Opinion Score (NISQA-MOS), Floating-Point Operations (FLOPs), Inference Time (IT) per batch, Word Error Rate (WER), Character Error Rate (CER) and Real-Time Factor (RTF). No prior work in the literature has employed such an exten-sive set of evaluation metrics for ETS systems. 

3.2 Dataset and Hyperparameter 

We evaluate on the state-of-the-art (SOTA) public English dataset (Gaddy and Klein, 2020), compris-ing 19 hours of facial EMG data collected under silent and vocalized speech conditions. We do min-imal preprocessing to facilitate stable model op-timization. We also evaluate on a 6-channel multi-speaker Mandarin dataset AVE Speech (Zhou et al., 2025), consisting of ∼55 hours of synchronized audio and EMG data from 100 speakers (see Ap-pendix A.11 for detailed data preparation and pre-processing on both datasets) . We ablate on unmod-ified EMG data, which already contains inherent real-world noise (cross-talk, shifts, interference) to validate robustness under noisy conditions. 3.3 Comparative Analysis with Baselines 

Table 2 comprehensively evaluates our CGS-ETS against four recent SOTA baselines: (Gaddy and Klein, 2020, 2021; Scheck et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2025) on English and Mandarin datasets. Histori-cally, most prior works report only text-based er-ror rates like WER or CER, and none of them do point-to-point quality assessment due to tem-poral misalignment issues in the post-vocoder stage. A full suite of perceptual and structural met-rics (LSD, STOI, PESQ, SI-SDR) requires point-to-point (frame-by-frame) speech calculation. To solve this issue, we use DTW at the post-vocoder stage to ensure sample-by-sample comparison. Comparing against the strongest fully evaluated baseline 2 (P2) on the English corpus reveals that CGS-ETS establishes a definitive new standard for acoustic reconstruction and fidelity. Our chaos-inspired framework with the GLARE attention de-livers a 2.08 × improvement in spectral reconstruc-tion accuracy (LSD = 1.08 vs 2.25), a 4.69 × in-crease in objective speech intelligibility (STOI = 0.61 vs 0.13), and a 1.28 × enhancement in noise suppression (SI-SDR = -32.6 vs -42.0). CGS-ETS yields an improved WER of 41.36% and a CER of 23.2%. Please note that CGS-ETS achieves recon-struction improvement with a significant degree of architectural compression. 6 Limitations 

Our current work establishes a new baseline us-ing the single-speaker English dataset from (Gaddy and Klein, 2020), which serves as the gold stan-dard for EMG-to-speech synthesis, as well as a multi-speaker Mandarin dataset from (Zhou et al., 2025). However, these datasets do not consider multi-modal environments, such as visual images or video. Therefore, we focus our design to ro-bustly handle intrinsic physiological artifacts for speech synthesis. CGS-ETS introduces specific computational constraints during the training phase while it achieves state-of-the-art performance in multi-speaker EMG-to-Speech synthesis. The integration of the LER and MSDFA requires the reconstruc-tion of high-dimensional phase-space manifolds. For an utterance of T = 200 frames with C = 80 

features, the LER estimation involves computing a distance matrix across a concatenated feature vec-tor of m × C dimensions. In addition, while our localized GLARE atten-tion improves parameter efficiency by 73 .49% , the architecture employs a fixed sliding window ( k =32 ) and relative positional biases. A.1 Convolutional Feature Extraction (CFE) 

CFE block comprises three residual blocks follow-ing (Gaddy and Klein, 2021). Each block com-prises two 1D convolutional (Conv-1D) layers (ker-nel size=3) interspersed with ReLU activations, augmented by a parameterized skip connection. The first Conv-1D downsamples inputs by 2×

(stride=2) while the second one maintains the same resolution (stride=1). The skip connection utilizes a matching projection as the first Conv-1D (stride=2) to guarantee dimensional congruency for the residual connection. A uniform channel capacity of CD = 512 is rigorously maintained across all layers. This streamlined configuration is designed to capture the rapid, dynamic EMG pat-terns of facial articulation, which achieves supe-rior phonetic decoding performance with a consid-erably reduced parameter footprint.

A.7 Disentangling the Contributions of CGS-ETS Components 

To address potential concerns regarding the ar-chitectural complexity of the proposed CGS-ETS model, this section rigorously isolates and quan-tifies the independent contributions of our novel mechanisms: the GLAME encoder, the chaotic loss functions (LER and MSDFA), the LyRIC activa-tion function, and the GLARE attention module. Our comprehensive ablation studies demonstrate that no single component is redundantly layered; rather, each explicitly targets a distinct representa-tional or computational bottleneck in ETS synthe-sis. Table 17 provides a consolidated overview of these marginal utilities, demonstrating the specific performance and efficiency gains relative to stan-dard baselines. Notably, the table captures both the macro-level efficiency of the proposed GLAME en-coder and the micro-level contributions of its inter-nal components. The detailed logic and analysis for each component are discussed below. 

A.7.1 The Macro-Architectural Role of the GLAME Encoder 

While the subsequent subsections detail the in-ternal mechanisms of our model, Table 3 high-lights the overarching structural contribution of the GLAME encoder itself. Standard baseline en-coders (Gaddy and Klein, 2021) suffer from se-vere architectural bloat, requiring 44.03M param-eters and 2400M FLOPs to process EMG fea-tures. A.8 EMG as A Chaotic Dynamical System 

Articulatory EMG captures the coordinated, spatio-temporal activation of facial and laryngeal muscles during speech production (Gaddy and Klein, 2020, 2021). This myoelectric generation is a non-linear dynamical process characterized by deterministic chaos (Padmanabhan and Puthusserypady, 2004; Ravier et al., 2005). The physiological “source” of the signal is the central neural drive, which recruits populations of motor neurons. These neurons sub-sequently generate asynchronous Motor Unit Ac-tion Potentials (MUAPs) across varying muscle fibers. However, before reaching the surface elec-trodes, these localized action potentials undergo severe non-linear spatial filtering through the visco-elastic subcutaneous tissue, skin layers, and shift-ing muscle geometries (Merletti and Farina, 2016). The resulting surface EMG is not a simple linear summation, but rather a highly complex interfer-ence pattern characterized by amplitude modula-tion, quasi-periodic bursting, and phase cancella-tions (Farina et al., 2014). Essentially, the articulatory neuromuscular sys-tem operates through a continuous, closed-loop sensorimotor framework. Motor unit recruitment is dynamically modulated in real-time by proprio-ceptive feedback from muscle spindles and Golgi tendon organs (Todorov, 2004). Because the cen-tral nervous system rapidly adjusts firing rates based on this non-linear feedback, the resulting muscle contractions exhibit extreme sensitivity to initial conditions. Consequently, the EMG signal frequently undergoes bifurcations– such as sudden motor unit synchronization during rapid coarticu-lation or fatigue-induced tremors—intermittently driving the signal into chaotic regimes rigorously indicated by positive Lyapunov exponents (Pad-manabhan and Puthusserypady, 2004). The bio-signal naturally alternates between quasi-stationary periods (e.g., sustained vowel postures) and highly stochastic, irregular micro-13 fluctuations (e.g., rapid consonant transitions) dur-ing continuous phonetic articulation. Moreover, the transient biochemical kinetics of ion channels (e.g., 

N a +/K+ gradients) and varying action potential conduction velocities introduce continuous micro-temporal jitter into the firing patterns (Merletti and Farina, 2016). This creates a non-stationary, fractal-like time-series where minor physiological pertur-bations map to massive variations in the recorded interference pattern. A purely linear regression model or standard sequence-to-sequence transformer basically over-looks these critical bio-physical realities (Gaddy and Klein, 2021). Conventional models mathemat-ically average out the sub-harmonic firing frequen-cies, bifurcations, and aperiodic MUAP bursts by optimizing strictly for mean-squared reconstruc-tion. In the context of ETS synthesis, this di-rectly causes the network to output unnaturally over-smoothed, muffled acoustic representations devoid of natural prosody and crisp phonetic transi-tions. Therefore, to faithfully invert this bio-signal into highly intelligible speech, the predictive ar-chitecture must explicitly incorporate non-linear, chaos-inspired topological constraints (e.g., LER, MSDFA) capable of mapping both the structured macroscopic muscle gestures and the deterministic chaotic micro-structures of the electrophysiologi-cal source.

A.10 Theoretical Analysis of the LER Loss 

Deterministic Chaos theory (Strogatz, 1994; Lorenz, 1963) of Lyapunov in nonlinear dynami-cal systems motivates the formulation of the LER loss . It describes complex, non-periodic behavior in nonlinear dynamical systems that are highly sensitive to initial conditions. While such systems are governed by deterministic laws, their long-term evolution is inherently unpredictable due to the exponential amplification of infinitesimal perturbations. 

Deterministic Chaos: In Deterministic Chaos theory, the fundamental metric for quantifying the chaotic behavior is the Lyapunov Exponent (λ), which measures the mean exponential rate |δx (t)|

of trajectory divergence in state-space. The spec-trum of λ classifies the system dynamics: λ < 0 de-notes a stable, over-damped system converging to a fixed point; λ = 0 corresponds to marginally sta-ble limit cycles or periodic oscillators; and λ > 0

serves as the definitive indicator of deterministic chaos. In such chaotic regimes, microscopic varia-tions in the initial state map to macroscopic, diver-gent variations in the system’s output– a hallmark of human phonation and neuromuscular motor con-trol .

Deterministic Chaos for Designing LER Loss: 

Conventional ETS systems optimize for frame-level reconstruction losses (e.g., L1 or MSE loss), which mathematically enforces a linear, stable con-vergence ( λ ≤ 0). This objective forces the net-work to eliminate natural jitter, aperiodic bursts, and rapid state transitions, resulting in unnaturally smoothed acoustic prosody. To counter this, we introduce the Lyapunov Exponent Regularization (LER) loss , designed to preserve the chaotic man-ifold of the source signals. As formalized in Al-gorithm 1, LER operates by reconstructing the la-tent phase-space trajectories of both the predicted EMG synthesized features and the ground-truth tar-gets. We utilize the Rosenstein-based divergence estimation (Rosenstein et al., 1993), which com-putes the average logarithmic rate of separation between neighboring trajectories. By enforcing a penalty on the distance between the predicted ( λ ˆY )and ground-truth ( λY ) Lyapunov exponents, we constrain the encoder to replicate the divergence characteristics of the source neuromuscular sig-nals. In the context of our EMG systems, this physi-cally grounded supervision ensures the model does not collapse into a stable equilibrium. Instead, it 17 compels the network to represent the deterministic chaos inherent in the electrophysiological source– particularly the sub-harmonic firing patterns, bifur-cations, and timing irregularities– which are essen-tial for synthesizing human-like, temporally coher-ent speech. Therefore, the LER objective serves as a topological constraint, ensuring that the synthe-sized acoustic features faithfully reflect the com-plex, non-linear micro-structure of the underlying EMG articulatory gestures. 

A.11 Dataset Preprocessing Pipeline A.11.1 English Corpus 

We utilize the established SOTA 2020 ETS En-glish dataset (Gaddy and Klein, 2020) to bench-mark our proposed CGS-ETS architecture. The dataset comprises nearly 20 hours of facial EMG and parallel audio recordings from a single English speaker, bifurcated into closed-vocabulary and open-vocabulary conditions. To facilitate acous-tic mapping, the corpus utilizes parallel tuples of silently mouthed EMG, vocalized EMG, and simul-taneous vocalized audio. During the training phase of our model, DTW on the vocalized pairs gener-ates alignments used to transfer audio targets to the silent EMG signals. The closed-vocabulary sub-set contains ≈56 minutes of data restricted to 67 unique words and was recorded in a single, contin-uous session to eliminate spatial variance in elec-trode placement. Conversely, the open-vocabulary condition encompasses the vast majority of the cor-pus, featuring open-domain sentences from public domain books recorded across multiple sessions, which deliberately introduces a realistic variance in electrode positioning. For standard evaluation, this open-vocabulary data is partitioned into an 8:1:1 split for training, validation, and testing, with strict precautions taken to ensure that the specific texts utilized in the test set are excluded from any ex-ternal language model training corpora to prevent label leakage. Prior to experimentation, we rigor-ously verified that the corpus is devoid of Person-ally Identifiable Information (PII) and any abusive or harmful content, adhering to ethical standards for bio-signal research. The corpus is completely partitioned into training, validation, and test splits following the standardized JSON indices provided in the benchmark. 

Preprocessing: Each utterance is contextually loaded by concatenating it with its temporally adja-cent neighbors ( i − 1, i, i + 1 ) to precisely mit-igate transient boundary artifacts during digital filtering which isolates the target sequence only after continuous filtration. We suppress ambient electromagnetic interference by applying a rigor-ous comb notch filter at 60 Hz and its subsequent seven harmonics (up to 420 Hz). A 3rd-order But-terworth high-pass filter (2 Hz cutoff) is subse-quently applied to eliminate bio-electrical baseline drift. The continuous signals are then subsampled to synchronize with the acoustic frame rate ( ≈689 Hz). Following filtration, we extract a dense, multi-domain feature representation utilizing a sliding window (16-sample length, 6-sample hop). This results in localized time-domain statistics (mean amplitudes, RMS envelopes, zero-crossing rates of the high-frequency residual) alongside frequency-domain STFT magnitudes. The concatenated EMG features are standardized through global mean-variance normalization and tightly bounded using a scaled hyperbolic tangent projection ( 8 tanh( x/ 8) )to guarantee strict topological stability during gra-dient propagation. 

Acoustic and Linguistic Processing: Ground-truth acoustic waveforms are dynamically loaded, collapsed from stereo to mono configurations to isolate the primary channel, and rigorously resam-pled to 22.05 kHz. We extract 80-dimensional mel-spectrograms to formulate the primary regres-sion targets utilizing a 1024-point STFT, a 1024-sample Hanning window, and a 256-sample hop size, bounding the spectral representation between 0 and 8,000 Hz. Concurrently, to facilitate our auxiliary linguistic optimization, we parse forced-alignment TextGrid annotations to extract frame-level phonetic labels across a 48-token inventory (including silence). Finally, raw text transcripts are stripped of punctuation, lowercased, and normal-ized through the JiWER pipeline to ensure pristine linguistic mapping during multi-task supervision.

In pure mathematical formulation, an expression such as x|x| requires two distinct operations per element: an absolute value calculation and a multiplication. However, in deep learning software implementations, func-tions like torch.abs() are typically executed at the hardware level as highly optimized bitwise operations (e.g., clearing the sign bit) and are frequently omitted from standard FLOP profilers. Consequently, the practical implementation cost of x|x| registers as 1 FLOP/element for the mul-tiplication, rather than 2 (see Table 1 for details on FLOPs/element of LyRIC activation function). This software-hardware optimization paradigm ap-plies broadly across our element-wise tensor op-erations, resulting in implementation-based FLOP counts that are slightly lower than their theoretical mathematical counterparts. More importantly, this distinction in FLOP counting methodology does not meaningfully impact the overall complexity analysis of the model. Activation functions con-sist entirely of element-wise operations that scale linearly. Their computational overhead is micro-scopically small when compared to the dominant operations in the overall architecture. Because the 20 network’s computational bottleneck is dictated by these high-density matrix operations, any theoreti-cal variance between the PyTorch implementation-level FLOPs and pure mathematical FLOPs for element-wise activations alters the total network complexity by a negligible fraction of a percent. This 61.39% reduc-tion in absolute computational operations mitigates thermal throttling and power consumption con-straints on portable hardware. Feature-Level Synchronization: Computes a pairwise cosine distance matrix between the predicted spectrograms and the ground-truth targets. To explicitly resolve the inherent, non-linear timing mismatches between anticipa-tory muscle actuation and the actual vocaliza-tion, it utilizes DTW to align the temporal res-olution of the latent features, ensuring stable and reliable loss optimization. 4. Waveform Inversion: Inverts the strictly aligned mel-spectrogram predictions into high-fidelity, time-domain acoustic wave-forms. By utilizing the pre-trained HiFi-GAN vocoder, this final phase robustly reconstructs the complex phase information and heav-ily suppresses synthesis artifacts, ultimately yielding highly natural and perceptually intel-ligible continuous speech. 5. Waveforms are downsampled to 16 kHz prior to computing PESQ and NISQA-MOS, en-suring strict compliance with their rigid fre-quency constraints and guaranteeing standard-ized, mathematically valid perceptual scores.
