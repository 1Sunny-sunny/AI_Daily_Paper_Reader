ABSTRACT
EEG foundation models (EFMs) capture reusable knowledge from large-scale
EEG data, while many EEG recordings also include companion physiological
signals that provide complementary information beyond the EEG-only interface.
The challenge is to preserve this pretrained knowledge while extending the EFM
to heterogeneous multimodal recordings through an adaptation inferred from un-
labeled target data.
We introduce ZeroMAG, a zero-shot multimodal adapter
generation framework that extends a frozen EEG encoder and prediction head
using unlabeled target recordings, without target labels or target-side optimiza-
tion.
The target datasets are held out from all model training and selection
in the ZeroMAG pipeline. ZeroMAG organizes companion modalities around
a configuration-invariant adapter, constructs a modality–subject–task condition
from unlabeled recordings and task context, and generates adapter weights in a
function-constrained latent space learned from source adapters. Across six held-
out target datasets and three EFM backbones, ZeroMAG improves balanced ac-
curacy by 7.22 percentage points over EEG-only inference and 4.89 points over
direct weight regression, while coming within 0.50 points of supervised multi-
modal adaptation on average. Ablations further show that removing functional
supervision from either representation learning or conditional generation degrades
generated-adapter performance, confirming the contribution of both components.

INTRODUCTION
Electroencephalography (EEG) is a non-invasive and widely used modality across clinical, cogni-
tive, and brain–computer interface applications (Craik et al., 2019; Wolpaw et al., 2002; Lee et al.,
2025). Its large and growing body of mostly unlabeled recordings has enabled EEG foundation
models (EFMs) that learn reusable representations across downstream tasks (Kuruppu et al., 2025;
Jiang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025; 2024a; Ma et al., 2026; Zhou et al., 2025).
EEG is increasingly recorded alongside companion signals that capture complementary physiolog-
ical information, such as eye movements, muscle activity, and cardiac dynamics (Fig. 1(a)). Be-
tween 2016 and 2024, at least 124 deep learning studies combined EEG with EOG, EMG, ECG, or
GSR (Lee et al., 2025), and concurrent EEG–fNIRS studies increased markedly after 2016 (Yeung
& Chu, 2022); yet the number and type of available signals vary widely across datasets (Fig. 1(b)).
A common approach to exploiting these signals is multimodal physiological or general time-series
foundation models (Thapa et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2025). However, this often
means training a new multimodal backbone instead of reusing the strong EEG representations al-
ready learned by existing EFMs, while also fixing the supported modality set in advance. Adapting
an existing EFM is cheaper, but new recordings often arrive unlabeled because annotation requires
expert review, making supervised adaptation for each target dataset impractical. Label-free adapta-

Can multimodal capabilities be added to an existing EFM without target-side training?
Answering this question requires addressing three challenges.
C1: Heterogeneous modality configurations. EEG datasets differ in their companion signals:
sleep recordings often include EOG and EMG, whereas emotion-recognition datasets may include
eye movement. An adapter must accommodate these differences while allowing the available signals
to contribute complementary information. The challenge is to accommodate arbitrary configurations
across datasets within a single unified framework, without per-dataset redesign.
C2: Adaptation needs are target-dependent but labels are often unavailable. Even with the
same companion signals, the appropriate adaptation differs across datasets and prediction tasks,
so it must be determined for each target. However, target labels are usually unavailable because
annotation is costly and requires expert review. The challenge is therefore to infer the target-specific
adaptation from unlabeled recordings together with the task context.
C3: Parameter reconstruction does not ensure functional consistency. The generator is trained
to reproduce adapters fitted on the source datasets. Two adapters with similar weights can still make
different predictions, because the output is much more sensitive to some parameters than to others.
A generator trained only to minimize parameter error may therefore produce adapters that are close
to the targets in weight space but behave differently. The challenge is to make the generated adapters
agree with the source adapters in their predictions.
To address these challenges, we propose ZeroMAG, which treats multimodal extension as personal-
ized parameter generation: given unlabeled target recordings and task context, it directly generates a
target-specific adapter while keeping the EFM fixed. A configuration-invariant adapter organizes
reusable modality-specific blocks around a fixed EEG anchor (C1); a modality–subject–task condi-
tion captures target-specific information without dataset identifiers (C2); and function-constrained
generation maps this condition to adapter parameters through a block-aligned latent space learned
from fitted source adapters (C3).

3.2
MULTIMODAL ADAPTER ARCHITECTURE
Intuition. To generate adapters for heterogeneous modality configurations with a single generator,
the adapters must share a common parameter structure. Without it, each configuration would require
its own output space. This suggests a 1+N organization, where the frozen EEG representation serves
as the invariant anchor and modality-specific companion branches provide configuration-dependent
extensions.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Datasets and Tasks.
We evaluate ZeroMAG on a multi-dataset benchmark spanning sleep staging,
emotion recognition, cognitive load, motor imagery, and freezing-of-gait detection. The recordings
cover EOG, EMG, ECG, eye movement, acceleration, and skin conductance, including settings
in which modality combinations or modality types are excluded from generator training. Table 1
reports results on six target datasets that are disjoint from the source training datasets; FoG is also
a task-holdout target with no source adapter for its task. Task coverage, the remaining targets, and
further analyses are in Appendix C.
Backbones and Baselines.
We use three frozen EEG foundation models: CBraMod (Wang et al.,
2025), CSBrain (Zhou et al., 2025), and CodeBrain (Ma et al., 2026). Each backbone has a sep-
arately trained generator. ZeroMAG and the target-label-free baselines use the same frozen EEG
encoder and prediction head. These baselines include EEG-only inference, mean and nearest source
weights, and Direct MLP. A separate supervised multimodal fine-tuning experiment uses target train-
ing labels to measure the gap to supervised adaptation on the same query split; it is not part of the Ze-
roMAG pipeline. PhysioOmni (Fu et al., 2025), SleepFM (Thapa et al., 2024), and Brant-X (Zhang
et al., 2024) serve as cross-paradigm references.
Protocol.
Evaluation uses a dataset-disjoint source–target split: all target datasets are excluded
from EEG encoder/head training, source adapter fitting, both generator-training stages, and model
selection. For each target subject, calibration and query subsets are disjoint. ZeroMAG uses only
unlabeled calibration signals and the supported task to generate an adapter, then predicts on query
inputs with all model parameters fixed. Query labels are used only to compute evaluation metrics.
Dataset lists and split details are given in Appendix H.3.
Metrics and Implementation.
We report balanced accuracy (B-ACC) averaged over five matched
seeds. Both stages use AdamW (learning rate 10−4, batch size 64); the CBT and DiT have 2 and 4
layers of width 256, the decoder uses 4 experts with top-2 routing, and latent tokens have width 128.
Loss weights are λcos=λlat=λw=0.1, λfun=λgf=1, β=10−4, and λbal=0.01, with the low-noise
gate ¯αt>0.5. The source bank holds 311 adapters per backbone, and each target subject provides 64
unlabeled calibration windows. Full settings are in Appendix H (Table H.1).
4.2
PERFORMANCE COMPARISON WITH BASELINES
Comparison with label-free and supervised adaptation.
ZeroMAG ranks first among target-
label-free methods in all 18 backbone–dataset evaluations, improving B-ACC on average by 7.22
points over EEG-only inference, 6.78 over mean source weights, 6.00 over nearest source weights,
and 4.89 over Direct MLP, the strongest competing generator. Despite using neither target labels nor
target-side optimization, it remains only 0.38–0.61 points below full-modality supervised tuning in
every setting (0.50 on average), recovering 93.50% of the supervised gain over EEG-only inference.
Backbone and cross-paradigm generalization.
The gain over EEG-only inference is stable
across CBraMod, CSBrain, and CodeBrain at 7.12, 7.30, and 7.24 points; the range is approxi-
mately 0.19 points. With CodeBrain, ZeroMAG exceeds SleepFM on all six displayed targets by
1.85 points on average, trails PhysioOmni by only 0.31 points, and trails Brant-X by 0.80 points on
average.

4.4
GENERALIZATION AND EFFICIENCY
Unseen modalities.
On unseen combinations of known modalities and modality types in M ex-
cluded from generator training, ZeroMAG recovers 89.7% and 82.6% of the gain achieved by su-
pervised multimodal adaptation, respectively. It outperforms mean source transfer, nearest-source
retrieval, and direct weight regression in both settings.
Data and target-side efficiency.
Performance saturates at approximately 64 unlabeled calibration
windows. Moreover, using only 50% of the source adapter bank retains 97.2% of the full multimodal
gain. ZeroMAG constructs the condition and generates an adapter in 2 minutes, compared with 15
minutes for supervised target adaptation under the same hardware and protocol (7.5× faster); offline
training cost is amortized across targets (Appendix F).

RELATED WORK
EEG foundation and multimodal physiological models.
EEG foundation models provide trans-
ferable backbones across downstream tasks (Kuruppu et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2024; Wang et al.,
2025; 2024a; Yang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023), but largely retain EEG-centered interfaces.
Prior multimodal physiological work integrates signals through multimodal foundation-model pre-
training or EEG-to-physiology alignment (Fu et al., 2025; Thapa et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024).
ZeroMAG generates a plug-in adapter for an existing EFM while keeping its encoder and prediction
head frozen.
Adaptation and weight generation.
Parameter-efficient adaptation methods (Houlsby et al.,
2019; Hu et al., 2022; Lester et al., 2021; Han et al., 2024; Suzumura et al., 2024) still require
target-side optimization, whereas hypernetworks generate parameters from task descriptors or unla-
beled prompts (Ha et al., 2017; Karimi Mahabadi et al., 2021; Charakorn et al., 2025; Liang et al.,
2025). ZeroMAG instead generates a modular physiological adapter from unlabeled target infor-
mation in a structured latent space. Unlike test-time adaptation (Liang et al., 2023; Wang et al.,
2021; Liang et al., 2020), deployment requires no pseudo-labels, gradients, or iterative optimization
(Appendix J.1).

CONCLUSION
We introduced ZeroMAG, a zero-shot framework that extends frozen EFMs to heterogeneous mul-
timodal recordings through generated plug-in adapters. Combining configuration-invariant adap-
tation, target-derived conditioning, and function-constrained latent generation, it consistently im-
proves over target-label-free baselines and approaches supervised multimodal adaptation, requiring
no target labels or target-side optimization for the added adapter and no repeated multimodal pre-
training. ZeroMAG relies on a supplied fine-tuned EFM and adequate source-bank coverage, and
cannot incorporate modality types outside M without retraining.

A
NOTATION AND MODEL SCOPE
A.1
EEG REPRESENTATIONS
EEG representation learning has progressed from task-specific supervised networks (Craik et al.,
2019) to foundation models trained across recordings (Kuruppu et al., 2025). These models differ in
tokenization, objectives, and recording domains. LaBraM uses neural signal tokenization (Jiang
et al., 2024); EEGPT learns EEG representations through self-supervised transformer pretrain-
ing (Wang et al., 2024a).
BIOT addresses heterogeneous biosignal inputs (Yang et al., 2023),
whereas Brant focuses on intracranial neural recordings (Zhang et al., 2023). These differences
matter when defining the adapter’s input and output interface. Our experiments use the three back-
bone instantiations described below.
A.2
FROZEN MODELS AND SEPARATE GENERATORS
For each backbone, we directly load the EFM encoder checkpoint and pair it with a compatible
prediction head trained on source data for the supported task. The head has a shared feature layer
and task-specific linear output layers, and it is kept frozen throughout; no target labels are used. The
Daphnet Freezing of Gait dataset (Roggen et al., 2010), introduced in the associated study (Bächlin
et al., 2010), provides source supervision for the freezing-of-gait output; it is used to train the com-
patible head, not to fit reference adapters or train the adapter generator. EEG-only inference sets
the auxiliary residual to zero; multimodal inference adds the adapter-generated residual before the
shared head layer. Other source labels are used offline to fit reference adapters.
For a fused representation zfused = zEEG + ∆zaux, the head is

The shared projection (Ws, bs) is specific to a backbone, as are the task output layers (Wt, bt). For
a given task t, the same output layer is used across its datasets. The adapter and the frozen head
therefore share the same prediction path in EEG-only and multimodal evaluation.
We train the generators for CBraMod (Wang et al., 2025), CSBrain (Zhou et al., 2025), and Code-
Brain (Ma et al., 2026) separately. The results across these backbones evaluate separate instantia-
tions of the same framework. They do not represent the transfer of one shared generator checkpoint
from one backbone architecture to another. Within each instantiation, the adapter layout, decoder
output, frozen EEG representation, and prediction head form the applicable model interface. The
block schema is shared within an interface, but the number of blocks and total parameter count
depend on the active modalities.
Throughout the appendix, f, h, e, d, Econd, and sθ refer to the selected backbone instantiation. The
conditioning input remains the recording and task; the selected generator determines the backbone
interface.

B.6
SUPPORT OF THE CONDITION
The task factor identifies a supported prediction task rather than a dataset identifier. Dataset holdout
and task holdout are distinct: a new dataset can share a task with source data, whereas a task-
holdout target has no source adapter for its task. FoG is the task-holdout target in our benchmark; its
task factor Cτ is a learned unspecified-task embedding. Similarly, a new combination of supported
signals differs from a signal type outside M. The supported block schema defines the modality
interface; it does not by itself create a new encoder for an arbitrary signal type.

E.1
CANONICAL LATENT COORDINATES
The block posterior is defined before cross-block contextualization. Let Fη denote adding block
embeddings and applying CBT. For pre-contextual tokens Upre, the stochastic output is Fη(Upre)
and the canonical output is Fη(µ). In general,
Fη(E[Upre])̸ = E[Fη(Upre)].
(E.1)
For example, a scalar nonlinear map F(u) = u2 gives F(E[u]) = µ2 but E[F(u)] = µ2 + σ2. The
same distinction applies to nonlinear contextualization. The Stage 2 target is the explicit means-
before-CBT path in Eq. (D.6), not an estimate of the exact mean after a nonlinear transformer.

There are also two distinct discrepancies: reconstruction compares d(¯e(ω∗)) with ω∗, while genera-

conditional diffusion model recovers the appropriate latent for every recording.

recording-specific information and its alignment are useful. The generator variants compare direct
parameter regression, latent regression, diffusion, and MoE decoding. The adapter variants alter
modality-specific encoding, spectral information, and shared coordination.
Results analysis.
The modular latent MLP exceeds direct weight MLP by 2.89 points; replacing
that latent regressor with DiT contributes another 3.11 points in the displayed sequence, and the
full configuration exceeds the no-MoE variant by 5.07 points. These are incremental differences
between the listed variants, not an additive causal decomposition independent of training configu-
ration. Removing subject conditioning and shuffling it cause substantial declines, consistent with
recording-aligned information contributing beyond task and modality identity.
G.2
INTERPRETING THE INTERVENTIONS
The shuffled-subject comparison changes alignment between a condition and its recording while re-
taining the set of subject-condition vectors. Its 9.24-point decrease is consistent with the importance
of recording-aligned information. The task-plus-modality variant removes that factor and decreases
accuracy by 11.09 points. The two interventions answer related but distinct questions: availability
of subject information and correctness of its assignment.
The temporal-only variant has an 8.12-point decrease, compared with 4.16 points for a shared modal-
ity encoder and 6.58 points for mean fusion. The larger temporal-only decrease is consistent with
a useful contribution from the spectral branch in this experiment. It does not quantify an indepen-
dent spectral contribution that can be added to the other decreases, because the variants are separate
trained systems.
The reported decreases are paired with the standard deviations given in main Table 2. They are com-
puted from seed-wise paired differences rather than from the two marginal score standard deviations.

FUNCTIONAL SUPERVISION
We evaluate four functional-loss settings using CBraMod on the same set of nine held-out target

protocols. B-ACC is reported as mean ± standard deviation over the five seeds.
Evaluation.
Reconstruction and generated-weight errors use the same parameter-distance defini-
tion, comparing eω and bω, respectively, with the supervised reference ω∗. The prediction discrepancy
is DKL(pω∗∥pbω), evaluated on a fixed independent evaluation subset and averaged over samples, tar-
gets, and seeds. Full ZeroMAG is the comparison baseline in Table G.2: each error is divided by
the corresponding error of Full ZeroMAG. Its normalized values are therefore 1.00 by definition.
The normalization baseline is distinct from the reference adapter ω∗used in the underlying error
definitions; the three normalized metrics need not have equal absolute values.
Results.
Removing Stage 1 functional supervision reduces reconstruction error by 6%, yet in-
creases final generated-weight error by 5% and prediction KL by 31%; B-ACC falls by 1.15 per-
centage points. Thus, better reconstruction of reference parameters does not necessarily yield better
generated-adapter predictions. Removing Stage 2 functional supervision leaves Stage 1 reconstruc-
tion error unchanged, while generated-weight error increases by 8%, prediction KL by 48%, and
B-ACC decreases by 1.58 points. Removing both losses gives the largest observed B-ACC de-
crease, 2.87 points, and raises prediction KL by 82%. These results support functional supervision
during adapter representation learning and conditional generation.

H.2
SEPARATE BACKBONE INSTANTIATIONS
Each of CBraMod, CSBrain, and CodeBrain has a separately trained generator. The EFM encoder
is loaded from its checkpoint, and the compatible source-trained prediction head is fixed throughout
the ZeroMAG pipeline (Appendix A.2). Reference-adapter fitting updates only adapter parameters.
Functional losses use the same frozen head, whose parameters are not part of the adapter bank or
generator output. At deployment, the known task selects its output layer; no target labels or target-
side updates are used.
Within a selected instantiation, generated vectors use the block layout expected by its decoder and
adapter. This scope is essential for the meaning of bω = g(R, τ): the symbol g identifies that
instantiation’s generator, rather than one universal weight generator shared by all architectures. The
reported multi-backbone evaluation tests repeated applicability of the framework.

CALIBRATION DATA
Performance saturates at approximately 64 unlabeled calibration windows in the experiment sum-
marized in the main text. This concerns the amount of data used to form the condition, rather than
a target optimization batch size: there is no target-side fitting step. Once constructed, the condition
guides generation of the adapter used on the disjoint query subset.
A window count describes the number of calibration units rather than a universal duration in seconds.
Window duration and signal sampling rates differ across physiological tasks. The reported saturation
point therefore characterizes the evaluated calibration setting and does not imply that all recordings
or tasks require exactly the same observation duration.
I.3
SOURCE-BANK SIZE
Using 50% of the source adapter bank retains 97.2% of the full-bank multimodal gain in the reported
data-efficiency experiment. This is a 2.8% reduction in the reference gain, not a 2.8-point reduction
in B-ACC. The denominator is the full-bank multimodal gain.
Together, the calibration and bank-size summaries concern two different resource requirements.
Calibration windows are collected from a new target recording at deployment. Source adapters
are fitted and organized offline. Reducing one does not directly reduce the other, and the reported
summaries do not provide a joint optimum over calibration budget and bank size.

J
COMPARISON WITH RELATED METHODS
J.1
EXTENDED RELATED WORK
Multimodal physiological models.
SleepFM and PhysioOmni learn multimodal physiological
representations through pretraining (Thapa et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2025), whereas Brant-X uses a
pretrained EEG foundation model to align EEG and other physiological signals and trains an EXG
encoder (Zhang et al., 2024). ZeroMAG generates a plug-in adapter for a selected existing EFM,
preserving its frozen encoder and prediction head at target deployment.
Parameter-efficient and test-time adaptation.
Adapters, LoRA, and prompt tuning (Houlsby
et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2022; Lester et al., 2021; Han et al., 2024), including EEG-specific adapter
designs (Suzumura et al., 2024), reduce the number of trainable parameters but still optimize them
on labeled target data. Test-time and unsupervised domain adaptation (Liang et al., 2023; Wang
et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2020) avoid target labels but update parameters on target data, where
hyperparameters and stopping criteria are difficult to validate. ZeroMAG amortizes adaptation into
an offline-trained generator and performs no target-side gradient step.
Parameter generation.
Hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2017) and meta networks (Munkhdalai & Yu,
2017) generate weights from task representations, shared hypernetworks generate adapters for multi-
task fine-tuning (Karimi Mahabadi et al., 2021), and neural network diffusion models parameters
with latent diffusion (Wang et al., 2024b). Recent work applies parameter generation to zero-shot
adaptation of large language models: Text-to-LoRA generates LoRA adapters from natural-language
task descriptions (Charakorn et al., 2025), and Drag-and-Drop LLMs maps batches of unlabeled task
prompts to LoRA weights (Liang et al., 2025). ZeroMAG shares the goal of replacing per-target
optimization with generation, but differs in three respects: its condition is computed from unlabeled
physiological recordings rather than text; the generated adapter must accommodate varying modality
configurations within a shared block schema; and generation combines parameter-level targets with
functional agreement through the frozen EFM.
