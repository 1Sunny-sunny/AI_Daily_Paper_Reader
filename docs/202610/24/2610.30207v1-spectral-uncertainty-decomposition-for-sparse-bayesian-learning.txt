Abstract
Estimating distributed neural sources from M/EEG measurements is an ill-posed
inverse problem where principled uncertainty quantification remains challenging.
The usual conditional Gaussian posterior covariance does not explicitly distin-
guish uncertainty along likelihood-visible source directions from uncertainty that
is invisible to the measurements. We propose a spectral framework based on
the singular value structure of the prior-preconditioned forward operator, which
provides an exact, interpretable decomposition of the posterior covariance into
data-informed and indeterminacy epistemic components. This formulation iden-
tifies uncertainty contributions that are reducible by better measurements from
those that are fundamentally irreducible by sensing geometry. We further derive
an equivalent subtractive decomposition that expresses the prior covariance as the
sum of posterior and data-driven reduction terms, enabling precise accounting of
uncertainty reduction through Bayesian learning. Together, these complementary
representations offer both theoretical insight and practical tools for interpretable
uncertainty quantification in M/EEG source imaging for linear-Gaussian Bayesian
inverse solvers where the posterior covariance is available in closed form.

Introduction
Electro- and magnetoencephalography (EEG and MEG) are non-invasive techniques that measure
brain electromagnetic activity with high temporal resolution. The mathematical model of the M/EEG
sensing procedure can be described by the linear forward model:

Estimating X from Y is a severely ill-posed and ill-conditioned inverse problem: typically M ≪N,
meaning that even small perturbations in the input (sensor data) can lead to large differences in the
output (estimated sources). Regularization is therefore essential to obtain well-posed and stable source
estimates [6]. A principled view of regularization is provided by Bayesian modeling, where priors
encode structural assumptions on sources. With fixed prior parameters, maximum-a-posteriori (MAP)
estimation, or Type-I Bayesian learning, recovers many classical regularizers as special cases (e.g.,

Spectral Uncertainty Decomposition
3.1

Discussion and Outlook
We introduced two complementary representations of the posterior covariance for uncertainty quan-
tification. The additive spectral decomposition, Eq. (7), splits Σpost into a visible (data-informed)
component and a nullspace (indeterminacy) component, whereas the prior-minus-reduction identity,
Eq. (10), expresses Σpost = Σprior −Rdata with mode-wise weights 1 −ri =
s2
i
σ2+s2
i . The additive
form is most suitable for visualization because it cleanly separates reducible uncertainty confined
to the visible subspace from irreducible nullspace uncertainty; by contrast, the subtractive form is
most suitable for information accounting, since Rdata reports exactly how much prior variance the
data remove per mode and satisfies the identity Σpost + Rdata = Σprior. Regarding epistemic versus
aleatoric effects, Σpost is epistemic; aleatoric noise appears only parametrically via the shrinkage
factors ri =
1
1+s2
i /σ2 and additively in the predictive sensor covariance LΣpostL⊤+ σ2I. The
nullspace term constitutes the structurally irreducible component under the fixed geometry (e.g.,
forward operator). A practical limitation is degeneracy under empirical-Bayes sparsity: pruning
γi →0 yields zero posterior variances in some directions; although the decompositions remain valid,
such pruning may lead to overconfidence; calibration is not evaluated in this experiment. Mitigat-
ing degeneracy (e.g., via radial basis functions or treatments of hyperparameter uncertainty) is an
important direction for future work.
In summary, the additive decomposition reveals that Bayesian learning systematically reduces uncer-
tainty only in sensor-observable directions while nullspace indeterminacy remains an unavoidable
limitation. Concurrently, the subtractive decomposition demonstrates that posterior uncertainty
diminishes relative to the prior precisely when data provide stronger informative constraints.
