EEG-CAPTIONER: GROUNDING EEG-TO-LANGUAGE GENERATION IN FUNDAMENTAL
SIGNAL PROPERTIES
Leyuan Jin1, Jiahao Mei1, Yixuan Yuan2, Shimeng Li1, Mengyue Wu1, Yue Ding2
1X-LANCE Lab, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai, China
2Shanghai Mental Health Center, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai, China
ABSTRACT
Electroencephalography (EEG)-to-language models offer a promis-
ing approach for translating complex neural signals into human-
readable descriptions.
However, generating plausible language
does not necessarily demonstrate that a model has learned to in-
terpret the underlying EEG signal.
A direct way to assess such
interpretation is to probe objectively measurable properties of the
signal itself. We therefore construct an EEG-language dataset of
160,781 segments annotated with signal-level question answering
(QA) pairs, where each question targets one of five property families
and requires a quantitative comparison involving spectral, spatial,
temporal, or lateral signal properties. Each segment is also paired
with a feature-grounded EEG caption. We further propose a Dual-
Domain EEG Encoder that jointly models temporal waveforms and
spectral-spatial representations, and introduce EEG-Captioner, a
unified EEG-to-language framework trained with both signal-level
QA and EEG caption supervision. Experiments show that the pro-
posed encoder achieves the highest overall macro-averaged balanced
accuracy (Macro-BA) among the compared EEG encoders, while
EEG-Captioner achieves a Macro-BA of 88.81% on signal-level QA
and improves caption factual consistency from 82.06% to 83.46%
over caption-only language adaptation, demonstrating the comple-
mentary benefits of signal-level QA and caption supervision.
Index Terms— Electroencephalography (EEG), EEG-to-language,
large language models, EEG representation learning, EEG caption
generation
1. INTRODUCTION
Electroencephalography (EEG) provides a non-invasive and tem-
porally precise measurement of neural activity. Interpreting EEG
requires reasoning jointly about multiple signal characteristics, in-
cluding temporal dynamics, spectral patterns, spatial distributions,
and hemispheric asymmetries. Large language models (LLMs) offer
a promising way to translate such complex signals into human-
readable descriptions. However, generating plausible text does not
necessarily imply that an EEG-to-language model has captured the
underlying signal characteristics. Faithful EEG descriptions should
therefore be grounded in measurable signal characteristics.
Recent EEG-to-language studies have made progress toward
such grounding. E2-LLM [1] integrates pretrained EEG represen-
tations with an LLM for affective reasoning, but does not explic-
itly supervise the underlying signal properties. CerebraGloss [2]
instruction-tunes a vision-language model for fine-grained clinical
EEG interpretation, focusing primarily on clinically relevant EEG
characteristics. NeuroNarrator [3] incorporates spectro-spatial and

temporal modeling and uses LLM-based scoring to assess signal
properties in generated captions.
Despite these advances, exist-
ing approaches still lack explicit and comprehensive supervision
and direct evaluation of fundamental EEG properties, making it
difficult to determine whether generated descriptions are systemat-
ically grounded in the underlying temporal, spectral, spatial, and
lateral characteristics. This need for explicit signal-level supervi-
sion also raises a question of whether the EEG representations used
by language-based models preserve these signal properties. EEG
representation learning has progressed from compact convolutional
and Transformer architectures, such as EEGNet [4] and SleepTrans-
former [5], to large-scale pretrained models including BIOT [6],
EEGPT [7], LaBraM [8], and CBraMod [9]. Although strong on
downstream tasks, these models are not primarily designed to pre-
serve the EEG properties needed for grounded language generation.
To address these limitations, we first construct an EEG-language
dataset that provides explicit supervision and evaluation of funda-
mental EEG properties through signal-level question answering
(QA) and feature-grounded captions.
The QA annotations cover
five property families across temporal, spectral, spatial, and lateral
dimensions. To better preserve the signal information required by
these properties, we further propose a Dual-Domain EEG Encoder
that jointly models raw temporal dynamics and spectral-spatial
structure. Instead of serializing precomputed EEG features as text,
the encoder maps raw EEG into compact learned tokens, enabling
more efficient and scalable EEG-to-language generation. Building
on the dataset and encoder, we introduce EEG-Captioner, a uni-
fied framework jointly trained with signal-level QA and caption
supervision for grounded EEG caption generation.
Experiments show that the proposed Dual-Domain EEG En-
coder achieves higher macro-averaged balanced accuracy (Macro-
BA) than the compared encoders on signal-level QA, while joint
QA-caption training further improves both QA performance and
grounded caption generation over the single-supervision variants.
The main contributions are:
• We construct an EEG-language dataset that pairs EEG seg-
ments with signal-level QA and feature-grounded captions,
enabling explicit supervision and direct evaluation of funda-
mental EEG properties alongside caption generation.
• We propose a Dual-Domain EEG Encoder that jointly mod-
els temporal waveforms and spectral-spatial representations,
achieving stronger performance than existing EEG encoders
on the proposed signal-level QA tasks.
• We introduce EEG-Captioner, a unified EEG-to-language
framework that jointly learns from signal-level QA and cap-
tion supervision, improving both fundamental EEG under-
standing and grounded caption generation.

We construct the EEG-language dataset from the Temple Uni-
versity EEG Corpus (TUEG) v2.0.2 [10]. EEG recordings are pro-
cessed using a standardized pipeline consisting of channel harmo-
nization, filtering and resampling, fixed-length segmentation, and
multi-level quality control.
For each valid EEG segment, we compute a deterministic nu-
merical feature summary describing its temporal, spectral, spatial,
and lateral characteristics. Specifically, each segment is divided into
Early, Middle, and Last intervals, from which we extract channel-
level band power, region-of-interest (ROI)-level band power, source-
network power, inter-hemispheric asymmetry, time-domain features,
and spectral-domain features.
Based on these features, we construct signal-level QA for five
EEG property families: dominant band, strongest region, peak pe-
riod, temporal pattern, and lateral dominance. Rather than regressing
absolute feature values, which can vary substantially across record-
ings and subjects, we formulate the tasks as relational comparisons
that capture interpretable structure across frequency bands, spatial
regions, temporal intervals, and hemispheres. Each property fam-
ily contains five predefined feature conditions, yielding 25 candidate
QA pairs for each segment. Ground-truth answers are computed di-
rectly from full-precision numerical features. We further apply task-
specific validity and ambiguity criteria to remove unreliable anno-
tations, including invalid feature values, insufficient separation be-
tween competing maxima, and inconsistent temporal patterns. The
strongest region task retains fewer samples because regional compar-
isons require all constituent channels to satisfy the validity criteria.
Along with signal-level QA pairs, we generate a feature-
grounded caption for each segment. The numerical feature sum-
mary is provided to DeepSeek-V4-Flash [11], which is instructed to
describe the observed temporal, spectral, spatial, and lateral charac-
teristics while avoiding clinical or cognitive inference. Each sample
therefore contains the raw EEG segment, the retained signal-level
QA pairs, and an EEG caption, as summarized in Table 1.

formation from the raw temporal waveform and its spectral-spatial
structure. The encoder contains two parallel branches whose repre-
sentations are subsequently aligned and fused.

ducing the final EEG tokens used by downstream language tasks.
2.3. EEG-Captioner
EEG-Captioner connects the Dual-Domain EEG Encoder to an LLM
through a learnable projector. Given an EEG segment, the encoder
produces a sequence of EEG tokens, which are mapped by the pro-
jector into the LLM embedding space and provided as multimodal
context for language generation.
We jointly train the framework using the two annotation types
provided by the EEG-language dataset. For signal-level QA, the
model receives a property-specific question and generates the cor-
responding answer.
For caption generation, the model generates
a natural-language description of the signal characteristics. Both
tasks share the same EEG encoder, projector, and LLM, while task-
specific instructions specify the expected output format. The model
is jointly optimized on both types of training examples.
3. EXPERIMENTS
3.1. Experimental Setup
Dataset.
All experiments are conducted on the proposed EEG-
language dataset. The 160,781 EEG segments are split into 128,625
/ 16,078 / 16,078 training, validation, and test samples in a filename-
disjoint manner, and the same partitions are used for all experiments.
Evaluation metrics. For fundamental signal understanding, we
report balanced accuracy (BA) for each relation family and Macro-
BA, computed as their unweighted average. For caption generation,
we report BLEU-4 [14] and ROUGE-L [15] to measure lexical over-
lap with reference captions. To assess signal grounding beyond lex-
ical similarity, we further evaluate factual consistency using GPT-
5.6 Sol through the ChatGPT web interface with High reasoning ef-
fort. The evaluator compares each generated caption with a feature
summary derived from the corresponding EEG segment and assesses
four signal-level aspects: dominant spectral pattern, temporal trend,
spectral features, and spatial distribution, producing an overall fac-
tual consistency score from 0% to 100%.
Implementation details. For the classifier stage, all encoders
are trained with a single-layer classifier for 30,000 steps using
AdamW, cosine decay, 1,000 warmup steps, and weight decay 0.01.
CBraMod and LaBraM are initialized from pretrained weights and
optimized with an encoder learning rate of 3 × 10−5, whereas the
Dual-Domain encoder is trained from scratch with a learning rate
of 3 × 10−4; all classifier heads use a learning rate of 3 × 10−4.
Effective batch sizes are 16 for CBraMod/LaBraM and 48 for Dual-
Domain.
For the encoder-to-Qwen stage, the encoders from the
classifier stage are frozen and connected to frozen Qwen2.5-Omni-
7B [16] through a newly initialized projector. Low-Rank Adaptation
(LoRA) [17] is applied to the Q/K/V/O projections of the language
model (r = 8, α = 32, dropout=0.05). The projector and LoRA
parameters are trained for 30,000 steps using AdamW with a learn-
ing rate of 1 × 10−4, weight decay 0.01, and an effective batch size
of 4 on two GPUs. For EEG-Captioner, all variants use the same
frozen Dual-Domain EEG encoder obtained from the classifier stage,
thereby isolating differences in the language-adaptation strategy
from differences in EEG-encoder pretraining. For the caption-only
adaptation, the projector and LoRA parameters are re-initialized and
trained for 15,000 steps using only caption supervision at a learning
rate of 5 × 10−5. Validation performance had converged by the end
of the 15,000-step training. EEG-Captioner instead initializes the
projector and LoRA parameters from the signal-level QA checkpoint
and continues training for three consecutive 15,000-step stages with
caption fractions of 0.25, 0.75, and 0.90, respectively. All captioning
stages use AdamW with weight decay 0.01, an effective batch size
of 4, and seed 42.

4. RESULTS
Comparison of EEG Encoders. Table 2 compares different EEG
encoders under signal-level QA evaluation. At the classifier stage,
the proposed Dual-Domain EEG Encoder achieves 89.87% Macro-
BA, outperforming LaBraM (76.06%) and CBraMod (50.45%). The
gains are particularly pronounced for strongest-region, temporal-
pattern, and lateral-dominance prediction, suggesting that the pro-
posed representation better preserves information relevant to these
signal-level EEG properties.
After transferring the frozen encoders to Qwen, the Dual-
Domain encoder retains its advantage, achieving 86.06% Macro-BA
compared with 73.47% for LaBraM and 47.96% for CBraMod, sug-
gesting that the signal-level information captured by the proposed
encoder can be effectively transferred to language-based QA.
EEG input perturbation further leads to substantial performance
degradation, suggesting that the model’s predictions depend on the
EEG input rather than solely on language-side shortcuts.

5. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we present EEG-Captioner, a unified EEG-to-language
framework for grounded EEG understanding. We construct an EEG-
language dataset that pairs EEG captions with signal-level QA pairs
covering fundamental temporal, spectral, spatial, and lateral sig-
nal properties. We further introduce a Dual-Domain EEG Encoder
to model temporal and spectral-spatial information. Experiments
show that the proposed encoder achieves improved performance on
signal-level QA tasks, while joint QA-caption training improves
both signal-level QA performance and caption factual consistency.
These results suggest that incorporating explicit signal-level supervi-
sion can help improve the grounding of EEG-to-language generation
in measurable EEG properties.
