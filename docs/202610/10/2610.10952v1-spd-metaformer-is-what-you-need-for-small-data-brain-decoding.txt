# SPD-M ETA FORMER IS WHAT YOU NEED FOR SMALL -DATA BRAIN DECODING 

Zhida Wang, Wei Lyu, Guo Yu, Sui Tang 

# ABSTRACT 

Brain signal decoding is challenging because neural recordings are noisy and vary across individuals, while labeled data are often limited. Recent attention-based models on the symmetric positive definite (SPD) manifold have nevertheless achieved strong performance using covariance and connectivity representations, yet the contribution of learned token weighting remains unclear. We examine two representative architectures, MAtt (based on log-Euclidean geometry) and GBWAtt (based on generalized Bures–Wasserstein geometry), and find that their learned attention weights remain close to uniform after training. We relate this behavior to bounded similarity parameterizations that, under the original softmax scaling, limit attention-weight contrast. Moreover, replacing learned weights with uniform weights, throughout training and evaluation, has little effect on mean predictive per-formance while preserving each model’s original aggregation geometry. Motivated by these findings, we introduce SPD-MetaFormer, an attention-free architecture built on uniformly weighted Fr´ echet aggregation under log-Euclidean geometry. Its backbone uses a geodesic residual to update a summary token and a shared spectral feedforward map to transform all tokens, followed by a learned weighted readout. Token states remain SPD-valued until tangent-space classification. Across three EEG benchmarks, SPD-MetaFormer achieves competitive results relative to published Euclidean and manifold baselines. Separate matched reproductions test learned versus uniform weighting within MAtt and GBWAtt. These results suggest that, in the short-sequence and limited-data regimes studied, carefully de-signed SPD architectures can provide a simpler and effective alternative to adaptive manifold attention. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Understanding the human brain is a long-standing challenge in science and medicine. Brain decoding connects neural recordings to cognitive and clinical states, supporting brain–computer interfaces, neurorehabilitation, and the study of neurological and psychiatric conditions such as ADHD, Parkin-son’s disease, and depression (GBD 2021 Nervous System Disorders Collaborators, 2024; GBD 2019 Mental Disorders Collaborators, 2022; Wolpaw et al., 2002; Chaudhary et al., 2016; Woo et al., 2017). The learning problem is demanding because recordings are high-dimensional, noisy, and heterogeneous, while labeled data are often limited and costly to collect (Lotte et al., 2018; Varoquaux, 2018). Reliable decoding must therefore recover stable structure without overfitting to subject- and session-specific variation. Existing approaches include convolutional decoders for electroencephalography (EEG) signals (Lawhern et al., 2018; Mane et al., 2021) and methods that explicitly model second-order structure. In EEG, covariance matrices capture power and coordinated channel variation relevant to motor imagery (MI), steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEPs), and error-related negativity (ERN), while functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) connectivity describes covariation among regional time series (Barachant et al., 2012; Congedo et al., 2017; Dai et al., 2020; You & Park, 2021). Although these quantities have different neuroscientific meanings, appropriately regularized estimates from both are symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices. Once a metric is specified, SPD geometry supplies meaningful distances and averages without learning these operations from scarce labels, supporting classical Riemannian classifiers and deep SPD networks (Pennec et al., 2006; Arsigny et al., 2006; Huang & Van Gool, 2017; Brooks et al., 2019). 1arXiv:2610.10952v1 [cs.LG] 7 Oct 2026 Preprint Building on these representations, recent methods use Transformer-style attention to model inter-actions among manifold-valued tokens. Manifold attention assigns these tokens input-dependent weights before geometry-aware aggregation. MAtt (Pan et al., 2022) and GBWAtt (Jin et al., 2026) are representative examples, using Log–Euclidean and generalized Bures–Wasserstein geometries, respectively. Other methods explore deep SPD features, correlation matrices, and gyrovector spaces (Wang et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). Related Transformers likewise preserve SPD structure throughout their layers (Seraphim et al., 2024). Although these models achieve com-petitive performance, it remains unclear how much the learned attention weights contribute to the improvement. We investigate this question in MAtt and GBWAtt by comparing learned attention weights with uniform weights while keeping the rest of each architecture unchanged. We find that both models learn nearly uniform attention weights. Replacing these weights with uniform weights during both training and evaluation has little effect on mean predictive performance, while preserving each model’s original aggregation geometry (see Section 4.4; Appendix A.5 and Appendix B). Our analysis shows that bounded scores limit the contrast between attention weights at unit temperature, while temperature controls reveal no consistent benefit from sharper attention. Together, these findings suggest that attention in these two architectures acts primarily as geometry-aware averaging in the studied regime. This motivates using uniform geometric aggregation as the token mixer and designing effective transformations around it. MetaFormer, developed for computer vision tasks (Yu et al., 2022), and related work on token mixing (Tay et al., 2021) mirror these observations by demonstrating that simplified token mixers suffice for effective representations. Adapting these ideas to small-data brain decoding requires operations that preserve SPD structure while combining information across covariance groups and keeping local evidence available for classification. We therefore introduce SPD-MetaFormer, which retains local covariance tokens and refines a separate summary through uniform geometric aggregation of transformed tokens. A shared spectral transformation improves matrix conditioning, and a geometric readout combines the local tokens and summary using learned weights shared across inputs. The resulting representation enters a tangent space only for final classification. In summary, we make the following contributions: • Controlled diagnosis of manifold attention. Across three EEG benchmarks, MAtt and GBWAtt learn nearly uniform attention weights, and matched uniform-weight interventions change mean scores by at most 0.36 points. Score analysis bounds attention-weight contrast at unit temperature, while temperature controls reveal no consistent benefit from sharper attention in the studied settings (Section 4.4; Appendix B). • SPD-MetaFormer. We introduce an attention-free SPD backbone that separates local covariance memories from a global summary. Uniform Log–Euclidean aggregation of transformed memories drives a gated geodesic update of the summary, while keeping local tokens available for readout. A shared, single-parameter spectral transformation preserves positive definiteness and cannot increase the spectral condition number of any token. A geometric readout then combines local and global information using learned weights shared across inputs (Sections 3.2–3.5). • Empirical evaluation. SPD-MetaFormer achieves mean accuracies of 75 .44% and 70 .79% 

on MI and SSVEP and a mean AUC of 82 .53% on ERN, exceeding the highest baseline means in the published comparison compiled by Jin et al. (2026). On ABIDE fMRI, it achieves 79 .14% AUC, the highest mean among the compared baselines under shared subject splits.

# 3 PROPOSED METHOD 

3.1 ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW AND DESIGN RATIONALE 

We introduce SPD-MetaFormer, a covariance-based decoder that uses uniform geometric mixing directly on SPD matrices. The central design problem is to integrate information across covariance groups and preserve the evidence contributed by each group. SPD-MetaFormer addresses this problem with local covariance memory tokens that retain group-specific second-order statistics and a data-dependent summary that accumulates global information. This role separation keeps the local sources accessible throughout summary refinement and final readout. # 4 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate SPD-MetaFormer on three public EEG benchmarks spanning motor imagery (MI), steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEP), and error-related negativity (ERN). An additional study on the ABIDE fMRI cohort examines whether the same SPD processing architecture transfers beyond EEG, with the complete protocol and results reported in Appendix C. The evaluation first establishes its predictive performance against existing convolutional and geometry-aware EEG models. Controlled comparisons then examine the contribution of the complete SPD processing path and its static geometric readout. Finally, we revisit MAtt and GBWAtt through locked-uniform interventions and allocation-entropy measurements to determine whether their content-dependent weights contribute to predictive performance. 

Datasets and protocol. We use BCIC-IV-2a for four-class MI decoding with nine subjects (Brunner et al., 2008), MAMEM-SSVEP-II for five-class SSVEP decoding with eleven subjects (Nikolopoulos, 2016), and BCI-ERN for binary ERN detection with sixteen subjects (Perrin et al., 2012), following prior manifold-attention studies (Pan et al., 2022; Jin et al., 2026). Each model is trained independently for each subject using seeds 0–9. For MI, we form a class-stratified validation subset from the training session and use the separate evaluation session for testing. For SSVEP and ERN, the first four sessions support training and validation (one held out), and the final session is used for testing, following MAtt (Pan et al., 2022). All paired ablations share the data splits and select the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss. Full training settings appear in Appendix A.3. 

Metrics and uncertainty. We report accuracy for MI/SSVEP and ROC-AUC for imbalanced ERN, on a 0–100 scale. ERN AUC follows the evaluation convention of MAtt and GBWAtt (Pan et al., 2022; Jin et al., 2026). Our scores average seeds within subjects, then subjects equally; ± values and error bars report the mean within-subject sample SD across runs, also used for entropy dispersion. Paired bootstrap intervals resample subjects after seed averaging and are interpreted descriptively without multiplicity correction (Appendix A.3). Published baselines retain their source uncertainty definitions. 4.1 COMPARISON WITH EXISTING METHODS

The benchmark results do not determine whether performance arises from the convolutional frontend, covariance tokenization alone, or the complete SPD block and static geometric readout. We therefore construct four controls that share the frontend initialization, subject–seed grid, and optimization recipe of SPD-MetaFormer. Convolutional features only flattens the feature map F = fconv (E)

and applies a linear classifier, removing covariance tokenization and all subsequent SPD operations. 

SPD tangent readout retains the covariance tokens, data-dependent summary, and shared congruence map We, but replaces the SPD block and static geometric readout with a linear classifier applied to the concatenated log-vectorizations of Z(0) = ( X1, . . . , X m, S ). Euclidean MetaFormer preserves the same covariance tokenization and shared congruence map, then processes the log-vectorized tokens using a pre-LayerNorm uniform mixer, additive residuals, and a two-layer GELU MLP with an expansion ratio of four, followed by mean readout. Memory-only geometric readout removes the summary and the SPD block, applying the static geometric readout (equation 12) to X1, . . . , X m

alone. SPD-MetaFormer outperforms all four controls on every dataset (Table 2). Relative to direct SPD tangent readout, it improves MI and SSVEP accuracy by 5.17 and 11 .44 points and ERN AUC by 1.66 points. The Euclidean MetaFormer also performs worse despite having more parameters (Appendix A.4). Retaining the geometric readout but removing the summary and SPD block still lowers MI, SSVEP, and ERN by 1.03 , 2.86 , and 1.70 points. The paired subject-bootstrap intervals exclude zero in all twelve comparisons. The control hierarchy argues against four simpler explanations for the observed performance. The convolutional frontend alone is insufficient, constructing SPD tokens followed by direct tangent classification is insufficient, geometric readout of the local memories alone is insufficient, and generic uniform mixing in a Euclidean backbone does not recover the full model. These results support the complete SPD processing path rather than uniform mixing alone. The inverse-log score used by both implementations further constrains the attainable alloca-tion contrast (Pan et al., 2022; Jin et al., 2026). For a nonnegative distance dij , the score

The score parameterization therefore bounds the pairwise allocation ratio independently of the input. This structural constraint alone does not imply the near-maximal empirical entropies. Temperature controls further show that sharper attention does not consistently improve performance over uniform mixing (Appendix B). Together, the interven-tion, entropy measurements, and score analysis show that MAtt and GBWAtt operate in a nearly uniform regime in which content-dependent weighting has little effect on predictive performance. Combined with the whole-path and readout controls, this diagnosis supports the design emphasis of SPD-MetaFormer on uniform geometric mixing, shared spectral transformation, and static geometric readout. 

# 5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we revisited the role of adaptive manifold attention in small-data brain decoding. Matched experiments on MAtt and GBWAtt show that their learned allocations remain close to uni-form and that replacing them with locked-uniform weights has little effect on predictive performance in the settings studied. Motivated by this finding, we introduced SPD-MetaFormer, an attention-free architecture built around uniform geometric mixing, geodesic summary refinement, shared spectral transformation, and geometric readout. Across three EEG benchmarks, SPD-MetaFormer achieves competitive performance relative to existing Euclidean and manifold-based methods, while additional results on ABIDE support its applicability to fMRI connectivity. Overall, our results suggest that, for short sequences and limited data, the effectiveness of SPD models can arise from geometry-aware representation, transformation, and aggregation without requiring strongly selective input-dependent weighting. An important direction for future work is to understand why optimization nevertheless drives existing manifold-attention models toward nearly uniform allocations. Although bounded similarity scores constrain attainable attention contrast, they do not fully explain the near-maximal entropies observed in practice. # C FUNCTIONAL CONNECTIVITY : ABIDE-I 

We evaluate SPD-MetaFormer on ABIDE-I autism classification to assess its applicability to fMRI connectivity beyond EEG, keeping the backbone unchanged. 

Data and tokens. The CPAC-preprocessed ABIDE-I release (Di Martino et al., 2014) of the Preprocessed Connectomes Project (Craddock et al., 2013) contains 1,035 subjects (505 with autism, 530 controls) from 20 sites. We use its band-pass-filtered and global-signal-regressed CC200 time series (Craddock et al., 2012). Four evenly spaced windows of round( T / 2) frames, spanning each 

T -frame scan from its first to last frame, provide four memory tokens; the full scan provides the summary token. Each token is a shrunk Pearson correlation matrix 0.3R + 0 .7I200 . The entry map (Equation 5) projects these tokens to d = 32 , without a convolutional frontend. The resulting model has 10,538 parameters. 

Protocol. All methods share ten 80/10/10 subject splits (828/103/104 subjects; seeds 0–9), stratified by site and diagnosis. SPD-MetaFormer uses a fixed AdamW recipe: learning rate 2 × 10 −3,weight decay 10 −4, batch size 32, class-weighted cross-entropy, gradient-norm clipping at 5, and cosine decay over at most 20 epochs. The best validation-AUC checkpoint is retained, with early stopping after five unimproved epochs. AUC uses positive-class probabilities; accuracy and balanced accuracy use the threshold in {0.20 , 0.21 , . . . , 0.80 } maximizing validation accuracy. Each test split is evaluated once. We report mean ± population SD ( ddof = 0 ) across the ten splits. 

Baselines. Euclidean baselines use the unshrunk full-scan correlation R: MLP and L2 logistic regression on its upper triangle, BrainNetCNN (Kawahara et al., 2017), and a two-layer GCN on thresholded R (Kipf & Welling, 2017). SPDNet-BN (Brooks et al., 2019) uses the shrunk full-scan matrix with BiMap widths 100/50/30. MAtt (Pan et al., 2022) uses the same five tokens as SPD-MetaFormer and dout = 32 , with divided-difference spectral derivatives and a validation-selected AdamW recipe.
