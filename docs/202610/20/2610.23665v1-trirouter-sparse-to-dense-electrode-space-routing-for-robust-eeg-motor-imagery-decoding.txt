ABSTRACT 

Motor imagery brain-computer interfaces require reliable decoding from multichannel EEG, but practical acquisi-tion often yields sparse or corrupted electrode montages. We propose TriRouter, an electrode-space routing frame-work with discriminative-preservation, mask-robustness, and latent-compensation paths. A validation-calibrated router uses observable channel availability to allocate path contri-butions, preserving complete-channel discrimination while increasing representation recovery under severe missingness. In BNCI2014-001, TriRouter outperforms standard public backbones and a dynamic-spatial-filtering corrupted-EEG baseline. It improves accuracy over the strongest corrupted-EEG baseline by 3.68, 4.02, and 6.46 percentage points at nominal 25%, 50%, and 75% missing settings, and remains effective under structured regional missingness and additional low-channel dataset evaluation. 

Index Terms — EEG, motor imagery, missing channels, robust decoding, path fusion 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) translate neural activity into control commands and have broad value in neuroreha-bilitation, assistive control, and human-computer interaction [1]. Among noninvasive BCI modalities, EEG is widely used because it is portable, low cost, and temporally precise. Motor imagery (MI) is a mature EEG-BCI paradigm that de-codes user intention from sensorimotor rhythm modulation, as described by Pfurtscheller and Lopes da Silva [2]. Recent deep MI-EEG decoders have improved complete-channel classification, but practical deployment still faces un-stable channel availability. Electrode detachment, abnormal impedance, artifact rejection, and low-density replacement can all make the test-time channel set different from train-ing. For models that rely on learned spatial filters or spatio-temporal dependencies, missing channels remove informa-tion and shift inter-channel statistics. This can cause a sharp degradation when complete-channel models are evaluated on incomplete inputs [3].

The central difficulty is that missing-channel decoding changes its nature as the missing rate increases. Mildly in-complete inputs still contain useful spatially discriminative cues, while severely incomplete inputs depend more on re-lation recovery and robust aggregation. Treating all missing rates with a single augmentation strategy may, therefore, sac-rifice low-missingness discrimination or provide insufficient high-missingness robustness. Motivated by this observation, we formulate robust missing-channel MI-EEG decoding as electrode-space com-pletion with missing-severity-conditioned path allocation. We propose TriRouter. The framework constructs three de-coding paths around discriminative preservation, mask ro-bustness, and latent compensation, and uses the observable missing rate of each test sample to route predictions under a functional prior calibrated to validation. This design pre-serves fine-grained spatial discrimination under complete and mildly incomplete inputs while strengthening cross-channel representation recovery under severe missingness. It there-fore mitigates the tension between low-missingness accuracy preservation and high-missingness robustness. The main contributions are as follows. 1. We propose TriRouter, a sparse-to-dense electrode-space routing framework that treats channel availability as an acquisition state for robust EEG decoding. 2. We design three complementary paths for discrimina-tive preservation, mask robustness, and latent compen-sation and calibrate their fusion from validation perfor-mance and a missing-severity functional prior. 3. Experiments show consistent gains over public back-bones and a corrupted-EEG baseline under random, re-gional, and low-channel missingness. 

2. RELATED WORK 

Early MI-EEG decoding mainly relied on common spatial patterns, filter banks, and linear classifiers, while BCI Com-petition IV and MOABB established widely used benchmarks and evaluation protocols [4, 5]. Recent studies further em-phasize the importance of protocol consistency for reliable arXiv:2610.23665v1 [cs.LG] 16 Oct 2026 Fig. 1 . TriRouter for sparse-to-dense electrode-space routing under missing channels. comparison [6]. Deep learning has since advanced end-to-end EEG representation learning. Representative models include ShallowConvNet [7], EEGNet [8], FBCNet [9], attention-based architectures [10], EEG Conformer [11], and CTNet [12]. More recent work explores multi-branch modeling, dilated temporal convolutions, CSP-enhanced networks, flex-ible variable-channel representations, and foundation-model-style MI learning [13, 14, 15, 16, 17]. However, most of these methods still assume consistent channel sets between training and testing. Robust EEG modeling addresses missing or corrupted channels through signal completion [18], generative inpaint-ing [19], dynamic spatial filtering [3], channel dropout [20], and few-channel prediction [21]. These approaches improve tolerance to channel variability, but usually rely on a single model or a unified robustness strategy across all missing-ness levels. As a result, they do not explicitly distinguish the need to preserve discriminative spatial information under mild missingness from the need for stronger compensation under severe channel loss. Although dynamic spatial fil-tering and flexible variable-channel modeling suggest that observable acquisition states can guide adaptation [3, 16], missing-severity-aware functional specialization and fusion remain underexplored. 

3. METHOD 

TriRouter treats an incomplete EEG montage as a sparse ob-servation of an underlying dense electrode-space represen-tation. It decomposes decoding into three complementary paths: discriminative preservation, mask robustness, and la-tent compensation. Instead of forcing a single encoder to pre-serve complete-channel discrimination and compensate se-vere channel loss simultaneously, it assigns these compet-ing objectives to specialized paths. A validation-calibrated router then adapts their contributions according to the ob-served missing severity, as shown in Fig. 1.

4. EXPERIMENTS 4.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate TriRouter on BNCI2014-001 (BCI Competition IV 2a) [4] and BNCI2014-004 (BCI Competition IV 2b), fol-lowing the official subject-specific train-test protocol on all nine subjects. For BNCI2014-001, nominal 0%, 25%, 50%, and 75% missing settings remove 0, 6, 11, and 16 of 22 chan-nels, i.e., actual ratios of 0%, 27.27%, 50%, and 72.73%; the router computes ρ from the binary mask. BNCI2014-004 is evaluated with 0, 1, and 2 missing channels. We compare with EEGNet [8], ShallowConvNet [7], FBCNet [9], CTNet [12], and DSF-CTNet [3]. Public backbones and DSF-CTNet use the same random channel-missing augmentation range, while TriRouter uses the path-specific objectives in Sec. 3.1. Model selection and routing calibration use only the training-set validation split. Accuracy is reported as mean ± standard deviation over nine subjects and three random seeds.

4.3. Mechanism Analysis 

The ablation study examines whether the gains come from functional specialization rather than added capacity. Fig. 4 shows a clear shift in path reliability as channels are removed: the discriminative path works best when the spatial layout is intact, the latent-compensation path dominates under severe missingness, and the mask-robust path stabilizes the interme-diate range. Table 2 supports this interpretation. Uniform fusion uses the same paths but ignores the reliability shift. Validation-only calibration improves the severe-missingness case but is not uniformly better than uniform fusion at lower miss-ing rates, whereas adding the functional prior yields a more Fig. 3 . Removing the latent-compensation path mainly weakens high-missingness robustness, removing the discriminative path hurts complete and mildly incomplete inputs, and removing the mask-robust path reduces low-to-medium stability. These results make a capacity-only expla-nation unlikely and show that calibrated path assignment is central to the gain. Table 3 shows that the compensation path recovers meaningful signal structure. It greatly reduces missing-channel log-bandpower error relative to zero filling. The reported Pearson correlation is computed between recon-structed missing-channel bandpower vectors and the cor-responding complete-trial targets, linking high-missingness gains to latent recovery quality.

5. CONCLUSION 

This paper proposed TriRouter for robust MI-EEG decod-ing under missing channels. TriRouter decomposes decoding into discriminative preservation, mask robustness, and latent compensation, and fuses them through a validation-calibrated missing-severity router. Experiments show consistent gains over public backbones and a corrupted-EEG baseline under random and regional missingness. Latent recovery analysis further confirms that compensation restores meaningful band-power structure, suggesting that missing severity is an infor-mative state for adaptive EEG decoding. 6.
