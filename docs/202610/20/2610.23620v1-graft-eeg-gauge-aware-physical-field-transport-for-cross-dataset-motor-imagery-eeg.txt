ABSTRACT 

Cross-dataset EEG decoding often treats montage, reference, and active-sensor changes as representation shift, although they also alter the observation operator. We study target-statistics-blind transfer: fitting receives no target signal, label, or empirical statistic, and electrode coordinates only instantiate a fixed prediction operator. G RAFT -EEG removes the active-set common-reference gauge and trans-ports each native mask through a template motor leadfield. A source-fitted spectral classifier anchors the decision; a bounded state-space-model (SSM) residual is sec-ondary. We establish restricted identifiability and an operator-perturbation bound. Across five leave-one-dataset-out targets (253 participants), dynamic G RAFT -EEG achieved 60.865% equal-target balanced accuracy versus 52.684% for strict-clean 5/5 BIOT (hierarchical difference +8 .182 points; 95% CI +6 .382 to +10 .196 ;positive in every target). On four EEGPT-clean targets, the descriptive means were 61.520% and 59.057%. In four post-hoc exact same-parent tests, FULL exceeded coordinate-basis, matched-spectrum, row-shuffled, and wrong-anatomy controls by 9.959, 9.698, 11.241, and 3.985 points, respectively (all 95% CIs excluded zero; Holm-adjusted P < 0.001 ). A separate 60-participant same-paradigm evaluation retained a positive physical-operator contrast ( +4 .029 points; +2 .338 to +5 .773 ), whereas dynamic–static was inconclusive; its preregistered status is partial con-firmation rather than unrestricted external validation. The SSM added +1 .059 

points ( +0 .013 to +1 .892 ). Claims remain bounded to five binary motor-imagery domains and the tested operators. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Public motor-imagery EEG datasets differ in electrode count, location, reference, amplifier, sessions, and unusable channels. Transfer therefore couples physiological and procedural shift with a change in observation coordinates. Channel intersection resolves tensor shape by discarding native sensors; learned adapters can retain them but may obscure which target information fitted the adapter. Large EEG models learn reusable representations through masking, tokenization, and diverse corpora (Jiang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; 2025; Jiang et al., 2025). BIOT tokenizes channels separately; newer systems use coordinates or learned channel-unification modules (Yang et al., 2023; El Ouahidi et al., 2025; D¨ oner et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026). These advances do not isolate whether a full unseen montage can be decoded before opening any target signal or empirical statistic. We test explicit observation-operator transport under that fixed contract, a distinction that matters when compact BCI decoders remain competitive and conclusions are protocol-sensitive (Yang et al., 2026). In our target-statistics-blind heterogeneous-montage contract, declared coordinates may instantiate geometry-only operators, but fitting uses source signals and labels only. Target geometry supplies no covariance, normalization, calibration, validation loss, or stopping signal; after source fitting, target signals are mapped once and labels reach only the scorer. Exact same-parent controls fix split, seed, head, features, ridge rule, and budget while replacing the motor leadfield with coordinate-only, spectrum-preserving random, row-shuffled, or wrong-anatomy operators. GRAFT -EEG first removes the unidentifiable common-reference component on each trial’s active channels and transports the remaining field through a mask-specific inverse into a fixed bilateral motor basis. It then separates a static spectral anchor from a small bounded state-space residual, whose zero initialization recovers the static classifier and whose stable decay bounds the recurrent correction. Our contributions are: • a precise access contract for cross-dataset EEG decoding that distinguishes target geometry from target signal, label, and distributional statistics; • an active-set reference quotient and physical motor-field transport into an eight-dimensional latent, with restricted identifiability, an operator-perturbation bound, a static anchor, and a secondary bounded residual; • a five-domain evaluation with strict and partial denominators, domain-aware uncertainty, four exact same-parent operator controls, baseline-health diagnostics, and a same-paradigm partial-confirmation external evaluation. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Large EEG models use masked pretraining, channel tokenization, coordinates, or learned queries to accommodate heterogeneous layouts (Yang et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; 2025; Jiang et al., 2025; El Ouahidi et al., 2025; D¨ oner et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026). Reported downstream protocols are not uniformly target-statistics-blind, so their results do not answer the same access-constrained question. We study a boundary in which the entire target dataset remains unopened during fitting, except for its declared electrode coordinates. G RAFT -EEG is therefore neither a foundation model nor a claim of topology-agnostic inference. Geometry-based FI/SSI and Riemannian alignment provide strong alternatives to common-channel selection (Mellot et al., 2024). Related cortical work uses participant-specific surface eigenmodes to constrain EEG/MEG source imaging (Wang et al., 2026). Our operator instead uses montage coordinates and a shared template motor basis for decoding; it is not participant-specific source localization. The matched FI/SSI controls receive the same native active channels and are denied target recentering, covariance fitting, and calibration. EEGNet and recent state-space decoders motivate the compact heads (Lawhern et al., 2018; Ma et al., 2026; Ryoo et al., 2025), but our temporal branch is restricted to a bounded residual and supports no streaming claim.

6 RESULTS 

6.1 STRICT FIVE -TARGET COMPARISON 

Dynamic G RAFT -EEG achieved 60.865% equal-target BA, static G RAFT -EEG 59.806%, and BIOT 52.684% (Figure 3c). Dynamic exceeded BIOT by +8 .182 pp (hierarchical 95% interval, +6 .382 

to +10 .196 ); all five target effects were positive ( +6 .506 to +10 .437 pp), and leave-one-target-out estimates were 7.618–8.600 pp. Cho was the worst target for both dynamic G RAFT -EEG (58.172%) and BIOT (50.086%). The exact same-parent ladder separated FULL from every replacement operator (Figure 3a). FULL exceeded the coordinate basis by +9 .959 pp (95% CI +8 .214 to +11 .771 ), the matched-spectrum 7random operator by +9 .698 pp ( +8 .348 to +11 .052 ), the row-shuffled operator by +11 .241 pp (+9 .450 to +13 .174 ), and the wrong-anatomy leadfield by +3 .985 pp ( +3 .231 to +4 .752 ). All four target-stratified paired-subject tests had two-sided bootstrap P < 0.001 and remained below 0.001 

after Holm correction. The coordinate effect was positive in every target ( +6 .79 to +13 .67 pp). These controls distinguish the tested motor leadfield from geometry-only, spectrum-preserving, correspondence-breaking, and anatomically mismatched alternatives; they do not establish superiority over every possible physical or learned operator. The SSM is secondary to cross-montage harmonization: dynamic minus static was +1 .059 pp (hierarchical 95% interval, +0 .013 to +1 .892 ; Table S10). Four target effects were positive, whereas BNCI was −0.586 pp ( −2.423 to +1 .497 ); leave-one-target-out estimates were 0.792–1.470 pp. The tighter subject-paired interval ( +0 .584 to +1 .569 pp) is within-panel evidence, not the principal cross-dataset interval. In the stricter source-only-geometry sensitivity, all 25 fits were frozen before the target montage was opened. Dynamic and static BA were 60.460% and 59.713%, respectively; dynamic was 0.406 pp below its canonical result and exceeded static by +0 .747 pp. The held-out montage is still required after sealing to instantiate the fixed prediction operator, so the method is not geometry-blind. Per-target values and the independent audit are in Appendix C. 6.2 COVERAGE , FAIRNESS , AND EXTERNAL BOUNDARY 

BIOT is the best-performing clean pretrained comparator with a 5/5 denominator and is therefore the primary foundation-model comparison. EEGPT and LaBraM executed 25/25 target–seed cells but are strict-clean on 4/5 targets because PhysionetMI overlaps with pretraining. CodeBrain also executed 25/25 cells but is provenance-ineligible on 0/5 targets; no CodeBrain score enters a strict comparison. On the same four clean targets available to EEGPT (BNCI, Cho, Lee, and Shin), dynamic GRAFT -EEG averaged 61.520% BA and EEGPT 59.057%, a descriptive difference of +2 .463 pp. The target effects were −1.328 , +8 .857 , −0.667 , and +2 .989 pp, respectively. This 4/5 comparison is not inserted into the complete 5/5 ranking. Figure S13 preserves the full matrix and all eligibility denominators. A read-only post-score diagnostic checked source validation, target positive rates, prediction entropy, target–seed coverage, and single-class counts without training or parameter updates. Dynamic and static G RAFT -EEG met the prespecified healthy-source rule and had no single-class target cells. BIOT was nondegenerate but had marginal source validation (mean BA 53.6%); several other baselines were already near chance on source validation, and the classical CSP/FBCSP/Riemann rows had systematic single-class target predictions with source validation unavailable. No direct implementation defect was confirmed. Accordingly, weak target results are not attributed uniformly to domain shift, and collapsed or source-weak baselines are not used as mechanistic evidence for GRAFT -EEG; Appendix E reports the complete diagnostic. Supplementary analyses retain the original denominators. Under the registered few-shot procedure, revealing 1%, 5%, or 10% target labels and fine-tuning the full GRAFT head gave 61.323%, 61.477%, and 61.722% BA, respectively, compared with 60.865% in the zero-target protocol. These small increments contextualize the absolute zero-target BA under this adaptation rule; they are not a fully supervised oracle or a performance ceiling. In the separate 60-participant Dreyer2023A evaluation, dynamic versus static was +0 .57 pp [ −0.08 , +1 .23 ] (inconclusive), whereas physics versus no-physics was +4 .03 pp [ +2 .34 , +5 .77 ] (positive). The preregistered decision is partial confirmation ,not unrestricted external validation. The post-primary coordinate sensitivity refitted no model. Across 50 target–condition units, reduced-operator change tracked dynamic BA change ( ρ = −0.449 ; 95% CI −0.760 to −0.282 ; permutation 

P < 0.001 ); at 20 mm, equal-target BA changed by −1.064 pp for tangent and −2.220 pp for rigid errors. Responses were heterogeneous (Figure S14). 

7 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS 

The central finding concerns the observation operator rather than the temporal head. Without target signals, labels, or statistics during fitting, the native-montage model exceeded the strongest clean 5/5 8pretrained comparator by 8.18 pp in every target. Under the same parent and budget, replacing the motor leadfield with four distinct controls reduced BA by 3.98–11.24 pp. The smaller but still positive wrong-anatomy contrast shows that an anatomically structured template is not interchangeable with the task-matched motor field; the larger coordinate, spectrum-matched, and row-shuffled contrasts additionally reject explanations based only on dimension, singular spectrum, or electrode-wise response magnitude. The ladder remains post-hoc and operator-specific rather than proving that physics is universally optimal. Propositions 1 and 2 state the corresponding observability and conditioning limits. The SSM contribution was modest and heterogeneous ( +1 .06 pp overall; four positive targets; 

−0.59 pp on BNCI; inconclusive external contrast), supporting an incremental interpretation. The row-shuffled and spectrum-matched controls in Appendix K evaluate inverse recovery with synthetic known sources; they are not a real-EEG same-parent classification ladder. Participant-specific source recovery lies beyond scope. After the primary scores were fixed, three of four matched FI/SSI intervals excluded zero; FI+tangent crossed zero, so this supplementary evidence remains contrast-specific rather than supporting a general superiority claim. Calibration lowered probability error while leaving discrimination unchanged, and performance losses under stress reached −8.93 pp. In the separately registered legacy three-target stability diagnostics, GRAFT -EEG showed greater seed-to-seed dispersion than the coordinate and no-physics comparators. Those adverse dispersion results are distinct from the new five-target exact same-parent performance control. Failed conditions also outnumbered recoverable ones in the known-source tests. Claims are consequently confined to the operating range characterized by these evaluations, rather than extended to unrestricted inverse recovery. The independent generalization unit is the held-out dataset ( n = 5 ), not the 253 participants, and dataset remains confounded with device and protocol. The primary task is binary hand motor imagery; non-motor paradigms were not tested. Blindness applies to target statistics, not declared target coordinates, and the source-only-geometry experiment is sensitivity analysis rather than a replacement for the fixed primary result. The fsaverage basis is a common coordinate system, not participant-specific localization, so causal and clinical interpretations remain outside scope. Coordinate sensitivity is now characterized only for two synthetic error families on the five declared montages; it neither identifies a clinical placement threshold nor covers arbitrary cap deformation or anatomy. Matched FI/SSI and other post-score diagnostics are supplementary; arbitrary montages, streaming, and clinical deployment remain outside the evidence boundary. 

8 CONCLUSION 

Across five leave-one-dataset-out targets, G RAFT -EEG operated on native montages and trial-level active masks without using target signals, labels, or fitting statistics. Dynamic G RAFT -EEG achieved 60.865% equal-target BA, compared with 52.684% for strict BIOT. Four exact same-parent replace-ments reduced BA by 3.985–11.241 pp, supporting a material contribution from the task-matched physical observation operator within this parent model. This remains operator-specific evidence from five binary motor-imagery domains, not a universal claim over geometry models or EEG paradigms. The bounded SSM supplied a smaller, target-dependent increment. 

Reproducibility. Fitting, label-blind prediction, and scoring are process-separated. Immutable manifests bind queues, masks, checkpoints, predictions, and result tables; complete gates and source-data hashes appear in Appendices S–T. The reproducibility package retains figure source data and executed-cell ledgers for every denominator.

Q IMPLEMENTATION AND REGISTERED INFERENCE 

Active-set quality control. A channel is marked inactive if it contains a non-finite value, exceeds 5,000 μV in absolute amplitude, or has peak-to-peak amplitude below 0.1 μV. Trials are removed when more than 20% of native channels are inactive or fewer than nine channels remain. The queue record binds the resulting mask to its subject and trial identifiers. 33 Template basis and source-only selection. For each hemisphere, the fsaverage forward model is restricted to the prespecified motor region. Gauge-projected lead fields from the five declared montages are stacked, and the four leading right-singular vectors are retained; each mode sign is set by its largest-magnitude loading. Source-library validation searches ρ ∈ { 10 −6, 10 −4, 10 −2, 10 −1} and 

C ∈ { 0.01 , 0.1, 1, 10 }, maximizing the unweighted mean subject-level BA. Ties favor the stronger ridge and then the smaller C. The dynamic branch uses AdamW (learning rate 10 −3; weight decay 

10 −4), batch size 128, gradient clipping at 5, a maximum of 60 epochs, and patience 10. After selecting the epoch count, the branch is refitted on all source trials. 

Exact same-parent operator ladder. The coordinate control evaluates the constant-free real spherical-harmonic basis {Ylm : l ∈ { 1, 2}, −l ≤ m ≤ l} on unit electrode directions; degree zero is omitted because the active-reference quotient removes the common mode. The matched-spectrum control uses deterministic random left and right singular vectors with the physical operator’s eight singular values. The row-shuffle control applies a no-fixed-point permutation to electrode rows, preserving the operator entries while breaking electrode–response correspondence. The wrong-anatomy control uses an equally sized visual-cortex leadfield solved with the same fsaverage head model. Each operator passes through the same active-reference projection and ridge inverse as FULL and is refitted on the same source libraries under inherited hyperparameters and epoch budgets. All 75 new fits were frozen before the 75 target evaluations began; physical, coordinate, and source-only sealed results were reused read-only. The independent composite audit covers 253 participants, 1,012 seed-averaged subject–control rows, and 5,060 subject–seed rows, and returned PASS ( P 0 = P 1 = 0 ). Source-only geometry remains a separately sealed sensitivity and is excluded from the four-control Holm family. 

Calibration and uncertainty. Temperature calibration is fitted on source predictions only. Relia-bility curves pool trial counts into 15 fixed-width bins without smoothing; displayed bin intervals use 20,000 seed–target-cell bootstrap replicates. Scalar calibration metrics are computed per target and then averaged equally across targets. Main effects first average the five registered seeds within subject, sample the five target datasets with replacement, and then resample subjects within each selected target (20,000 replicates; seed 20260829). We report target-resolved effects, leave-one-target-out recomputations, and the worst target alongside the hierarchical interval. FI/SSI supplementary effects use 20,000 replicates with audit seed 20260824.

Five physiological parameters are sampled by stratified Latin hypercube for each target–seed pair.
