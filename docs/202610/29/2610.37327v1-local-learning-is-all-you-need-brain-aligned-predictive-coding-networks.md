## 摘要
预测编码（PC）是关于大脑表征如何通过分层、局部、自监督的基于预测的学习而涌现的一种有影响力的理论，无需全局传播的误差信号。尽管有大量证据支持PC，一个显著的悖论依然存在：基于这些具有生物学基础原理构建的计算模型，在性能和大脑对齐方面始终无法匹敌最先进的深度神经网络。在此，我们证明这一悖论是可以被克服的。我们引入了预测脑网络（PBNs），这是一个分层递归框架，实现了基于PC的局部学习，并达到了与人类腹侧视觉通路的表征对齐，匹配或超越了现有的视觉模型。重要的是，PBN的自上而下预测编码了独特的、与大脑对齐的信息，这些信息在自下而上的特征提取过程中未被捕获，并且这一贡献在局部训练的变体中更大。此外，PBN在下游分类任务上显著优于现有的受PC启发的网络，再现了PC的关键特征（如灵长类皮层中所观察到的那样，高层预测塑造低层视觉反应），并支持不确定性估计。因此，PBN通过结合两个在很大程度上被分离的特性，弥合了计算神经科学中长期存在的差距：预测编码的脑启发学习机制与现代深度网络的表征能力。它们提供了一个可扩展的框架，用于研究基于生物学的学习，而无需牺牲产生丰富神经表征的能力。

## Abstract
Predictive Coding (PC) is an influential account of how brain representations emerge through hierarchical, local, self-supervised prediction-based learning, without requiring globally propagated error signals. Despite extensive evidence supporting PC, a striking paradox persists: computational models built on these biologically grounded principles have consistently failed to match state-of-the-art Deep Neural Networks in performance and brain-alignment. Here, we show that this paradox can be overcome. We introduce Predictive Brain Networks (PBNs), a hierarchical recurrent framework that implements local PC-based learning and achieves representational alignment with the human ventral visual stream matching or exceeding established vision models. Importantly, PBN top-down predictions encode unique, brain-aligned information not captured during bottom-up feature extraction, with this contribution increasing in locally trained variants. Further, PBNs substantially outperform existing PC-inspired networks on downstream classification tasks, reproduce key signatures of PC (with higher-level predictions shaping lower-level visual responses as observed in the primate cortex), and support uncertainty estimation. PBNs therefore close a longstanding gap in computational neuroscience by combining two properties that have been largely separated: the brain-inspired learning mechanisms of predictive coding and the representational power of modern deep networks. They provide a scalable framework for studying biologically grounded learning without sacrificing the capacity to produce rich neural representations.
