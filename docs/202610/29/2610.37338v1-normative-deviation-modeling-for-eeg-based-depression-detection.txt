# Normative Deviation Modeling for EEG-based Depression Detection 

Wan Jiang 1, Jingqi Cheng 2, Hui Zhang 1, Xu Zhao 2, Xiaojing Chen 2, Shengeng Tang 1, Jingjing Wu 1, Richang Hong 11Hefei University of Technology 2Anhui University 

## Abstract 

Web-enabled digital health services are expanding access to physi-ological monitoring and mental health screening. However, elec-troencephalography (EEG)-based major depressive disorder (MDD) detection remains challenging across heterogeneous data sources, where individual variability and acquisition differences may affect disease-related representations. In this work, we investigate the use of healthy normative references for EEG-based MDD detec-tion. Specifically, structural and distributional reference models are independently trained using healthy control (HC) recordings to characterize departures from healthy EEG patterns. We develop two diagnostic approaches that incorporate normative deviations at different representation levels. Scalar-level Normative Deviation-guided Diagnosis (S-NDD) summarizes structural and distributional deviations into scalar scores and integrates them with deep EEG representations. Feature-level Normative Deviation-guided Diagno-sis (F-NDD) retains dimension-wise residuals and combines them with deep EEG representations through attention-based aggrega-tion and auxiliary supervision. We also employ target HC-assisted score calibration for cross-source evaluation without updating the trained models. Experiments on two public EEG datasets exam-ine both approaches under subject-independent within-source and bidirectional cross-source settings. The results demonstrate the ef-fectiveness of normative deviation-guided diagnosis across different evaluation settings.

## 1 Introduction 

Web-enabled digital health services have created new opportunities for disease screening and health monitoring beyond conventional clinical settings [9, 43]. The demand for such services is particularly urgent in Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) screening, given the limited access to mental healthcare and persistent stigma-related barriers [45, 47]. Among various screening modalities, electroen-cephalography (EEG)-based MDD detection has attracted consider-able attention, as EEG provides objective physiological measure-ments and generates digital signals that can be readily transmitted for remote analysis [19, 31]. Recent deep learning methods have achieved promising results by modeling the temporal, spectral, and spatial characteristics of EEG signals [6, 15, 49]. Despite these ad-vances, ensuring reliable EEG-based MDD detection across diverse subjects and recording conditions remains a significant challenge. This challenge primarily arises from the inherent characteris-tics of EEG signals, which are non-stationary, noisy, and subject-dependent. Consequently, supervised models may capture subject-specific or recording-related patterns alongside disease-related information, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen subjects and indepen-dently collected datasets [17]. The limited number of subjects in psychiatric EEG datasets, coupled with the high cost of clinical data collection and annotation, further exacerbates these general-ization difficulties. These limitations highlight the need for learn-ing disease-discriminative representations that remain robust to subject-specific and acquisition-related variations. A natural way to mitigate this data limitation is to enlarge the training set through data augmentation. In particular, generative data augmentation has attracted increasing attention in EEG analy-sis, where generative models synthesize additional EEG samples for training [44, 53]. However, generating realistic EEG signals re-mains challenging, especially in low-data settings [52]. Synthetic EEG samples may exhibit artifacts and deviations from the statisti-cal properties of authentic recordings, raising concerns about their fidelity [5, 11]. Moreover, the benefits of synthetic augmentation for downstream MDD classification may vary across datasets [4]. These concerns suggest that increasing data quantity alone does not necessarily ensure more reliable EEG-based MDD detection. Clinical studies have reported differences in EEG spectral activ-ity and functional connectivity between individuals with MDD and healthy controls (HC) [7, 20]. Consistent with these findings, our preliminary analysis reveals differences in inter-electrode correla-tion patterns between the two groups, as shown in Figure 1. These observations indicate that healthy and depressive EEG recordings exhibit distinct patterns of neural activity. Rather than treating HC recordings solely as a comparison group, their physiological patterns may also provide a reference for characterizing individual variations. Healthy normative modeling offers a means of establish-ing such references from HC recordings and quantifying deviations from them. These deviations may provide complementary infor-mation for identifying MDD-related abnormalities. normative deviations do not necessarily translate into discrimina-tive evidence for MDD detection. Their diagnostic utility depends on how disease-relevant variations are represented and integrated with EEG features, particularly across heterogeneous data sources. To this end, we investigate normative deviations at different lev-els of granularity, each capturing distinct aspects of departures from healthy EEG patterns. We therefore develop two diagnostic formu-lations to examine their respective contributions to MDD detection. Both establish structural and distributional references exclusively from healthy EEG recordings. Specifically, we propose Scalar-level Normative Deviation-guided Diagnosis (S-NDD), which summarizes structural and distributional deviations into compact scores and integrates them with deep EEG representations. We further intro-duce Feature-level Normative Deviation-guided Diagnosis (F-NDD), which preserves dimension-wise residuals and incorporates them into deep EEG representations through attention-based aggrega-tion and auxiliary supervision. To accommodate cross-source shifts in diagnostic scores, both approaches support target HC-assisted score calibration without updating model parameters. We evaluate their diagnostic performance under within-source and bidirectional cross-source settings. In summary, our key contributions are as follows: 

• We establish structural and distributional healthy references to characterize normative deviations in EEG recordings. These references are learned exclusively from HC and provide com-plementary information for MDD diagnosis. 

• We introduce two normative deviation-guided diagnostic approaches, S-NDD and F-NDD. S-NDD integrates scalar deviation scores with deep EEG representations, while F-NDD incorporates dimension-wise residuals through shared attention aggregation and auxiliary diagnostic supervision. 

• We conduct comprehensive evaluations on two public EEG datasets under subject-independent within-source and bidi-rectional cross-source settings. We further investigate target HC-assisted score calibration to accommodate cross-source variations in diagnostic scores. 

## 2 Related Work 2.1 EEG-based MDD Detection 

EEG-based MDD detection has progressed from handcrafted fea-tures to deep representation learning. Early CNN-based methods learned discriminative EEG features [1], while graph-based ap-proaches, including MAST-GCN [22], adaptive temporal graph networks [23], and ST-GCA [37], model spatial and temporal de-pendencies. Hybrid architectures combine graph convolution and Transformers, as in SLiTRANet [34], or adopt lightweight convolu-tional Transformers [14]. Temporal-spatial-spectral learning has also been explored using emotion-elicited EEG [28]. Research has further examined diverse acquisition and clinical settings, includ-ing single-channel detection [41] and multi-dataset diagnosis and treatment-response prediction [27]. General EEG representation models, such as EEG Conformer [35] and CBraMod [42], have advanced convolutional attention and large-scale pretraining. Nev-ertheless, discriminative EEG representations may retain subject-specific and acquisition-related variations, limiting generalization across independently collected datasets. 

## 2.2 Normative Modeling and Healthy References 

Normative modeling characterizes individual observations relative to a reference population, providing a framework for investigating heterogeneity beyond average case–control differences [25, 32]. In psychiatric research, normative models characterize individual deviations from expected physiological or behavioral patterns [25]. Practical frameworks have also been developed to estimate and evaluate such deviations [32]. Generative approaches further learn healthy data distributions and identify departures from these distri-butions for anomaly detection. For example, diffusion-based models have been trained on healthy brain images to detect and segment abnormalities in CT and MRI data [29]. These studies illustrate the potential of reference-based analysis for characterizing individ-ual abnormalities. However, findings from neuroimaging do not directly establish the diagnostic utility of normative deviations in EEG-based MDD detection. Moreover, the representation and uti-lization of normative deviations may influence their effectiveness across different subjects and data sources. 

## 2.3 Cross-domain EEG Diagnosis 

Domain adaptation aims to reduce distribution discrepancies be-tween source and target domains. Representative methods include DANN [10], which learns domain-adversarial representations, DAN [21], which aligns distributions through maximum mean discrepancy, and Deep CORAL [36], which matches feature covariance statistics. Cross-domain learning has also been explored for EEG-based de-pression detection. TFST-SDKA [48] combines temporal-frequency-spatial representations with sub-domain knowledge alignment for cross-subject detection. While cross-subject evaluation examines unseen participants, cross-dataset evaluation additionally involves differences in acquisition protocols, electrode configurations, and participant populations. Evaluation protocols depend on the avail-ability of target-domain data for adaptation or calibration. However, reducing distribution discrepancies does not necessarily separate disease-related variations from source-specific characteristics.

## 4 Normative Deviation-guided Diagnosis 

Our framework establishes a normative reference from healthy EEG recordings to characterize individual deviations from healthy patterns. Rather than relying solely on supervised EEG represen-tations, we incorporate structural and distributional deviations as complementary diagnostic information. As illustrated in Figure 2, we develop two formulations that represent normative deviations at different levels of granularity. S-NDD summarizes deviations into scalar scores, while F-NDD preserves fine-grained deviation features. Both formulations integrate the resulting deviations with learned EEG representations for MDD detection. 

## 4.1 Dual Healthy Reference Modeling 

We construct normative references exclusively from healthy con-trol (HC) recordings to characterize departures from healthy EEG patterns. Since a single reference model may not capture all aspects of healthy variability, we establish complementary structural and distributional references. Specifically, VQ-VAE [40, 51] captures recurring patterns through discrete latent quantization, while a diffusion model [13, 29] learns healthy data distributions through noise-conditioned denoising. To accommodate heterogeneous elec-trode configurations, EEG recordings are first aligned to a common spatial layout. S-NDD trains its normative models on the aligned EEG signals, whereas F-NDD independently trains corresponding models on handcrafted features described in Section 4.3.

## 5 Experiments 5.1 Experimental Setup 

Implementation Details. EEG recordings are band-pass filtered at 0.5–50 Hz, resampled to 256 Hz, spatially aligned to 19 electrodes, and segmented into 5-s windows with a 2.5-s stride. The VQ-VAE codebooks contain 512 entries of dimension 64 for S-NDD and 64 entries of dimension 32 for F-NDD, with 𝛽 = 0.25. Both methods use 500-step diffusion models with a linear noise schedule from 10 −4 to 0.02. Normative models are trained exclusively on source-domain HC samples and frozen during diagnostic training. S-NDD fuses scalar normative deviations with deep EEG features using 𝛼 = 0.7. F-NDD extracts 456-dimensional EEG features and estimates diffusion residuals at T = {25 , 125 , 250 } with 𝐾 = 2 noise realizations per timestep. Both methods use a diagnostic learning rate of 5 × 10 −4,with Adam for S-NDD and F-NDD. The auxiliary loss weight is 

𝜆 dev = 0.5. Further details are provided in Appendix A. 

Datasets. We evaluate the proposed methods on Mumtaz2016 [26] and MODMA [3]. Both datasets contain MDD and HC recordings, supporting normative modeling and cross-dataset evaluation un-der heterogeneous acquisition settings. Mumtaz2016 comprises 34 MDD and 30 HC subjects with 19 electrodes, while MODMA includes 24 MDD and 29 HC subjects with 128 electrodes. After pre-processing, they yield 13,953 and 6,355 EEG segments, respectively.

Evaluation Metrics. Following established evaluation practices in EEG-based MDD detection [37, 44], we report Accuracy (Acc), Precision (Pre), Recall (Rec), F1-score (F1), and ROC-AUC (AUC) at both window and subject levels. Both S-NDD and F-NDD generate window-level predictions from individual EEG windows. For S-NDD, subject-level predictions are obtained by majority voting across windows, with ties assigned to MDD, and subject-level AUC is computed from the mean MDD probability. For F-NDD, subject-level predictions are generated directly from attention-aggregated window representations, as described in Section 4.3.

## 5.3 Cross-Dataset Generalization 

Mumtaz2016 to MODMA. We train S-NDD and F-NDD on Mum-taz2016 and evaluate them on MODMA, mapping its 128-electrode recordings to 19 electrodes through spatial adaptation. F-NDD ad-ditionally uses target-domain HC data for score calibration. As shown in Table 2, S-NDD achieves the highest accuracy of 78.50% and F1 of 69.96%. Compared with the best results among DANN, DAN, Deep CORAL, and TFST-SDKA, these represent gains of 21.14 and 10.52 percentage points, respectively. S-NDD also exceeds the strongest non-ours results in accuracy and F1 by 8.60 and 6.48 points, while achieving an AUC comparable to the best baseline (79.31% versus 79.44%). These results support the effectiveness of normative deviation modeling beyond the evaluated domain align-ment and augmentation approaches. Compared with calibrated F-NDD, S-NDD achieves higher recall (75.34% versus 56.94%) with lower precision, yielding better overall accuracy and F1 without target-domain calibration. 

MODMA to Mumtaz2016. To account for potential shifts in pre-diction scores, we evaluate reverse transfer using target HC-assisted score calibration for all methods. Seven target HC record groups are reserved for calibration and excluded from diagnostic testing, with model parameters kept fixed. Table 3 shows that F-NDD achieves the highest accuracy, precision, F1, and AUC of 89.15%, 93.08%, 91.56%, and 94.21%, respectively. It outperforms KNN, the strongest baseline in accuracy, by 5.43 percentage points in accuracy and 4.83 points in F1. Its accuracy also exceeds Deep CORAL, the strongest domain adaptation baseline, by 13.18 points. S-NDD achieves the highest recall of 97.62%, whereas F-NDD combines 90.48% recall with substantially higher precision (93.08% versus 80.44%). Thus, un-der the same calibration setting, F-NDD provides a more favorable balance between MDD detection and false-positive control.

## 5.4 Ablation Study 

Scalar-level Deviation (S-NDD). As shown in Table 4, removing both structural and distributional deviations reduces accuracy from 95.28% to 85.56%, indicating the contribution of normative informa-tion beyond deep EEG representations. Removing the distributional or structural component yields 90.40% and 93.11% accuracy, respec-tively, with a larger reduction observed when distributional devia-tions are excluded. Replacing deviation-guided fusion with decision-level fusion decreases accuracy to 93.12%, supporting the adopted feature-fusion strategy. Alternative normative priors based on Ma-halanobis distance, OCSVM, and Simple VAE achieve accuracies of 92.62%, 92.25%, and 92.56%, respectively. Although OCSVM attains slightly higher recall (97.43% versus 97.18%), S-NDD achieves the highest accuracy, F1, and AUC among these configurations. These results support the combined use of structural and distributional references with deviation-guided feature fusion. 

Feature-level Deviation (F-NDD). As shown in Table 4, remov-ing both deviation components reduces accuracy from 95.20% to 88.75%. The larger degradation relative to removing both deviations also suggests that retaining only one residual component does not nec-essarily improve the learned representation. The com-plete F-NDD achieves 95.20% accuracy and 94.69% F1, supporting the combined use of spectral and inter-electrode correlation infor-mation for feature-level deviation modeling. ## 6 Conclusion and Future Work 

In this paper, we investigate healthy normative deviations for EEG-based MDD detection through two approaches, S-NDD and F-NDD. Both learn structural and distributional references from healthy con-trols and incorporate deviations at scalar and feature levels, respec-tively. Experiments on two public EEG datasets demonstrate their effectiveness under within-source and bidirectional cross-source settings. The two approaches exhibit distinct strengths across diag-nostic levels and transfer directions. Optional target HC-assisted score calibration further improves performance in selected cross-source settings. These findings highlight the value of healthy norma-tive references in complementing supervised EEG representations. As spatial interpolation mainly aligns electrode geometry, future work will investigate unsupervised domain adaptation to mitigate remaining acquisition-related differences across datasets. ## A.3 F-NDD Implementation Details 

Algorithm 1 summarizes the inference procedures of S-NDD and F-NDD. Window Processing and Spectral Estimation. Each EEG win-dow is sampled at 256 Hz. ## B Cross-Dataset Evaluation Protocols 

Source Evaluation and Partitioning. Data splits and prediction aggregation follow the main manuscript. Normative references are trained only on HC examples from the training partition, whereas diagnostic training uses labeled HC and MDD examples. Training-derived normalization statistics, including Eq. (22), remain fixed during validation and testing. Multiple windows from one individ-ual are not treated as independent subjects. 

Mumtaz2016 to MODMA. S-NDD is trained on Mumtaz2016 and evaluated on MODMA without updating model weights or normalization statistics using target data. MODMA recordings are mapped to the 19-electrode montage as described in Appendix A.1. The source-trained references and diagnostic classifier remain fixed, yielding the source-only, zero-shot setting reported in the main manuscript. 

MODMA to Mumtaz2016. F-NDD is trained on MODMA and eval-uated on Mumtaz2016 at the record-group level. Each calibrated run uses seven target HC record groups, excluded from diagnostic testing, to estimate prediction-score calibration parameters. Both the normative references and diagnostic network remain fixed. Un-calibrated and calibrated predictions are distinguished; the latter constitute HC-assisted transfer rather than strict source-only eval-uation. Test record groups overlap across calibration runs, so these runs do not represent independent subject cohorts. 

Domain Adaptation Comparisons. The adaptation comparisons include DANN, DAN, Deep CORAL, and TFST-SDKA in both trans-fer directions. Target-data adaptation is distinguished from source-only prediction and post-hoc HC score calibration. In particular, adaptation restricted to target HC data is not equivalent to UDA with unrestricted unlabeled target data. Differences in target-data access and prediction aggregation must be considered when in-terpreting comparisons. ## C Generative Baseline Implementation 

This section details four generative augmentation strategies devel-oped in this study based on DiffMDD [44]: Diffusion Augmenta-tion (Diff-Aug), hard-negative-guided synthesis (Hard Neg), artifact cleaning (Clean), and spatial mixing (Hybrid). Unless otherwise specified, all methods are implemented in PyTorch and optimized using Adam with a batch size of 32 on a NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPU. 

(i) Diff-Aug. Diff-Aug evaluates synthetic data augmentation as an alternative to normative deviation modeling, retaining the same downstream 1D-CNN–Transformer classifier. Following DiffMDD, the pipeline consists of three stages. First, a 1D-CNN–Transformer classifier is pre-trained to extract class-aware condition embed-dings. Next, a conditional diffusion model employs a 1D-CNN– Transformer noise predictor, incorporating these embeddings through Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM). Finally, synthetic EEG seg-ments are generated from randomly selected training anchors using classifier-guided DDIM sampling and combined with the original training data to train the classifier from scratch. Implementation settings are summarized in Table 5.

## D Computational Efficiency Analysis 

Table 8 summarizes the computational cost and inference latency of different methods. All latency measurements are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPU. Unlike generative augmentation methods, S-NDD and F-NDD estimate normative deviations without itera-tive sample synthesis. S-NDD requires 1.70M parameters and 1.42 GFLOPs, while the native single-model F-NDD contains 0.457M parameters and requires 0.00405 GFLOPs of profiled neural oper-ations. The inference latency is approximately 2.9 ms for S-NDD and 16.5 ms for F-NDD. F-NDD latency includes feature extraction and data transfers, while its FLOPs exclude CPU feature process-ing. In practice, the fixed cost of S-NDD stems from one VQ-VAE reconstruction pass, one denoising score evaluation, and the di-agnostic classifier. In contrast, DiffMDD relies on 100-step DDIM synthesis, which dominates its 101.69 GFLOPs and approximately 94.5 ms latency; S-NDD thus reduces the FLOPs by about 72 × and the latency by more than 30 × with comparable parameters (1.70M versus 1.32M).
