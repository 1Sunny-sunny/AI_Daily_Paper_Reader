# ORBITAL: Optimization of Robust Brain–Computer Inference Through Architecture-Aware Low-Precision Compression 

Saim Rehman and Muhammad Shafique 

eBRAIN Lab, New York University Abu Dhabi, Abu Dhabi, UAE sr7849@nyu.edu, muhammad.shafique@nyu.edu 

Abstract —Compact electroencephalography (EEG) decoders en-able embedded brain–computer interfaces (BCIs), but compression can alter adversarial robustness without proportional hardware savings. We present ORBITAL, a robustness-constrained frame-work combining hardware-realizable structured compression, recovery-aware operating-point selection, multi-attack evaluation, and native edge validation. Across nine subjects and three seeds on BCI Competition IV-2a, S25-TRADES achieves the highest observed mean true-union robustness among compressed EEGNet models (23.06% vs 21.27% for standard Full), with 26.27% fewer parameters and 25.10% fewer MACs. Matched controls identify TRADES as the main source of robustness recovery, improving S25 union accuracy by 6.74 percentage points over clean recovery (p=0.0039), while per-example analysis reveals boundary redistribution rather than uniform margin expansion. On Jetson Orin Nano, FP16 compression reduces board energy by up to 16.20% and latency by 8.00%. Despite 99.3% prediction agreement, native INT8 Q/DQ incurs about twice the FP16 latency and energy, demonstrating the need for joint robustness and hardware-aware optimization. 

Index Terms —EEG, Brain–Computer Interface, Adversarial Robustness, Structured Pruning, Quantization-Aware Training, Edge AI, TensorRT. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

EEG-based BCIs increasingly rely on compact convolutional neural networks such as EEGNet [1] and ShallowConvNet [2]. Their embedded use creates three coupled objectives: (i) preserve task accuracy, (ii) resist small adversarial pertur-bations, and (iii) reduce real latency/energy. Conventional compression work largely optimizes storage or arithmetic cost [3]–[6], whereas adversarial training and evaluation are usually studied independently [7]–[9]. In EEG, this separation is particularly problematic because compact decoders operate on low-amplitude, non-image signals for which perturbation geometry and hardware overheads differ from standard vision workloads. Fig. 1 illustrates how structured compression changes clean accuracy, adversarial robustness, and model complexity non-monotonically. Mathematical compression does not automat-ically guarantee actual hardware efficiency; thus, relying on a fixed compression ratio is insufficient for dependable edge deployment. Recent BCI studies established that EEG decoders are vulnerable to adversarial manipulation [10] and bench-marked multiple defenses [11]. ORBITAL integrates physical structured compression, post-pruning recovery, robust evaluation, and deployment profiling into a unified pipeline. Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete procedure. The following subsections detail each stage. 

A. Physical Structured Compression 

ORBITAL performs structured channel pruning to obtain smaller, dense EEG decoders rather than sparsely masked networks. For EEGNet, coupled temporal and pointwise filter dimensions (F1, F 2) are reduced while preserving the depthwise structure and downstream compatibility. Retained parameters are transferred into the rebuilt architecture. As for ShallowConvNet, filter importance accounts for filter magnitude and downstream connectivity, with corresponding convolutional, normalization, and classifier dimensions rebuilt consistently. Unlike unstructured sparsity, the resulting models directly reduce parameter storage and arithmetic requirements [4], [5].

Dataset and models: Experiments use BCI Competition IV-2a [14], containing nine subjects, 22 EEG channels, and four motor-imagery classes. Signals are sampled at 250 Hz and band-pass filtered to 4–38 Hz. Each subject provides 230/58/288 training/validation/test trials with 4.5-s input windows. EEGNet is the primary compression and deployment architecture; ShallowConvNet evaluates cross-architecture generalization. 

Compression and recovery: We evaluate EEGNet width-reduction targets {0, 10 , 20 , 25 , 30 , 40 , 50 , 60 }% with valid coupled (F1, F 2) dimensions. Requested width reductions are distinguished from realized parameter and MAC reductions. Raw pruning, clean recovery, and TRADES recovery are evaluated. TRADES uses β = 2 , ϵ = 0 .005 , ten inner steps, step size 0.00125 , and an anchor coefficient of 1. Recovery uses a learning rate of 5×10 −5, up to 30 epochs, and validation-based early stopping. 

Selection and robustness evaluation: The screening stage uses PGD20 with one random restart and predefined clean and robustness tolerances of δc = 0.03 and δr = 0.02 .Selected candidates are confirmed using PGD100 ×10, APGD-CE, APGD-DLR, and FAB under a common ℓ∞ threat radius 

ϵ = 0 .005 . Robustness is reported using the clean-correct true union. 

Reproducibility and statistics: EEGNet compression-search and ShallowConvNet experiments cover nine subjects and three seeds {1, 48 , 550 }. Seed results are averaged within each subject before paired subject-level Wilcoxon tests and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Selected aggressive EEGNet QAT experiments remain single-seed. 

Hardware evaluation: Native deployment uses TensorRT on NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super [15], [16] under fixed 

MAXN SUPER clocks. FP16 configurations are measured in five independent 60-s runs. We report inference latency, throughput, active whole-board power ( VDD IN ), and board energy per inference. Learned-scale Q/DQ exports are sepa-rately evaluated to distinguish numerical fidelity from native INT8 deployment cost. IV. R ESULTS & A NALYSIS

D. Cross-Architecture Generalization 

Across nine subjects and three seeds, ShallowConvNet S25-TRADES achieves 57.56% clean and 41.23% union accuracy, compared with 57.90% and 41.86% for Full; neither paired difference is significant. At S50, raw pruning yields 34.84% union accuracy; clean recovery restores it to 40.74%, compared with 39.76% for S50-TRADES. Hence, the preferred recovery method depends on architecture and compression severity rather than following a universal TRADES advantage. 

E. Hardware Efficiency and Precision 

Table III shows that S25 reduces Jetson board energy by 5.35% with little latency change, whereas S50 reduces latency and energy by 8.00% and 16.20%, respectively. In contrast, Fig. 5 shows that learned-scale INT8 Q/DQ preserves 99.3% prediction agreement but incurs roughly twice the FP16 latency and energy. Conversion and reformat overheads therefore limit the benefit of lower precision. V. C ONCLUSION
