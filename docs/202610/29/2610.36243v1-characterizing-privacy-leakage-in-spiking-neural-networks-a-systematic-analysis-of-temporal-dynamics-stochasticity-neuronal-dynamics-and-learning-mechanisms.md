## 摘要
脉冲神经网络不仅在神经元是否发放中编码信息，还在神经元何时发放中编码信息，而这一时间维度塑造了训练后的网络对其训练数据的泄露程度。受生物启发的放电模式可以在不改变网络架构的情况下降低脉冲神经网络中的成员资格泄露，这使时间动态成为隐私设计变量。然而，放电行为的改变会同时改变随机性、脉冲时间、神经元响应和学习问题，因此要利用这一效应，需要确定是其中哪些性质产生了该效应。本研究通过在统一实验框架中分离时间表示、随机性、内在神经元动力学和学习来追溯这一来源，并在图像和表格基准上以黑盒成员推理攻击进行评估。改变时间表示表明，与规则放电相比，爆发式放电模式可降低泄露，但会以精度为代价。向输入、脉冲时间和脉冲序列中注入随机性并不能复现这种泄露降低，这表明泄露降低来自脉冲在时间上的排列方式，而非噪声。隐私收益进一步取决于这些放电模式的来源。当神经元通过自身的自适应动力学产生这些模式时，泄露下降且精度得以保持，因此隐私不再以性能为代价。学习算法随后设定了基线泄露水平。在进化训练下，攻击已接近随机猜测水平，使时间动态几乎没有进一步改善隐私的空间。因此，脉冲神经网络中的隐私并非脉冲的固有属性，而是时间活动如何被生成、处理和学习的结果，这为设计保护敏感数据的神经形态系统提供了原则性基础。

## Abstract
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) encode information not only in whether neurons fire but also in when they fire, and this temporal dimension shapes what a trained network reveals about its training data. Biologically inspired firing patterns can reduce membership leakage in SNNs without changing the network architecture, which positions temporal dynamics as a design variable for privacy. A change in firing behavior, however, alters stochasticity, spike timing, neuronal response, and the learning problem at once, so harnessing this effect requires identifying which of these properties produces it. This study traces that origin by separating temporal representation, stochasticity, intrinsic neuron dynamics, and learning within a common experimental framework, evaluated under a black-box membership inference attack across image and tabular benchmarks. Varying the temporal representation shows that burst-like firing patterns reduce leakage relative to regular spiking, but at a cost in accuracy. Injecting randomness into the input, spike timing, and spike trains does not reproduce this reduction in leakage, which shows that the reduction arises from how spikes are arranged in time rather than from noise. The privacy benefit further depends on where these firing patterns originate. When neurons generate these patterns through their own adaptive dynamics, leakage falls while accuracy is preserved, so privacy no longer comes at the expense of performance. The learning algorithm then sets the baseline leakage. Under evolutionary training, the attack already performs near chance level, leaving temporal dynamics little room to improve privacy further. Privacy in SNNs is therefore not an inherent property of spikes but a product of how temporal activity is generated, processed, and learned, which offers a principled basis for designing neuromorphic systems that protect sensitive data.
