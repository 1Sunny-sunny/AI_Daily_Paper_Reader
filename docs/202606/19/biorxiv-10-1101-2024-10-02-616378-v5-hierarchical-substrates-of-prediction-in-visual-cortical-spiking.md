## 摘要
分层预测编码模型近年来在神经科学中蓬勃发展。前馈和反馈处理是分层预测编码模型的核心。先前的功能磁共振成像、脑电图/脑磁图以及局部场电位实验研究未能可靠地区分反馈调制与局部计算及前馈输出。在此，我们利用开放科学、多物种、多脑区、高密度、层状神经生理学，实验性地检验分层预测编码是否是塑造视觉刺激皮层处理的关键组成部分。为了分离视觉信息处理并消除运动/奖励混淆因素，我们采用无报告任务。我们的任务利用所谓的全局奇异刺激作为不可预测的偏差刺激，以规避低级适应。我们考察了它们相对于局部奇异刺激的反应，我们将局部奇异刺激习惯化为高度可预测的先验。该数据集中的四个令人惊讶的发现挑战了许多现有的分层预测编码模型。首先，全局奇异刺激的反应仅限于更高阶、更具认知性的脑区，而非早期至中期视觉皮层。其次，在灵长类和啮齿类动物中进行的抑制性中间神经元靶向光遗传学操作以及灵长类中的波形形状分析都表明，没有证据表明预测性抑制是通过这些中间神经元实现的。第三，高度可预测的局部奇异刺激反应在超过50%的神经元中占主导地位，包括在理应预期它们的高阶皮层中，这表明预测性抑制的证据有限。最后，由全局奇异刺激引发的预测错误反应并未引发前馈处理。这些结果揭示了支配预测如何塑造视觉处理的回路动力学，推动了更具神经约束的预测处理模型的发展。

## Abstract
Hierarchical predictive coding (HPC) models have recently flourished in neuroscience. Feedforward and feedback processing are at the heart of HPC models. Previous experimental studies using fMRI, EEG/MEG, and LFP do not reliably resolve feedback modulation from local computations and feedforward outputs. Here, using open science, multispecies, multi-area, high-density, laminar neurophysiology, we empirically test whether hierarchical predictive coding is a key component shaping cortical processing of visual stimuli. To isolate visual information processing and eliminate motor/reward confounders, we use a no-report task. Our task leveraged so-called global oddballs (GO) as unpredictable, deviant stimuli that circumvent low-level adaptation. We examined their responses relative to local oddballs (LO) that we habituated into highly predictable priors. Four surprising findings in this dataset challenge many existing hierarchical predictive coding models. First, GO responses were exclusive to higher-order, more cognitive areas rather than early-tomid visual cortex. Second, inhibitory interneuron-targeted optogenetics in primates and mice and waveform shape analysis in primates revealed no evidence that predictive suppression was implemented via these interneurons. Third, highly predictable LO responses dominated in over 50% of all neurons, including in higher order cortex, which should have anticipated them, indicating limited evidence for predictive suppression. Lastly, prediction error responses evoked by GOs did not evoke feedforward processing. These results reveal circuit dynamics that govern how prediction shapes visual processing, motivating more neurally constrained predictive processing models.
