# ITP-STDP: An Intrinsic-Timing Power-of-Two Learning Engine for On-Chip SNN Training 

Haihang Xia, Xinyu Zhao, Xuecheng Wang, John Goodenough, Charith Abhayaratne, Member, IEEE, Panagiotis A. Panagiotou Member, IEEE, Chunyi Song, Member, IEEE, and Tiantai Deng 

Abstract —Spiking neural networks (SNNs) have the potential to emerge as the third generation of neural networks and have attracted increasing attention across a wide range of applications. However, the large number of synaptic connections in SNNs leads to intensive weight-update computation by on-chip learning algorithms during training, resulting in substantial hardware re-source utilization and energy consumption. Among existing SNN learning algorithms, spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) is one of the most extensively studied and widely adopted, serving as a fundamental learning component in SNNs. To address the hardware and energy overheads associated with SNN training, this paper presents intrinsic-timing power-of-two STDP (ITP-STDP) and its corresponding prototype learning engine hard-ware architecture. The proposed design is evaluated through a dedicated mean-field synaptic drift model for dynamical analysis and further validated across SNN networks of different scales and datasets. It is further implemented on both ASIC and FPGA platforms and compared with state-of-the-art approaches, includ-ing the original STDP and more complex STDP variants. The results demonstrate superior energy efficiency, higher operating speed, and substantially lower hardware resource utilization, as the proposed design eliminates most of the computational overhead of STDP through both algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations. On the FPGA platform, the proposed design improves energy efficiency by 4.5 × to 219.8 × over the compared designs. On the ASIC platform, the proposed design achieves a 4.8 × to 22.01 × speedup while consuming only 1.2% to 3.3% of the area required by prior works. 

Index Terms —Spike-timing-dependent plasticity, neuromor-phic computing, spiking neural networks, leaky integrate-and-fire neuron model, FPGA, ASIC, approximate computing. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# NEURAL networks have developed rapidly in recent years, while their growing energy consumption has be-come a key limitation [1]. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) provide a low-power alternative to conventional neural net-works due to their event-driven characteristics [2] and are considered the third generation of neural networks [3]. As a result, SNNs have been widely studied in applications such as communications, robotics, and aerospace [4]–[6]. SNNs are characterized by biologically inspired network structures, neuron models, and learning mechanisms [7].

Their event-driven spike communication and temporal neural dynamics make them a major computational paradigm in neuromorphic computing [8]. In neuromorphic hardware, the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) model is widely adopted in processors [9], such as Loihi [10] and NEXUS [11], due to its computational simplicity and biological plausibility [12]. In terms of learning mechanisms, spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) is one of the most widely studied and adopted learning rules, serving as a fundamental component of SNN learning [13]. It builds upon Hebbian learning [14], which describes synaptic plasticity based on the correla-tion between neuronal activities. Such plasticity is typically expressed through long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD), corresponding to the strengthening and weakening of synaptic efficacy, respectively [15]. As a spike-based and temporally asymmetric extension of Hebbian learning, STDP governs synaptic modifications according to the precise relative timing of presynaptic and postsynaptic spikes, thereby reinforcing synaptic connections associated with causal spike interactions while weakening noncausal or anti-causal ones [16]. STDP computation is highly sensitive to the precise relative timing of presynaptic and postsynaptic spikes. Moreover, the characteristics of STDP, including the temporal window and update amplitude, vary across synapse types and application scenarios. For instance, the LTP window in neocortical layer-5 neurons can reach approximately 100 ms [17], while the LTP and LTD windows at mossy fiber–granule cell synapses are limited to about ±25 ms [18]. Such variability increases the complexity of implementing flexible and hardware-efficient STDP learning circuits. STDP is also one of the most computationally intensive components in neuromorphic systems. A typical SNN may consist of tens of thousands of neurons and hundreds of thousands of synapses [19], [20], requiring massive local STDP computations for synaptic weight updates. Moreover, event-driven spike communication and weight updates in-troduce irregular and bursty memory access patterns, which can cause contention and data-transfer bottlenecks at high spike rates. These characteristics make STDP-based learning inefficient on conventional von Neumann architectures and impose additional challenges on dedicated hardware design. Therefore, optimizing STDP computation, synaptic weight transfer, and specific hardware architectures, including FPGA-and ASIC-based implementations, has attracted increasing research attention. In this context, [21] proposed the first digital implemen-tation of triplet-based STDP (TSTDP) without dedicated

By using low-resolution unsigned shift multipliers instead of floating-point multipliers, their FPGA implementation significantly reduced resource con-sumption while maintaining biologically plausible synaptic plasticity. In addition, approximation techniques such as piece-wise linear (PWL) functions have also been adopted in hard-ware designs to simplify STDP computation [38]. However, these approaches mainly optimize the arithmetic units rather than the underlying learning algorithm, leaving the over-all computation complex and the FPGA resource overhead still considerable. Motivated by these limitations, [23] pro-posed implicit-timing on-chip STDP learning (ImSTDP) to simplify the timing computation in STDP. ImSTDP derives the spike-timing difference from the indices of presynaptic and postsynaptic spikes, thereby avoiding counter-based time-difference calculation. It also precomputes and stores the weight-update values in a look-up table, which directly maps each index difference to the corresponding synaptic weight change. These techniques reduce computational complexity and improve local throughput of STDP. However, despite eliminating counter-based timing computation, ImSTDP still requires index-difference calculation which leads to relatively high hardware resource utilization. Moreover, its reliance on precomputed weight-update values reduces flexibility of STDP, and the quantization error introduced by index-based timing representation remains uncompensated. Similarly, [24] approximated the STDP curve with a linear function to avoid base-e exponential computation, thereby significantly reduc-ing hardware resource utilization and energy consumption. Nonetheless, the lack of error compensation causes the approx-imated rule to deviate substantially from the original STDP behavior, degrading its biological plasticity. To address the above limitations, this paper proposes an Intrinsic-Timing Power-of-Two STDP (ITP-STDP) on-chip learning engine that achieves low resource utilization, high energy efficiency, and high operating frequency. The proposed design replaces the base-e exponential function in conventional STDP with a base-2 exponential formulation, eliminating explicit exponential computation. The resulting ln 2 scaling error is absorbed into the time constant τ , enabling error compensation through time-constant adjustment and preserv-ing biological plasticity. In addition, the base-2 formulation allows the timing difference to be directly obtained from the spike-history shift register, avoiding both counter-based and explicit time-difference calculations while substantially reduc-ing computational complexity. In ITP-STDP, the spike history and the corresponding weight-update values are unified within the same representation, eliminating the lookup table (LUT)-based storage and access of precomputed weight updates required in ImSTDP. Consequently, the core STDP operation is reduced to spike-history storage and readout, with simple shift registers becoming the dominant hardware component. This design substantially reduces the hardware resources required for computation and data storage, thereby decreasing circuit area and power consumption. Furthermore, a prototype ITP-STDP learning engine with four presynaptic neurons fully connected to four postsynaptic neurons is designed and im-plemented, where neuronal connection mapping and weight updates are performed through simple read operations from register-based weight and spike-history arrays. This simplified data path enables a higher operating frequency and facilitates integration into larger SNN hardware systems. The proposed method is systematically evaluated by constructing a synaptic drift model and conducting validation from the synapse level to the network level. The proposed design is further implemented and evaluated on both FPGA and ASIC platforms. The main contributions of this paper include: 1) A new STDP implementation method, referred to as intrinsic-timing power-of-two STDP (ITP-STDP), is proposed. It substantially reduces the computational complexity of STDP through approximate computation, while compensating for the resulting approximation er-ror through systematic analysis. 2) Based on the proposed ITP-STDP algorithm, a specific hardware architecture is designed and evaluated on both FPGA and ASIC platforms, with comparisons against state-of-the-art STDP hardware designs, including ap-proximate implementations of conventional STDP and implementations of more complex STDP variants. B. Structure of ITP-STDP Learning Engine 

The ITP-STDP learning engine is designed to operate almost entirely through direct read and write access to spike sequences. Such operations enable both the computation of the weight update ∆w and the direct establishment of inter-neuron connections through spike-sequence reading. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the proposed ITP-STDP architecture uses the first bit of different spike histories as a trigger to determine the sign of the weight update and whether the associated neurons should be connected. Moreover, as shown in Fig. 4, the pro-posed architecture employing the ITP-STDP learning engine is applicable to neural topologies with arbitrary connectivity. The Spike History module stores the spike sequences of all neurons, which also encode the corresponding LTD and LTP information. A crossbar is employed to select the LTD and JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 5

LTP signals of arbitrary neurons, enabling the establishment of connections between any neuron pair. Subsequently, ∆w

is then directly applied to update the synaptic weights, which are stored in the weight memory and provided back to the neurons. Therefore, the ITP-STDP learning engine requires only a weight-update adder as its arithmetic unit, while all other components are devoted to weight and spike storage. Since inter-neuron connectivity is also realized through stor-age access, the proposed learning engine exhibits very low hardware resource utilization and power consumption.

Fig. 10 (a) shows the weight-reading struc-ture for all-to-all spike pairing, where the spike history is directly read and represented in two’s-complement form as the weight update. After the spike history is read, the one-hot representation of its most significant bit (MSB) is extracted. As illustrated in Fig. 11, the first ‘1‘ is detected from left to right, and only this bit is retained as ‘1‘ while all other bits are set to ‘0‘, thereby forming the MSB mask. The resulting mask is then converted into two’s-complement form to obtain the intermediate weight-update value. In the weight-update control logic, all control signals are generated from the spike activities of the two neurons. No weight update is triggered when both neurons either generate spikes or remain inactive. Once XOR and AND logic identify that only one neuron has fired, the resulting control signal drives a 3-input multiplexer to select between LTD and LTP. Subsequently, shift operations are performed according to the learning rate and the τ -error compensation to obtain the final weight variation. This final weight variation is then accumulated with the previous weight to generate the updated weight, thereby completing the entire weight-update process. 

B. Hardware Design of ITP-STDP Learning Engine 

Fig .9 illustrates the proposed ITP-STDP learning engine structure. In this design, spikes are generated by th LIF neuron model and sequentially written into the corresponding neuron spike arrays to record spike histories. The LIF neuron model is constructed according to (4), where the time constant τ is directly provided as a constant parameter and multiplied by the difference between v and vrest . The multiplication unit adopts the approximate LLSMu multiplier to further reduce hardware resource usage and power consumption, and the corresponding LLSMu structure is also illustrated in Fig. 9. The core opera-tion of LLSMu is carried out by the LLM Mutiplier, while the compensation is computed in parallel with the multiplication process, thereby improving throughput, reducing computation JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 9

latency, and decreasing computation error. The preprocessing module first detects the leading ’1’ and then shifts the entire operand so that the detected bit is aligned with the MSB. In addition, the LLSMu-based LIF neuron employs an eight-stage pipeline, which enables a single neuron hardware unit to serve as eight neuron models through time multiplexing. Their spikes are subsequently read out sequentially, one in each clock cycle. After the spikes of all neurons are generated, they are written into the corresponding spike-history shift registers of ITP-STDP, and the STDP weight-update computation is then carried out. Once the synaptic weights of all connec-tions are obtained, each post-synaptic neuron in the fully connected structure accumulates the weights from all pre-synaptic neurons as its input. The inclusion of each weight in the accumulation is directly controlled by the spike activity of the corresponding pre-synaptic neuron. The accumulated result is then taken as the input current I and applied to the LIF neuron again. VI. H ARDWARE IMPLEMENTATION RESULTS 

The ITP-STDP learning engine was implemented in Ver-ilog HDL on a Xilinx Zynq UltraScale+ FPGA board, and all hardware designs were synthesized and evaluated using Vivado 2023.2. The corresponding performance metrics were obtained from the Vivado implementation results. In addition, these designs were implemented and evaluated at the typical process corner (1.00 V, 25 ◦C) in TSMC 28-nm technology. Synopsys Design Compiler, VCS, and PrimeTime PX were used to perform logic synthesis, dynamic timing simulation, and power analysis for all hardware implementations. For a fair comparison, the FPGA and ASIC results are reported separately. Furthermore, as SNNs are generally built by repeat-edly replicating neuron models and learning algorithms, the evaluation and comparison of a single STDP algorithm with a simple learning-engine structure provide valuable insight into the scalability and overall performance of the network. In this context, optimizing the STDP learning algorithm and its hard-ware learning engine can effectively enhance the deployment performance of the complete network. 

A. FPGA Implementation Result of the proposed ITP-STDP 1) Hardware Overhead Evaluation of the proposed ITP-STDP Learning Algorithm: As shown in Table III, with the same 8-bit weight width, ITP-STDP requires only 5.3% of the slice registers and 3.4% of the slice LUTs of LLMu t-STDP. Compared with [21], [29], and [36]– [39], ITP-STDP achieves the lowest slice register and slice LUT usage, corresponding to savings of 50.0% to 99.1% in slice registers and 37.5% to 99.7% in slice LUTs. This highlights the outstanding hardware resource efficiency of ITP-STDP. The substantial reduction in hardware utilization arises from the fact that ITP-STDP eliminates most of the weight-update computation itself, so that the majority of hardware overhead is only associated with spike and weight storage, together with shift operations. In addition, the higher hardware resource utilization in [36], [37], and [39] may be attributed to the implementation of more complex STDP variants. In particular, [36] and [37] adopt calcium-based STDP, which incorporates more spike patterns than conventional STDP, whereas [39] augments conventional STDP with reward modulation. However, errors still remain when these methods implement the same pattern as pair-based STDP, and similar inaccuracies are also observed in other works implementing pair-based STDP, which can affect their biological plasticity. By contrast, ITP-STDP can achieve full consistency with the original STDP when implementing pair-based STDP, as illustrated in Fig. 5(a).

Compared with [21], [29], and [36]– [39], the throughput is increased by 1.1 × to 3.3 ×. Moreover, owing to its two-stage pipeline and high maximum operating frequency, ITP-STDP achieves the lowest latency among all compared designs, at only 0.932 ns, representing reductions of 90.0%, 94.2% and 96.2% compared with [29], [38] and [36], respectively. This demonstrates that the proposed ITP-STDP achieves high operating speed while maintaining very low hardware resource consumption, thereby accelerating weight-update computation during SNN training. 

3) Energy Efficiency Evaluation of the proposed ITP-STDP Learning Algorithm: The proposed ITP-STDP achieves signif-icantly lower power consumption than prior works, requiring only 1.5%–3.5% of the power consumed by those designs. At 100 MHz, its power consumption is identical to that of t-STDP, with both remaining below 3 mW. Nevertheless, when each design operates at its own maximum frequency, ITP-STDP reduces energy consumption by 77.8% compared with t-STDP and by at least 96.4% compared with the other works. As a result, ITP-STDP achieves the highest energy efficiency, exceeding the others by 4.5 × to 219.8 ×.Overall, ITP-STDP combines low hardware resource uti-lization, high operating speed, and low energy consumption. Moreover, after systematic error compensation, it introduces no practical computational error and maintains the same up-date curve as the original STDP. Consequently, it is well suited for large-scale SNN implementation, where it can significantly reduce the required hardware resources, training time, and energy consumption.

C. Hardware Implementation Results of ITP-STDP Learning Engine 

Furthermore, a hardware architecture based on the ITP-STDP learning engine, comprising eight LIF neurons arranged in a two-layer fully connected structure, was implemented and tested on both FPGA and ASIC platforms. By combining an LLSMu-based LIF neuron model with ITP-STDP and directly accessing weights from registers, the proposed design avoids complex inter-neuron connec-tion circuitry, thereby significantly reducing the hardware resource utilization of the ITP-STDP learning engine. On the FPGA platform, it occupies only 290 slice LUTs and 251 slice registers, without requiring any additional hardware resources such as DSPs or memory blocks. On the ASIC platform implemented in 28-nm technology, its chip area is only 1888 .74 μm2. Moreover, because the computationally intensive operations in both the neuron model and the STDP algorithm are optimized and implemented with a pipelined structure, the design achieves a very high operating frequency, reaching 398.72 MHz on FPGA and 2.44 GHz on ASIC. However, ITP-STDP employs a 10-stage pipeline structure, including 8 stages for the LIF neuron model and 2 stages for ITP-STDP, which results in latencies of 20.06 ns and 3.28 ns on FPGA and ASIC, respectively. Nevertheless, owing to its very high operating frequency, the overall latency remains at a relatively low level. Notably, the ITP-STDP learning engine also demonstrates excellent power efficiency. At its maximum operating fre-quency, the power consumption is only 11 mW on FPGA and 1.79 mW on ASIC. Owing to its high operating frequency, each spike operation requires only 27.58 pJ and 0.73 pJ, respectively, resulting in energy efficiencies as high as 36.26 GSOP/W and 1369.90 GSOP/W. This implies that large-scale SNNs built with the ITP-STDP learning engine can be deployed with very low hardware resource usage while maintaining high-speed computation and extremely low energy consumption.
