## 摘要
改变主意——独立于外部线索修订过去决策——可以带来更好的结果，但其神经基础仍知之甚少。因此，我们为大鼠设计了一项具有挑战性的空间任务，该任务中先天觅食偏好与任务规则之间的冲突导致了大量自发且具有典型修正性的主意改变（COMs）。在对海马体（一个与反事实思考有关的脑区）的神经记录中，揭示出两个不同阶段：在动物开始折返后，局部表征逐渐让位于远处替代选项的表征。这些表征预测了它们最终的选择，并且经常序列性地编码试验的起始和结束位置。我们的新任务范式揭示了自我纠正过程中所涉及的不同表征阶段，并展现了海马体与行为相关的丰富空间表征库。

## Abstract
Changing ones mind -- revising a past decision independently of external cues -- can lead to better outcomes, but its neural basis remains poorly understood. We therefore developed a challenging spatial task for rats where a conflict between innate foraging biases and task rules leads to abundant, spontaneous, and characteristically corrective changes-of-mind (COMs). Neural recordings in the hippocampus, a brain region implicated in counterfactual thinking, revealed two distinct stages wherein more local representations gave way to representations of distant alternatives after animals had begun to reverse course. These representations predicted their eventual choice, and often serially encoded trial start and end locations. Our novel task paradigm reveals distinct representational phases engaged during self-correction and uncovers a rich repertoire of hippocampal spatial representations tied to behavior.
