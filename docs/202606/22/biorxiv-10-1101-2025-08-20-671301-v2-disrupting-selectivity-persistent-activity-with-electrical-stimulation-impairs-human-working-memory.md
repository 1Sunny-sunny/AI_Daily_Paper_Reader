## 摘要
工作记忆（WM）能够临时维持和操作信息，支持灵活、目标导向的行为。虽然汇聚的证据表明海马体及其他脑区的持续活动对工作记忆存储有贡献，但这种联系尚未通过实验操作神经活动进行检验。在此，我们结合人类海马体的颅内单神经元同步记录与局部电刺激，测试了与记忆项目相关的选择性持续神经活动在工作记忆中的作用。30 名植入混合深度电极的患者执行了一项以图像为记忆材料的工作记忆任务。在部分试次的维持期间，对海马体施加电刺激（2 秒，50 赫兹，1 毫安）。行为上，刺激损害了工作记忆表现，增加了反应时间，尤其在低负荷条件下。神经元层面，刺激降低了海马体和腹侧颞叶皮层（VTC）中记忆项目选择性活动，从而破坏了工作记忆内容的特定神经表征。刺激引起的发放率变化程度中介了逐试次的记忆相关行为受损，将神经选择性受损与工作记忆损害联系起来。在群体水平，刺激使神经轨迹偏离吸引子状态，与记忆保真度下降一致。综上，这些数据证明海马体和 VTC 中单个神经元的选择性持续活动支持人类的工作记忆维持。我们的结果表明，海马刺激同时干扰了单神经元编码和群体水平吸引子稳定性，将细胞机制与行为联系起来，并凸显了持续活动对工作记忆维持的贡献。

## Abstract
Working memory (WM) enables the temporary maintenance and manipulation of information, supporting flexible, goal-directed behavior. While converging evidence suggests that persistent activity in the hippocampus and other areas of the brain con-tributes to WM storage, this link has not been examined by manipulating neural activity experimentally. Here, we combined simultaneous intracranial single-neuron record-ings with focal electrical stimulation in the human hippocampus to test the role of memoranda-selective persistent neural activity for WM. Thirty patients with implanted hybrid depth electrodes performed a WM task with images as memoranda. Electrical stimulation (2 s, 50 Hz, 1 mA) was delivered to the hippocampus during the mainte-nance period on a subset of trials. Behaviorally, stimulation impaired WM perfor-mance, increasing response times especially in low-load conditions. Neuronally, stim-ulation reduced memoranda-selective activity in hippocampus and ventral temporal cortex (VTC), thereby disrupting content-specific neural representations of WM con-tent. The extent of stimulation-induced change in firing rate mediated trial-by-trial im-paired WM-related behavior, linking disrupted neural selectivity to impaired WM. At the population level, stimulation shifted neural trajectories farther from attractor states, consistent with degraded mnemonic fidelity. Together, these data provide evidence that selective persistent activity of individual neurons in hippocampus and VTC sup-ports WM maintenance in humans. Our results demonstrate that hippocampal stimula-tion disrupts both single-neuron coding and population-level attractor stability, linking cellular mechanisms to behavior and highlighting the contributions of persistent activi-ty to WM maintenance.
