## 摘要
我们的决策过程内容如何转化为主观信心？当前旨在解释选择和信心报告行为的计算模型在两个基本问题上存在分歧：选择和信心报告是源于共享还是独立的评估过程，以及信心报告的证据采样采用何种停止规则。在这里，我们采用了一种基于神经信息的建模方法，通过比较竞争模型在行为拟合度以及再现人类证据累积的电生理特征方面的能力来进行模型比较。参与者在随机点运动任务上表示感知选择后，以自定进度进行信心报告。仅基于行为数据的模型比较显示，基于边界的信心报告停止规则比基于时间的停止规则提供了更好的拟合度。然而，当比较由单一证据累积过程决定选择和信心报告的模型与调用不同过程的模型时，结果并不确定。重要的是，单一过程模型在解释观察到的神经证据累积模式方面显著优于其他模型，包括在初始选择被报告之前几百毫秒，信号幅度就能预测报告的信心这一事实。我们的研究表明，选择行为、主观信心及相关的神经决策信号可以通过一个调用单一证据累积过程的模型来共同解释，该过程对于初始选择和随后的信心报告分别设置了不同的边界。

## Abstract
How is the content of our decision making processes translated into subjective confidence? Current computational models designed to account for choice- and confidence-reporting behaviour disagree on two fundamental questions: do choice and confidence reports arise from shared versus distinct evaluation processes and what stopping-rule is applied to evidence sampling for confidence reports? Here, we employed a neurally-informed modelling approach in which competing models were compared both based on their fits to behaviour and their ability to recapitulate human electrophysiological signatures of evidence accumulation. Participants made self-paced confidence reports after indicating perceptual choices on a random dot motion task. Model comparison based on behavioural data alone showed that boundary-based stopping rules for confidence reporting provided superior fits than time-based stopping rules. However, results were inconclusive when comparing models with a single evidence accumulation process dictating choice and confidence reports compared to models invoking distinct processes. Importantly, the single-process model markedly outperformed the other models in its ability to account for the observed neural evidence accumulation patterns, including the fact that signal amplitudes predicted reported confidence several hundred milliseconds before the initial choice had been reported. Our study demonstrates that choice behaviour, subjective confidence, and associated neural decision signals can be jointly explained by a model invoking a single process of evidence accumulation with separate boundaries for the initial choice and subsequent confidence reports.
