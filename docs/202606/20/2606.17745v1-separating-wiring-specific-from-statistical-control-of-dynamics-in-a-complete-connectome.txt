## ABSTRACT 

Electron-microscopy reconstruction now yields complete synaptic wiring diagrams, or connectomes, of entire small brains, including the larval Drosophila , the first insect brain reconstructed in full. How far a wiring diagram alone fixes a circuit’s activity, as opposed to the finer physiological detail it does not record, is debated. We run a complete connectome as a fixed, rate-based dynamical operator in which no single-neuron parameter is fitted, so that, at one fixed dynamical regime, the model’s behavior reflects the wiring and its connection strengths rather than tuned single-neuron physiology, and compare it against a hierarchy of randomized networks that each preserve a coarser description of the wiring. The model’s overall dynamical regime, how strongly and how richly it responds, is mostly statistical: networks keeping only the connectome’s coarse wiring statistics reproduce it. The wiring beyond these statistics instead sets where activity travels and which circuits shape it. Sparse input is confined to a compact olfactory pathway that randomized networks flood, and the mushroom body, the insect learning center, takes an outsized role in the leading adjoint-side modes, the directions that weigh which neurons shape the recurrent dynamics. Coarse statistics set the regime; the precise pattern of connections sets the geometry, a separation that clarifies which connectome-based claims rest on wiring alone. 

## 1 Introduction 

How much of a brain’s activity is fixed by its wiring diagram alone? For most of neuroscience the question could not be answered for a real brain, because no complete connectome existed. That has changed. Electron-microscopy reconstructions now yield the synaptic wiring diagram, or connectome, of entire small nervous systems, which makes it possible to ask, for an actual brain, which dynamical properties follow from the precise pattern of connections and which follow from coarser statistical features of the wiring. Complete reconstructions now span the nematode C. elegans , the larval and adult Drosophila and larval zebrafish, and dense electron-microscopy volumes of mouse and human cortex are being assembled toward the same goal at mammalian scale [1–6]. The question above is more challenging than it first appears. Connectivity constrains dynamics only loosely: networks with identical wiring but different cellular properties can produce different activity, so function cannot be read off a connectome alone [ 7]. The choice of comparison is a second challenge. Showing that a biological connectome differs from an unstructured random network establishes that it is non-random, but it cannot say whether a given property depends on the exact placement of synapses or only on coarse features such as how many partners each neuron has and how connection strengths are distributed. Telling these apart requires a null model that preserves the coarse features and scrambles only the placement. The larval Drosophila brain is well suited for this purpose. Complete synaptic connectomes had previously been mapped only for organisms with a few hundred neurons; the larval fly is the first complete insect connectome: 3’013 neurons joined by 111’243 directed connections, of which a strongly connected core of 2’825 neurons carries 109’438, in an animal with a behavioral repertoire that includes associative learning and navigation [ 1]. The same wiring has already been used as a fixed computational substrate, as a reservoir for time-series prediction [ 12 , 13 ] and, for the larval connectome, as a fixed recurrent network trained only at its input and output [ 14 ]. Here, we characterize the operator itself, with no readout and no training, asking what the wiring determines rather than what it can be trained to compute. Because it is a real reconstruction, we are deliberate about interpretation: we study the connectome as a frozen dynamical operator, a fixed rate model in which no single-neuron parameter is tuned, so that every measured property is attributable to the wiring and its weights. We report structural-dynamical properties of that operator, not a physiological simulation of the larva. We organize the analysis as a ladder of null models, each matched to the level of the claim it tests. To detect gross differences, we compare the connectome with an unstructured Gaussian control. To ask whether a property depends on the specific wiring beyond its coarse statistics, we compare it with a degree-and-weight-matched ensemble: rewired networks that preserve every neuron’s in-degree, out-degree and total outgoing weight, together with the global multiset of connection strengths, while scrambling where the connections go [ 15 ]. For claims that a particular circuit carries a property, we add two further controls, one that checks the concentration is specific to the operator’s eigenmodes rather than its high-gain singular subspace and one that tests the circuit against arbitrary sets of the same size. The picture that emerges is a separation of levels. The operator’s global dynamics, its gain, its dimensionality and its near-linearity, are mostly statistical: at a common spectral radius, the matched ensemble reproduces them, so they do not depend on the specific wiring. The specific wiring instead governs where activity travels. Under sparse input, the connectome restricts activity to a small fraction of the brain and routes it into the olfactory pathway, where matched rewiring distributes it widely. This confinement is partially reproduced by cell-class architecture, with the remainder driven by fine synaptic placement concentrated in the mushroom body. On the driving side of the dynamics the mushroom body concentrates the operator’s dominant modes beyond every matched control and depletes them from the convergence neurons, the small structured set of driving-side departures we find; a candidate lateral-horn localization on the driven side did not survive the size-matched control and is retracted, an instance of the null hierarchy working. Coarse statistics establish the global dynamical regime. In contrast, specific wiring determines the pathways of activity and governs which circuit dominates the leading driving modes. The model is a structural-dynamical instrument, not a simulation of the living larva: it omits action potentials, single-neuron time constants, synaptic and receptor kinetics, neuromodulatory and behavioral state, and gap junctions, and makes no physiological claim about odor-evoked activity in the animal. 

## 2 Results 

2.1 Global operator behavior is mostly explained by degree-and-weight statistics 

We first asked whether the recurrent core’s gross dynamical behavior comes from the precise placement of its synapses or from coarser features of the wiring. The distinction matters because it sets how much a connectome-constrained model owes to the exact circuit and how much it owes to statistics any wiring with the same degree and weight profile would share. Run as a frozen operator, the core is low-gain and only weakly non-normal: it amplifies activity modestly, and amplifies somewhat more in a single step than its long-run gain would suggest. We summarize this by the ratio of the largest single-step amplification to the asymptotic gain, σ1/ρ , which is 1.325 for the connectome (spectral radius ρ = 296 .6, largest singular value σ1 = 393 .1, Frobenius norm 1’825, before the assay’s spectral-radius normalization). The degree-and-weight-matched ensemble, which preserves each neuron’s in-degree, out-degree and outgoing weight and the global weight multiset while scrambling placement, reproduces this ratio closely (1.309 ±

0.007); the connectome sits high within the ensemble, at rank 980 of 1’001 (z = +2.2, with only 2.1% of rewires above it). An unstructured Gaussian operator is far higher (2.079) (Fig. 2a,b). The operator’s modest gain is therefore governed by the wiring statistics: scrambling the placement while preserving those statistics leaves it essentially unchanged [ 16 ]. We state this as an empirical reproduction rather than a proof of sufficiency, since the ensemble preserves each neuron’s in-degree, out-degree and out-strength, its column sum because weights travel with their source, and the global weight multiset, but not each neuron’s in-strength, its row sum. Because out-strength fixes each column sum, the matrix 1-norm is preserved across the ensemble by construction, so this reproduction of the gain ratio is in part definitional; the dimensionality and near-linearity reproductions, which the 1-norm does not fix, are not. In short, this null is a strong test of placement beyond degree and outgoing weight, but not a complete test of incoming weighted mass, which is why the routing analysis below adds a row-strength diagnostic. Dimensionality follows the same pattern. Effective rank, the number of independent activity directions an operator preserves, is far higher for the connectome (2’426 of a possible 2’825 dimensions) than for the Gaussian control (1’503), and here too the matched ensemble, not the random control, is the connectome’s near neighbor. The precise wiring leaves only a modest residual. Driven at low input rank ( k = 8 ), the connectome preserves slightly more dimensions than its rewires (effective rank 8.46 against 6.18), an excess of roughly 37% that shrinks to under 1% once the full input space is engaged. The operator is also marginally more linear than its rewires: its high-rank nonlinearity is 0.0179, below every one of the 1’000 matched rewires (ensemble 0.0323 ± 0.0002) (Fig. 2c). These wiring-specific differences are real but small, and we report them as effect sizes rather than as extreme ranks, because the matched ensemble’s spread is so narrow that rank alone would overstate their importance. The reading is consistent across all three measures. Under the assay’s normalization to a common spectral radius, the connectome’s global operator signature, its gain, its dimensionality and its degree of nonlinearity, is mostly set by its degree-and-weight statistics, with a wiring-specific increment of up to roughly 37% confined to low and middle dimensions. The specific wiring’s decisive effects appear elsewhere, in where activity is routed, which we turn to below. In other words, how strongly and how richly this circuit transforms its input is something almost any wiring with the same connection counts and strengths would do; what the exact placement of synapses decides is not how much the circuit computes, but where the computation goes. 2.2 Module maps recover known circuits, with definition-dependent boundaries 

We next asked whether the connectome’s communities correspond to the brain’s known circuits, and how cleanly each circuit can be drawn around. Because the later analyses ask whether particular circuits carry particular dynamical properties, we first define the circuits and state how sharply each is bounded. The mushroom body, the insect learning center, is the sharpest. Under dataset cell-class labels it comprises 153 Kenyon cells, the parallel-fiber neurons that store associations, together with 48 output neurons, 28 modulatory input neurons and the accessory anterior paired lateral (APL) neuron, 231 in total [ 8]. The APL count reflects the reconstructed APL skeletons in the dataset; the larval APL is classically a single identified GABAergic interneuron per hemisphere, so this dataset count and the classical single-cell description refer to the same neuron under two conventions [17, 18]. The lateral horn and the central complex are bounded less sharply, and the boundary depends on how the circuit is defined, whether by connectivity clustering or by expert anatomical annotation. For the lateral horn, the second-order olfactory center, the clustering-defined core contains 201 neurons, the annotation-defined set 445, and their intersection 164 [ 9, 10 ]. For the central complex, the navigation system, the clustering-defined set contains 77 neurons, the annotation-defined set 34, and their overlap 10 [ 11 ]. Every later claim about these circuits therefore states which boundary it uses, and we treat no central-complex result as a boundary-free statement about a single unambiguous set; the modular block structure these definitions partition is visible in the connectome’s community-ordered connectivity matrix (Fig. 3a), and the full set-size comparison is given in Supplementary Material. Connectivity-defined communities in this connectome align with cell type and function while their boundaries shift with the detection method [19]. As a structural check, the module maps recover the expected anatomy of olfactory-to-convergence flow: both the mushroom-body and lateral-horn output populations route prominently onto convergence neurons, the cells that pool signals from several pathways (see Methods). Outside the three modules, the remaining 2’316 core neurons fall, by dataset class, into a majority of typed but otherwise unclassified neurons (1’137, un-typed rather than unimaged), then descending (349), sensory (338), convergence (184), feedback (142) and olfactory projection and local neurons (114), with 52 further typed. 2.3 The mushroom body concentrates the driving modes; mode leverage is otherwise diffuse 

Is a single part of the circuit uniquely responsible for shaping these dynamics, or is this influence distributed uniformly across the brain? To move beyond this global perspective, we asked a more refined question: does the precise wiring afford any specific circuit a disproportionate role in the operator’s dominant dynamical modes, beyond what would be predicted by its degree and weight statistics? A dynamical mode has two sides, and we separate them throughout. The driving side (the left eigenvectors, equivalently the leading subspace of the adjoint operator) is the set of neurons that weigh most heavily in a mode; the driven side (the right eigenvectors) is the set the mode most loads onto (Fig. 4a). Because the operator is non-normal, concentration on a circuit’s left-eigenvector subspace is a spectral property of the wiring, not by itself a demonstration of causal driving or input-output influence, which would require a controllability analysis we do not attempt here; we use the term driving throughout in this spectral, adjoint-subspace sense. We tested seven pre-specified neuron sets: five anatomically defined circuits, the mushroom body, the lateral horn, the central complex, the convergence neurons and the descending output neurons; the mushroom body’s recurrent output network, formed by its output and modulatory neurons; and the population the connectome activates under sparse input drive 5Wiring-specific versus statistical control of connectome dynamics PREPRINT 

(defined in the routing analysis below). For each set we measured its leverage, the share of the leading modes’ energy that concentrates on it, across mode counts from 1 to 16, and required a candidate to pass two controls. The first asks whether the circuit is more extreme than arbitrary sets of the same size, by comparing it against 1’000 random size-matched sets and keeping a cell only when the connectome exceeds the 95th percentile of those sets and the random sets are themselves extreme in fewer than 20% of draws. The second asks whether the concentration is specific to the operator’s eigenmodes or is merely a feature of its high-gain directions, by checking that the leading singular subspace, the operator-norm subspace of maximal input-output gain, does not carry the same concentration. The two controls together separate a wiring-specific eigenmode role from one that the operator’s coarse statistics or its gain geometry would produce on their own. The result is diffuse across most of the circuits, with a small structured set of departures on the driving side (Fig. 4). Five cells meet the pre-specified guard; three are comfortably inside it and carry the result. The strongest is the mushroom body, the learning center, on the driving side. Its leading-mode concentration exceeds the entire 1’000-rewire ensemble at four modes (0.4418, against an ensemble mean of 0.2053 ± 0.0161 and maximum of 0.3222; z = +14 .7,rank 1’001 of 1’001) and at eight modes (0.3163, against 0.1935 ± 0.0133, maximum 0.2474; z = +9 .2) (Fig. 4c). Both survive the size-matched control with wide margin (random sets extreme in 4.7% and 7.1% of draws) and are not merely a gain-subspace effect: the driving-side concentration far exceeds what the singular subspace produces (z = +14 .7 and +9 .2 on the driving side, against +2 .9 and +1 .5 for the singular subspace), and the singular subspace is not itself at the extreme rank. Both reproduce exactly under a dense eigendecomposition, and the driving-side subspace is numerically well-conditioned despite the operator’s non-normality (Methods). The biological reading is that the learning center’s specific wiring, not merely its connection counts, gives it a distinctive role in the leading adjoint subspace, the left-eigenvector directions of the recurrent dynamics. Read in both registers, this is one finding: for the machine-learning reader, the operator’s driving modes concentrate on the mushroom body above the matched ensemble under both guards; for the neuroscientist, the learning center’s exact wiring is what lifts it into the set of neurons that weigh most heavily in the circuit’s leading modes. The convergence neurons give a second, weaker cell at eight modes, in the opposite direction (an under-concentration, z = −5.2), so the wiring concentrates driving influence in the mushroom body and away from the convergence pool. Two further cells, both at the highest mode count, sit at the edge of the size-matched threshold (random sets extreme in 19% of draws, against the 20% bound) and we do not lean on them. The central complex, whose boundary is itself definition-dependent, shows no certified cell, and we make no wiring-specificity claim for it. The lateral horn is the audit-critical negative, and it lies on the other side of the dynamics. An earlier analysis had flagged the lateral horn as a candidate on the driven side, where it appeared to take an unusually small share of the leading modes relative to the rewired ensemble. The size-matched control, at 1’000 sets, removes it. For driven-side mode counts 1 through 16, the lateral-horn deviation |z| = [18 .78 , 6.84 , 7.87 , 6.88 , 7.80] lies below the random size-201 95th-percentile envelope [42 .45 , 13 .16 , 8.40 , 10 .19 , 6.89] (Fig. 4b) at every count through eight; at sixteen modes it rises just above the envelope (7.80 against 6.89), but random sets of the same size are themselves extreme in 36% of draws, far above the 20% bound, so the control does not certify the lateral horn there either. We therefore retract the lateral-horn localization and fold it into the diffuse result. The retraction is on the driven side; the surviving mushroom-body result is on the driving side. The two concern opposite sides of the operator and are not in tension. Mode leverage is therefore mostly diffuse: across seven circuits and five mode counts, the exact wiring gives almost no circuit a dominant-mode role that its statistics do not already explain. The complete matrix of all seven sets, both eigenvector sides and the singular subspace across the five mode counts, 105 entries, is reported in Supplementary Table S3, so the certified cells are read against every test performed; five entries pass the guard and three carry the result. Across that 105-entry family a Westfall-Young max-statistic permutation over the degree-and-weight ensemble controls the family-wise error: the mushroom-body driving-side cells survive at adjusted p = 0.002 (m = 4) and 0.003 (m = 8) (Fig. 4d), and although all five flagged cells fall below an adjusted threshold of 0.05, the two highest-mode-count cells remain marginal under the size-matched guard, so we lean only on the three inside both controls. The robust departures form a small structured set on the driving side: the mushroom body over-concentrates the leading adjoint (driving-side) modes beyond every matched control, and the convergence neurons under-concentrate them. In plain terms, almost nowhere does the precise wiring single out a circuit beyond its raw statistics, and the one place it clearly does is the learning center, whose wiring concentrates the circuit’s most influential modes onto itself.

The connectome’s best seed expanded more than all 30 degree-preserving rewires (a ratio of 1.535 against the ensemble maximum of 1.522), which had looked like a wiring-specific effect. The expansion is therefore generic to a sparse fan-out with a threshold, which is the classical expectation [ 22 ], rather than a property of the specific calyx wiring. The plain reading is that the model recovers the calyx’s sparse-coding function, but that function would arise in any comparably wired fan-out, so it is the same control hierarchy, not a weaker result, that keeps the reconstruction and removes the wiring-specific overlay. 

2.5 Specific wiring confines sparse input drive into the olfactory pathway, beyond cell-class architecture and incoming strength 

Does the exact placement of synapses control where input-driven activity travels, or do the connectome’s coarse wiring statistics already fix it, and if the placement matters, is it the grouping of neurons into cell classes that does the work or a still finer pattern within those classes? The clearest wiring-specific result comes from routing rather than from the dominant modes. We drove the core through its afferent ports, an abstract input shell, and measured how widely activity spreads; here activity means the modeled operator state variance each neuron carries under the drive, and a neuron counts as active when that variance exceeds the threshold defined in Methods. Because the larval olfactory receptor neurons lie inside the modeled core rather than on this afferent shell, the drive enters the olfactory pathway downstream of the receptor layer, so the routing reported below refers to which modeled cell classes carry the propagated state, not odor-evoked activity in the larva. The connectome confines it to a fifth of the core (561 of 2’825 neurons, an active fraction of 0.1986), while the degree-and-weight-matched ensemble lets the same drive spread to two-thirds (0.6682 

± 0.0078) and an unstructured Gaussian operator, the trivial floor with no wiring structure, activates essentially the whole core (1.0). The connectome is the most confined of all 1’001 graphs (rank 1, z = −60 .56 ), a 3.4-fold deepening relative to its matched rewires (Fig. 5a,b). The confinement is routed, not uniform. The active fifth is the olfactory pathway: projection and local neurons (55% of them active), the lateral horn (52%) and the convergence neurons (42%) take most of the drive, while the mushroom body is largely spared (19% active) and the central complex receives none of it (0%). The reading is channeling rather than broadcast: even the most engaged class sits just above half, and the drive falls to a fifth in the mushroom body and to nothing in the central complex, so the wiring meters the drive everywhere it reaches and never saturates the regions it enters. The matched rewires flood all of these instead, activating 98% of the mushroom body, 90% of the lateral horn and 77% of the central complex in a representative rewire (Fig. 5). The specific wiring thus selects which neurons receive the drive, channeling it into a functionally coherent olfactory shell, a selection its degree and weight statistics do not make. Two facts establish that this is wiring-specific rather than a side effect of overall gain. First, the matched ensemble reproduces the connectome’s non-normality ratio (1.31 against 1.325), so the deeper confinement cannot follow from how strongly the operator amplifies. Second, the same ensemble, which preserves every neuron’s degree and out-strength and the entire weight multiset, destroys the connectome’s higher-order structure: directed reciprocity (0.2609 against an ensemble 0.0253), global and local clustering (0.2373 against 0.0546; 0.2620 against 0.0554), modularity (0.4988 against 0.0911) and triangle count (695’611 against 213’191) all stand far above the entire ensemble, each at rank 1’001 of 1’001 (Fig. 3b,c). Within those limits it is the paper’s clearest wiring-specific signature: with each neuron’s in-degree, out-degree and outgoing weight matched, the placement of synapses routes sparse input into a tight olfactory shell. The importance of this result is not that olfactory circuitry is anatomically localized, which is already expected, but that a frozen operator can separate localization due to coarse connection statistics from localization that requires the exact synapse-by-synapse placement. Restoring each node’s in-strength to a rewired graph, the one quantity the matched ensemble leaves free, recovers about 37% of the confinement magnitude but not its geometry: the active set overlaps the connectome’s only partially (Jaccard 0.35), with a different class profile (correlation 0.59) and far weaker concentration (Gini 0.69 against 0.85). ## 3 Discussion 

What aspects of a brain’s dynamics are constrained by its connectome, and what aspects remain indeterminate? Evaluated as a frozen operator, i.e., a recurrent network read directly from the anatomy without parameter fitting, the larval connectome allowed us to address this question by revealing two distinct classes of dynamical properties. 

3.1 The wiring sets the geometry; the statistics set the regime 

The connectome’s gross behavior is reproduced, at a common spectral radius, by any wiring that keeps its degree sequence and weight multiset: the modest non-normal amplification, meaning transient signal growth even though no single mode is unstable, the preserved activity dimensionality and the weak nonlinearity. Its geometry, where sparse input travels and which neurons dominate the leading modes, is fixed by its specific wiring instead (Fig. 6). In plain terms, many wirings with the same degrees and weights would give a similar global dynamical regime, but they would not send sparse input through the same pathway or assign the same leading-mode role to the mushroom body. The statistical side is expected from theory: in nonlinear recurrent networks the dimension of collective activity is set by the coupling variance and the effective rank of the connectivity [ 23 ], both coarse properties the degree-and-weight ensemble preserves, so the near-exact reproduction of the dimensionality is what that theory predicts. 

3.2 Two roles the exact wiring assigns, read as circuit and as operator 

Two of the results are specific to the exact wiring, and each can be described from both a machine-learning and a neurobiology perspective. Under a sparse afferent drive the connectome routes activity into a compact olfactory shell, the projection and local neurons, the lateral horn and the convergence cells, while a statistically matched network spreads the same drive across the brain; a block-preserving null that additionally fixes the cell-class architecture reproduces only part of this confinement, the residual concentrating in the mushroom body (Fig. 5). On the driving side of the dynamics the mushroom body, the insect learning center, concentrates the operator’s leading modes beyond every matched control, and away from the convergence neurons (Fig. 4); lesioning the mushroom body confirms it is mechanistically tied to the leading (four-mode) adjoint subspace, through its incoming recurrent embedding rather than its outgoing weight alone, while it does not control the non-mushroom-body routing (Supplementary). From a machine-learning perspective, these are properties of a structured operator: the wiring fixes a low-dimensional routing pattern for sparse input and an over-represented driving subspace that its second-order statistics leave unspecified. From a neurobiology perspective, the larval brain’s specific connectivity gives the olfactory pathway and the learning center dynamical roles that connection counts alone would not anticipate. 11 Wiring-specific versus statistical control of connectome dynamics PREPRINT 

Structural analyses of this connectome point the same way: an independent community analysis finds a rich-club of hub interneurons concentrated in a few midbrain communities, one of them aligned with the mushroom body’s Kenyon and output cells [ 19 ], the larval structural core is itself composed of mushroom-body neurons [ 24 ], and connectome modularity concentrates communication within functionally coherent clusters [ 25 ]. Our driving-mode concentration in the mushroom body and our routing of sparse drive into a compact olfactory shell are the activity-level counterparts of those structural findings, reached by a different instrument. 

3.3 The null hierarchy is both the method and the discipline that retracted two readings 

Drawing the line between the two kinds of property required a hierarchy of nulls, and that hierarchy is both the method we propose and the discipline that held the strongest claims in check. An unstructured operator shows only that the connectome is non-random. A degree-and-weight-matched ensemble tests whether a property needs the exact placement of synapses or only their coarse statistics [ 15 ]. A claim that a named circuit carries a property needs a further control, against arbitrary neuron sets of the same size, because a tight ensemble makes rank extremity common even where nothing is special. The discriminating power of these controls is itself a result. Each removed a candidate that a coarser test had passed: the lateral-horn concentration on the driven side was flagged against the degree-and-weight ensemble but removed once arbitrary sets of the same size proved extreme as often, and the calyx expansion exceeded all rewires on its single best seed but fell within ensemble noise once its three seeds were accounted. They left the central complex, whose anatomical boundary is itself definition-dependent in this immature brain, as a boundary-split signal. The two removals are what give the survivors their force: a property is reported as wiring-specific only after the matched ensemble and its stricter accounting have both failed to reproduce it, so the necessity of these controls is shown on real candidates rather than asserted. 

3.4 Scope of the operator claim 

These are statements about a frozen, rate-based, norm-rescaled operator. The model omits spikes and time constants, graded and short-term synaptic dynamics, receptor kinetics, neuromodulatory and behavioral state, gap junctions and any fitted biophysical parameter, so it does not report the operating regime of the living animal. It reports which structural features of the reconstructed wiring control which measured properties of the operator, and that narrower statement is the one the controls support. We make no spatial claim: the operator has no geometric term, and at single-neuron resolution straight-line distance poorly predicts synaptic connectivity in this connectome [ 19 ], so the placement that produces the confinement may itself be spatially organized in ways our nulls neither model nor need to separate. 

3.5 Stiff and sloppy directions of a wiring family 

The separation has a counterpart in connectome-constrained network models. There the unknown quantities are biophysical: a connectome alone often does not fix a network’s dynamics, because many single-neuron parameter configurations are consistent with the same wiring, and only some of these combinations are ’stiff’, strongly shaping the dynamics, while others are ’sloppy’ and barely change it [ 7 ]. Our setting fits nothing and has no single-neuron parameters at all, so the counterpart is structural: the degree-and-weight-matched ensemble is the family of wirings consistent with the connectome’s coarse description, and the separation says which dynamical signatures the whole family shares and which the exact wiring alone produces. In that vocabulary the magnitude properties are the sloppy directions, common to the family and robust to the exact placement, a robustness of the kind that marks biological systems, and the routing and driving-mode structure are the stiff directions, where the statistical degeneracy is broken and the placement is decisive. What we establish is a separation rather than a prediction of the connectome’s activity. The coarse synaptic statistics, often all that gets reported for a connectome, fix its gross dynamical regime but leave its routing and its driving modes undetermined; its specific wiring settles those. Whether the same split, statistics fixing the regime and specific wiring fixing the geometry, holds in other connectomes is an open question that the same null-matched approach can settle. For the larval Drosophila connectome the statement is definite: its global operator behavior is mostly statistical, its dominant-mode leverage is diffuse apart from a small driving-side set led by the mushroom body’s over-concentration, and the clearest mark of its specific wiring is the routing of sparse input into a compact olfactory shell, beyond cell-class architecture. We read this as the first in a line of work that asks, of each newly reconstructed connectome, the same null-matched question: which of its dynamical properties follow from coarse connection statistics that any matched wiring would reproduce, and which are written into the exact placement of its synapses. The instrument is portable because the 12 Wiring-specific versus statistical control of connectome dynamics PREPRINT 

ensembles travel with the graph rather than with the species, and the same logic can audit engineered recurrent architectures: it distinguishes performance or dynamics that come from coarse graph statistics from those that require a particular wiring geometry. The answer it returns is a clean partition: the part of a connectome’s dynamics that its wiring alone earns, separated from the part its statistics already concede. That partition is the principle we offer, and the larval brain is where we first read it off. 

## 4 Methods 

4.1 Known confounds and scope 

Several limitations bound every result below and are collected here. Synapse signs are not used: the recurrent matrix is built from unsigned synapse-count magnitudes, so excitation and inhibition are not distinguished (the routing result is shown to survive an excitation-inhibition sign cone). The matched ensembles preserve out-strength but not each neuron’s in-strength, so the sparse-input confinement is decomposed against a row-strength-restored control, which recovers part of its magnitude while its geometry remains placement-specific. Every operator is rescaled to a common spectral radius, so absolute gain is normalized away and the comparisons are of geometry at a fixed scale (the routing result persists across a grid of spectral radius, leak and input amplitude). Activity is thresholded at a fixed fraction of the driven-set variance, and the confinement holds across that threshold. The leading-mode concentration is a spectral, adjoint-side property, not a demonstration of causal control. All results are for a single connectome, the larval 

Drosophila ; whether they generalize is an open question. The model is a frozen operator, not a measurement of activity in the living animal. 

4.2 Connectome dataset and recurrent core 

We used the complete larval Drosophila connectome [ 1 ], taking the directed, weighted edge list in which each edge weight is the reconstructed synapse count between two neurons. The graph has 3’013 neurons and 111’243 directed synaptic edges; weights are integer synapse counts and are used as magnitudes only, without sign. Data integrity was checked by SHA-256 against the archived manifest before use. The recurrent substrate is the largest strongly connected component of this graph, computed independently with two libraries that agreed neuron for neuron: a core of 2’825 neurons joined by 109’438 internal directed edges. Reported neuron and connection counts for this connectome vary with the processing version: the core here is 2’825 neurons, against the 2’810-node strongly connected component in [25] and the 2’952 cells in [19]. The remaining 188 neurons form a thin feedforward periphery that partitions cleanly into 80 afferent ports (they send into the core and receive nothing back, through 524 couplings) and 97 efferent ports (they receive from the core and send nothing in, through 1’161 couplings), with the remaining 11 peripheral neurons connecting only within the periphery and no neuron serving both an afferent and an efferent role. The counts close on both sides: 2’825 + 80 + 97 + 11 = 3’013 neurons, and 109’438 core-internal, 524 afferent-injection, 1’161 efferent-readout and 120 periphery-internal couplings sum to the 111’243 directed connections. Within the core, 536 self-loops (autapses, weights 1 to 14) are retained on the diagonal, and 235 core neurons receive afferent input. The olfactory receptor neurons lie inside the core rather than on the afferent shell, so a port-only drive enters the olfactory pathway downstream of the receptor layer, a point we return to in the routing assay.

4.11 Random-set and singular guards, and the lateral-horn retraction 

Because a named set can rank extreme against the ensemble merely by virtue of its size or its overlap with a generic singular direction, a circuit is read as carrying a property only after two guards. The random size-matched guard, a size-matched permutation control, draws 1’000 random sets of the circuit’s size (seed 19019) and computes, per subspace and m, the 95th percentile of the absolute z-score across random sets and the fraction of random sets that are rank-extreme; a cell passes only if it is rank-extreme against the ensemble, its absolute z exceeds the random 95th percentile, and fewer than 20% of random sets are themselves extreme. The singular guard flags a cell as leakage if it is extreme while the same set’s singular cell at that m is extreme in the same direction, so that an apparent eigen-subspace effect that is really a generic singular-subspace effect is not counted. 4.14 Reproducibility, audits and provenance 

The operator is built deterministically with no random number generator; every stochastic step uses a fixed, recorded seed (rewire instances 2000 to 2999, random sets 19019, port streams 93101, Louvain 50503, rank-k sweep streams 70000 plus k). Each load-bearing value reproduces cold on a fresh process, and the headline numbers were re-locked at 1’000-instance resolution with a gate that halts and reports if any conclusion changes. Every load-bearing interpretive claim was checked against the null a skeptic would demand in a controls-completeness audit; two claims that did not survive (the driven-side lateral-horn localization and the wiring-specific calyx expansion) were retracted, with the original sealed text preserved and the retraction recorded append-only. Data provenance is fixed by SHA-256 against the archived manifest. The derived result files, with an integrity manifest and seeds, are publicly available at https://github.com/Stavros963/fcta , and the full analysis pipeline follows on publication; the connectome itself is publicly available from its original source [1].
