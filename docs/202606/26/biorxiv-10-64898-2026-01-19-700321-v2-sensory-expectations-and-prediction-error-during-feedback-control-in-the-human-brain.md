## 摘要
当身体受到的外部扰动能够被预先预测时，我们可以更好地抵消其影响。本研究通过功能性磁共振成像(fMRI)技术探究了概率性预测如何通过神经机制塑造反馈响应。结果表明，在手指受到机械扰动之前，初级运动皮层(M1)和躯体感觉皮层(S1)会接收一个线性编码预期感觉输入的信号。当扰动到达这些区域时，预期通过简单的叠加机制与感觉输入相结合，从而产生反映两者加权和的运动指令。同时，M1和S1还会接收一个预测误差信号，该信号编码了预期与实际感觉输入之间的差异。这一信号在人类的fMRI数据和非人灵长类的局部场电位中可见，但在M1或S1的锋电位活动中并未发现。

## Abstract
External disturbances to the body are better counteracted when their nature can be predicted in advance. Here, we investigated the neural mechanisms through which probabilistic predictions shape feedback responses using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) in humans. We show that, prior to a mechanical perturbation applied to a finger, the primary motor (M1) and somatosensory (S1) cortices receive a signal that linearly encodes the expected sensory input. When perturbations reach these areas, expectations are combined with the sensory input through a simple additive mechanism, yielding motor commands that reflect a weighted sum of the two signals. At the same time, M1 and S1 receive a prediction error signal encoding the difference between expectations and actual sensory input. This signal is visible in fMRI data in humans and in the local field potentials in non-human primates, but not in M1 or S1 spiking activity.
