# Stabilizability of neural fields from thick subsets 

Clemens Bombach 

Predictive Analytics TU Chemnitz 

Chemnitz, Germany clemens.bombach@hsw.tu-chemnitz.de

Predictive Analytics TU Chemnitz

Abstract —An important problem in neuro-engineering is the stabilization of neural fields. In applications, it is often assumed that the actuator placement can be chosen arbitrarily. In this work, we investigate the stabilizability of controlled Amari-type neural fields where the control input is prescribed to act only on a fixed subset of the neural field. We show that the linearized neural field is open-loop stabilizable under suitable assumptions on the interaction strength and a mild relative density assumption on the control set. Our geometric assumption requires that the volume of each cube intersected with the control set must be bounded below. As a consequence, we derive closed-loop stabilizability of the neural fields, under sensor/actuator placement constraints. Numerical simulations are used to illustrate the results and obtain empirical estimates on the control cost. 

Index Terms —neural fields, stabilization, control systems

The main technical difficulty arises in the study of the linearized system. This can be explained as follows: Our strategy for proving the open-loop stabilizability of (2) rests on the well-known duality between controllability or stabiliz-ability on the one side and observability on the other side. More precisely, we verify an abstract criterion for open-loop stabilizability proven in [12]. This criterion follows from the fact that the open-loop stabilizability of (2) is equivalent to a weak observability property of the adjoint system 

∂tϕ − B∗ϕ = 0 , ψ = 1E ϕ, ϕ(0) = ϕ0 .

Roughly speaking, the result of [12] states that (2) is open-loop stabilizable if a certain abstract uncertainty principle and dissipation estimate hold. This criterion can be seen as a generalization of the Lebeau-Robbiano strategy for proving null-controllability of the heat equation, see [13] and, for instance, the related works [14]–[16]. In our concrete situation, the relevant uncertainty principle is the Logvinenko-Sereda theorem, which states that if a function u is band-limited, i.e. if the Fourier transform ˆu is concentrated on a cube, then u cannot be concentrated on the complement of a thick set in a quantitative sense. The corresponding dissipation estimate requires that high frequencies in the Fourier decomposition of Stu must decay sufficiently quickly as t → ∞ . If the semigroup St were smoothing – as is the case, for example, when considering the heat semigroup – then this would be a straightforward application of the argument given in [16], see also [12] for an application to the semigroup generated by the fractional Laplacian. The main complication in the proof of stabilizabil-ity for the Amari-type field equation comes from the fact that the semigroup St is not smoothing. Thus, a careful analysis is required. In particular, we must assume a smallness condition on the coupling constant μ in relation to α and the Fourier transform of the interaction kernel k.The paper is structured as follows: In Section II we fix the mathematical notation and state the necessary preliminaries from harmonic analysis. The stabilizability of the linearized system is proven in Section III. The application to the nonlin-ear system is given in Section IV. In section V, we illustrate the theoretical results with a simulation study. In the final section VI, we situate the obtained results in a wider context and discuss directions for future research. VI. D ISCUSSION 

We have shown that neural fields are stabilizable from thick subsets of Rd. More precisely, we have shown that there are three cases that have to be distinguished, depending on the relation between the activity decay α and the terms μ, κ ∗,coming from the neural interaction. If μκ ∗ − α < 0, then the linear system is already stable and there is no need for stabilization. If μκ ∗ − α > 0 but μκ ∗ − 2α < 0, then stabilization is possible by an open-loop control (Theorem III.5). If μκ ∗ − 2α > 0, it is not known whether the system is stabilizable to an equilibrium state. This result stands in stark contrast to the one of [8]. There, the authors prove approximate controllability to arbitrary target states with an error of size 

O(T 2) as T → 0, under a mild spectral condition on the Fr´ echet derivative of the nonlinearity, but with the assumption that E = Rd. Under restrictive smallness conditions on μ

and the final time T , they even obtain exact controllability. This suggests that restricting the control set either puts a strong theoretical limit on controllability of neural fields, or requires more advanced methods to show even approximate controllability. Let us note that we have made various simplifying assump-tions throughout this work: For instance, we have assumed that there is only one population of neurons. More biologically plausible neural field models often involve multiple popula-tions of excitatory or inhibitory neurons. Furthermore, we have assumed that the activation occurs without delay. Again, it would increase the biological realism to incorporate a delay, as done for instance in [9]. Regarding possible ways to handle multiple populations, we note that a Lebeau-Robbiano strategy for coupled heat-like systems has already been implemented in [27]. It seems plausible that such an approach would extend to linearized Amari systems involving multiple populations by the method outlined here. Incorporating delay would be more challenging within our framework and require an understanding of how to implement the Lebeau-Robbiano strategy in this setting or the pursuit of an alternate approach, for instance via Carleman estimates. On the practical side, our numerical experiment indicates that the size of the control set plays a substantial role in the determination of the control cost. On the theoretical side, our work demonstrates the utility of the Lebeau-Robbiano strategy even in situations where the underlying equation is quite different from the heat equation for which this strategy was originally intended. Indeed, the heat equation is local, linear and smoothing while we are here dealing with a nonlocal, nonlinear and nonsmoothing equation. It is known from the work [28], that for fractional heat equations of the form 

∂ta − (−∆) sa = 1E u, controllability is only guaranteed if 

s > 1/2 and fails if s ≤ 1/2 . However, stabilizability continues to hold as long as s > 0 (see [12]). Morally speaking, the Amari-type field equation roughly corresponds to the regime where (−∆) s is replaced by ϕ(−∆) with ϕ

bounded. Thus, it appears that we are at the limit of what is possible with a straightforward application of the Lebeau-Robbiano strategy. We conclude that it is a challenging open problem to de-termine the sharp geometric condition on which approximate controllability of neural fields holds, in particular in the strong-coupling regime μκ ∗ − 2α > 0. As an intermediate step, it might be of interest to consider sets that satisfy the stronger Geometric Control Condition (GCC), introduced in [29] to study the null-controllability of the wave equation.
