Abstract

Introduction
Artificial intelligence plays an increasingly prominent role in prediction, and vast amounts of data
are available from diverse sources. In many applications, the relationship between the covariates
and the mean response remains the same across sources, while the covariate distributions can vary
substantially. For example, surveys may sample demographic groups in different proportions, and
online advertising systems may collect data under different ad-selection policies. We study this
form of covariate shift across multiple data sources and ask how to combine their observations to
improve prediction for a target population of interest.
To formalize this setting, suppose we observe data from J sources. For source j, the observations

This framework captures several common applications. In online advertising, click data are
collected under different ad-selection policies, each inducing a source distribution over user char-
acteristics, displayed advertisements, and display contexts (Agarwal et al., 2017). When the click
probability given these covariates remains unchanged across policies, historical observations can be
combined to predict responses under the new policy. The same framework applies to surveys that
sample demographic groups in different proportions and experiments conducted under different
input designs, provided that changes in sampling or design preserve the conditional mean response
(Shimodaira, 2000; Sugiyama et al., 2007).
1.1
Literature review
Early work on covariate shift developed importance-weighting methods that adjust the contribution
of source observations to better represent the target population (Shimodaira, 2000; Sugiyama et al.,
2007). Subsequent studies developed data-driven weighting procedures (Huang et al., 2006; Bickel
et al., 2009) and examined how variable or extreme weights affect estimation accuracy, motivating
theoretical error bounds and truncation methods (Cortes et al., 2010; Ionides, 2008).
Kernel and local smoothing methods have provided sharp statistical guarantees for nonparamet-
ric regression under covariate shift. Ma et al. (2023) develop kernel ridge regression estimators that
attain nearly minimax rates under bounded or potentially unbounded importance weights. Wang
(2026) uses pseudo-labels on unlabeled target observations to select the regularization parameter
in kernel ridge regression, achieving nearly minimax rates while adapting to the target distribution
and covariate shift. For Nadaraya–Watson kernel regression, Pathak et al. (2022) characterize how
source–target overlap determines the minimax estimation rate. Wang et al. (2025) study kernel-
weighted local polynomial regression when the target covariates concentrate near a low-dimensional
manifold. Despite these advances, conventional kernel and local smoothing methods remain subject
to the curse of dimensionality over general smooth function classes, with convergence rates dete-
riorating as the number of covariates increases. This motivates methods that exploit additional
structure in high-dimensional regression functions.
Neural network methods can adapt to low-dimensional structure in high-dimensional nonpara-
metric regression problems. In the standard setting without covariate shift, Schmidt-Hieber (2020)
and Kohler and Langer (2021) establish nearly minimax rates for regression functions formed by
hierarchical compositions of simpler functions. These results provide a foundation for studying
whether the advantages of neural estimation can be retained under covariate shift.
Neural methods have also been developed for regression under covariate shift. Feng et al. (2024)
study deep quantile regression with estimated importance weights, while Xu et al. (2025) identify
conditions under which unweighted neural regression retains accurate target prediction. Kengne and
Mossa Ockegna (2026) develop penalized neural estimators that adapt to unknown smoothness and
accommodate dependent observations. Yamamoto and Wainwright (2026) incorporate unlabeled
target observations into the training objective and establish guarantees for sparse ReLU networks.
These studies focus on a single source. Optimal prediction rates and adaptive procedures for high-
dimensional nonparametric regression under multisource covariate shift remain largely unexplored
in the literature.
