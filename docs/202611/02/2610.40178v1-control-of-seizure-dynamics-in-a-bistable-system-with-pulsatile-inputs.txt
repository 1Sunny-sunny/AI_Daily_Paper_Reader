# Control of Seizure Dynamics in a Bistable System with Pulsatile Inputs 

Xianlong Zhao and Yuzhen Qin 

Abstract — Bistable neural population models describe tran-sitions associated with epilepsy through the coexistence of a stable equilibrium representing normal neural activity and a stable limit cycle representing seizure-like oscillations. Pertur-bations that drive the system outside the equilibrium’s basin of attraction can trigger transitions to seizure-like activity. Enlarging this basin enables the system to recover normal activity from a larger set of perturbed states, providing a model-based mechanism for reducing frequency of seizure initiation. Motivated by electrical brain stimulation, this paper investigates basin enlargement using pulsatile feedback control. Combining higher-order averaging with periodic near-identity transformations, we establish conditions under which nonzero-mean and zero-mean pulsatile feedback gains enlarge the basin of attraction and derive guidelines for pulse design. Numerical simulations validate the theoretical enlargement. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Brain stimulation offers a treatment option for patients with drug-resistant epilepsy who are unsuitable for resective surgery. Although stimulation can reduce seizure frequency, responses vary and many patients continue to experience seizures [1]. Stimulation parameters are often selected empir-ically, and therapeutic mechanisms remain incompletely un-derstood. These limitations motivate theoretical approaches that connect stimulation waveforms to neural dynamics and guide predictable control design. We investigate whether stimulation-like inputs can enlarge the healthy state’s basin of attraction in a bistable neural population model. Its stable equilibrium and stable limit cycle represent normal activity and seizure-like oscillations, respectively [2]. Perturbations can initiate seizure-like ac-tivity by driving the state outside the equilibrium’s basin. Local stability alone therefore does not quantify resilience to such transitions. Enlarging the basin enables recovery from a larger set of perturbed states, including states that would otherwise approach the seizure-like attractor, providing a model-based objective for seizure frequency reduction. 

Related work. Fractional-order approaches support model-based seizure regulation through network stabiliza-tion [3], [4] and predictive neurostimulation [5]. Passivity-based feedback has also been used to establish local stabi-lization and estimate the basin of attraction of the Epileptor neural mass model [6]. Our earlier work analytically characterizes the healthy-state basin of attraction and relates its size to seizure susceptibility [10]. Pinning-like feedback has further been developed to stabilize the non-ictal equilibrium in networks of bistable oscillators [11]. Contribution. This paper investigates enlargement of the healthy equilibrium’s basin of attraction using pulsatile feed-back that has non-zero and zero means. Unlike most existing studies that rely on local stability analysis, we combine first- and higher-order averaging with periodic near-identity transformations to approximate the basins of attraction of the controlled systems. We analytically show that the basins of attraction under control strictly contain the uncontrolled one, indicating that enlargement is established. The obtained results also provide a way to design pulse parameters to max-imize the enlargement under practical constraints. Numerical simulations confirm the theoretical enlargement. II. This paper shows that both pulsatile feedback control can enlarge the basin of attraction of the stable equilibrium in a bistable neural population model, improving resilience to perturbations that may trigger seizure-like oscillations. We employ high-order averaging techniques for the analysis. Analytical estimate of the basin boundaries enable pulse-parameter selection that maximizes enlargement under am-plitude constraints, while numerical simulations validate the theoretical results. These findings could provide a theoret-ical basis for designing pulsatile stimulation to strengthen the resilience of normal neural activity. Future work could extend the analysis to strictly zero-mean control inputs and coupled neural populations. Other directions include studying robustness to noise and parameter uncertainty.
