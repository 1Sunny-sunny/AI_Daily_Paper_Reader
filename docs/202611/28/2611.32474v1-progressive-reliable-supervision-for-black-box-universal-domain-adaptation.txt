UniDA) aims to adapt a target model to an unlabeled target
domain under arbitrary label-space relationships, while neither
source data nor source-model parameters are accessible during
adaptation. Under such restrictive information access, reliable
target supervision is unavailable, while predictions from the
black-box source model can be unreliable due to source bias and
domain shifts. To address this challenge, we propose Progressive
Reliable Supervision (PReS), which progressively establishes and
refines reliable target supervision by coordinating complementary
knowledge from the black-box source model, foundation models,
and the evolving target model. PReS first identifies reliable known
samples by exploiting semantic consistency under diffusion-based
perturbations and cross-model consensus between the black-
box source model and CLIP. It then estimates the sample-
wise reliability of the two predictors and adaptively integrates
their complementary predictions to construct reliable pseudo
labels. The resulting supervision is exploited asymmetrically to
encourage confident and discriminative predictions for reliable
known samples while maintaining uncertain yet locally consistent
predictions for potential unknown samples. As adaptation pro-
ceeds, the emerging target-domain knowledge is further leveraged
to refine both the pseudo labels and the known/unknown sample
partition, forming a closed-loop process in which supervision
construction and target adaptation mutually reinforce each other.
Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets under
diverse label-space relationships demonstrate that PReS achieves
state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance under the
restrictive B2-UniDA setting.

I. INTRODUCTION
U
NSUPERVISED domain adaptation (UDA) [1]–[3] aims
to transfer knowledge from a labeled source domain to an
unlabeled target domain under distribution shifts. Conventional
UDA typically assumes an identical label space between
the two domains, i.e., Ys = Yt, corresponding to closed-
set domain adaptation (CDA) [4]–[6]. In real-world appli-
cations, however, diverse category shifts give rise to partial
domain adaptation (PDA) [7]–[10], open-set domain adapta-
tion (OSDA) [11]–[13], and open-partial domain adaptation
(OPDA) [14], [15]. Universal domain adaptation (UniDA) [16]
unifies these settings by only assuming the existence of shared
classes between the source and target domains. Nevertheless,

UniDA requires access to labeled source data during adap-
tation, which may be infeasible due to privacy, security, and
transmission constraints.
Source-free universal domain adaptation (SF-UniDA) [17]–
[19] relaxes this requirement by performing adaptation with
a pre-trained source model without accessing source data.
However, exposing the source model may still raise privacy
and intellectual-property concerns. Black-box universal do-
main adaptation (B2-UniDA) [20] further restricts source-
side information by allowing access to neither source data
nor source-model parameters; only prediction outputs can be
obtained by querying the black-box source model. Together
with unknown label-space relationships, these restrictions lead
to a fundamental challenge: reliable target supervision is
unavailable during adaptation.
Existing B2-UniDA methods primarily derive target super-
vision from black-box source-model predictions [20]–[22].
However, these predictions suffer from source bias under
domain shifts and are particularly unreliable for target-private
samples absent from the source label space. Directly using
them for pseudo-labeling or known/unknown separation can
therefore propagate erroneous supervision. Although existing
methods employ confidence-based sample selection, target
label-space estimation, or specialized adaptation criteria, effec-
tive B2-UniDA still requires constructing trustworthy supervi-
sion from limited and imperfect information and progressively
improving it during adaptation.
Foundation models [23] offer complementary knowledge
for addressing this challenge. Vision-language models such as
CLIP [24] provide generalizable semantic knowledge, while
diffusion models [25], [26] provide generative priors for pro-
ducing semantically consistent visual variations. This yields a
useful complementarity: the black-box source model provides
task-specific but source-biased knowledge, foundation models
provide generalizable but task-agnostic priors, and the target
model progressively acquires target-domain knowledge during
adaptation. Coordinating these knowledge sources therefore
provides a promising way to construct and progressively refine
reliable target supervision.
Motivated by this insight, we propose Progressive Reliable
Supervision (PReS), which progressively constructs and refines
reliable target supervision by coordinating complementary
knowledge throughout adaptation. PReS first identifies reliable
known samples by accumulating semantic-consistency and
cross-model-consensus evidence, and then constructs their
pseudo labels by adaptively integrating the black-box source-
model and CLIP predictions according to sample-wise relia-
bility. The resulting supervision enables asymmetric learning

II. RELATED WORK
A. Universal Domain Adaptation
Universal domain adaptation (UniDA) addresses domain
adaptation without assuming a specific relationship between
the source and target label spaces. UAN [16] estimates sample
transferability through instance-level weighting, while subse-
quent studies focus on known/unknown separation and target
representation learning. DANCE [27] exploits neighborhood
clustering to promote discriminative target representations, and
OVANet [28] performs unknown detection through one-vs-all
classifiers. More recently, CCCOP [29] incorporates cross-
domain category context optimization and learnable textual
prompts for category-shift adaptation. Despite their effective-
ness, these methods require access to labeled source data
during adaptation.
B. Source-Free Universal Domain Adaptation
Recent SFDA studies have explored various aspects of
target adaptation, including high-order target structural rela-
tions through hypergraph learning [30] and adversarial robust-
ness under source-free constraints [31]. Source-free univer-
sal domain adaptation (SF-UniDA) [17], [32], [33] performs
adaptation using a pre-trained source model and unlabeled
target data without accessing source data. UMAD [32] ad-
dresses source-free adaptation under universal category shifts,
while LEAD [18] exploits singular-value decomposition to
characterize transferable and domain-specific components for
known/unknown separation. GLC++ [19] exploits target neigh-
borhood structure through contrastive affinity learning, and
EGRS [34] estimates embedding shifts through perturbations
and progressively refines target prototypes. Nevertheless, these
methods still require access to the parameters and internal rep-
resentations of the source model, whereas B2-UniDA exposes
only its prediction interface.
C. Black-Box Universal Domain Adaptation
Black-box universal domain adaptation (B2-UniDA) [20]
allows access to neither source data nor source-model param-
eters during adaptation. UB2DA [20] decomposes adaptation
into intra-class discrimination and out-of-class detection and
identifies potential unknown samples according to prediction
uncertainty. ADU [22] modulates supervision according to
black-box prediction confidence, although it mainly considers
scenarios involving target-private classes. GSS [21] develops
a sample-selection strategy for known/unknown separation
without a manually predefined threshold. Since these meth-
ods primarily derive task-specific supervision from black-box
predictions, reliable target supervision remains difficult to
establish when such predictions are corrupted by domain shifts
or target-private samples.
Different from these approaches, PReS coordinates comple-
mentary knowledge from the black-box source model, foun-
dation models, and the evolving target model to progressively
construct and refine reliable supervision throughout adaptation.
D. Foundation Models for Domain Adaptation
Foundation models [23] pretrained on large-scale data pro-
vide rich prior knowledge for downstream visual tasks. Vision-
language models such as CLIP [24] learn transferable visual-
semantic representations from large-scale image-text data,
while subsequent models such as BLIP [35], Flamingo [36],
and FLAVA [37] further demonstrate the generalization capa-
bilities of multimodal pretraining. Recent studies have further
exploited such vision-language priors for domain adaptation,
e.g., by coordinating textual and visual prompt tuning for
domain alignment [38] or progressively integrating teacher and
student predictions to refine target pseudo-labels [39]. Such
semantic priors complement the task-specific but potentially
source-biased predictions of a black-box source model.
Diffusion models [25] provide another form of external
prior through their strong generative capabilities. Stable Diffu-
sion [26], in particular, can generate diverse visual variations
while preserving high-level semantic content. PReS exploits
these complementary priors for reliable supervision construc-
tion: CLIP provides semantic knowledge for cross-model
consensus and reliability-aware pseudo-label generation, while
Stable Diffusion provides semantic-preserving perturbations
for evaluating prediction consistency.

B. Method Overview
The overall framework of our proposed Progressive Re-
liable Supervision (PReS) is illustrated in Fig. 1. Under
the B2-UniDA setting, reliable target supervision is unavail-
able, while the accessible knowledge sources exhibit com-
plementary strengths and limitations. The black-box source
model provides task-specific but source-biased knowledge,
foundation models offer generalizable but task-agnostic priors,
and the target model can progressively acquire target-domain
knowledge but is unreliable at the beginning of adaptation.
PReS coordinates these complementary knowledge sources to
progressively establish reliable supervision during adaptation.
Our framework consists of four stages. First, Reliable
Known Sample Discovery identifies reliable known samples
through diffusion-based semantic consistency and cross-model
consensus. Second, Reliability-aware Pseudo-label Generation
adaptively integrates the black-box source-model and CLIP
predictions according to their sample-wise reliability. Third,
Asymmetric Target Learning exploits the resulting supervision
according to the distinct roles of reliable known and potential
unknown samples. Finally, Progressive Supervision Refinement
leverages the emerging target-domain knowledge to refine both
pseudo labels and the known/unknown sample partition.
These stages form a closed-loop adaptation process: com-
plementary external knowledge first establishes reliable super-
vision to facilitate target adaptation, while the emerging target
knowledge is subsequently fed back to refine the supervision
itself. Consequently, supervision construction and target adap-
tation mutually reinforce each other throughout training.
C. Reliable Known Sample Discovery (Stage 1)
Under the B2-UniDA setting, predictions from the black-
box source model are inevitably affected by domain shift and
therefore cannot be directly adopted as reliable supervision.
We identify reliable known samples by progressively accu-
mulating two complementary sources of reliability evidence.
Specifically, reliable known samples are expected to exhibit
stable predictions under semantic-preserving perturbations and
consistent semantic understanding across complementary vi-
sion models. Accordingly, we first construct semantic consis-
tency evidence through Diffusion-based Semantic Consistency
Verification and then further validate candidate known samples

F. Progressive Supervision Refinement (PSR, Stage 4)
The supervision constructed in the preceding stages is ini-
tially derived from the black-box source model and foundation
models and therefore remains imperfect under domain shift. As
adaptation proceeds, the target model progressively acquires
target-domain knowledge, making its predictions increasingly
informative for refining the supervision. Accordingly, after an
initial adaptation period of t epochs, we exploit the emerging
target knowledge to progressively refine both the pseudo labels
and the known/unknown sample partition.

porate target-domain knowledge into label-level supervision,
we update the pseudo label ¯yi using the target prediction ˆy′

pseudo labels from previous epochs. Thus, every sample in
the updated known set is equipped with label-level supervision
before subsequent target-model optimization.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Setup
Datasets and Adaptation Scenarios: We evaluate PReS
on four widely used domain adaptation benchmarks: Office-
31 [48], Office-Home [49], VisDA [50], and DomainNet [51].
Office-31 contains 4,652 images from 31 categories across
Amazon (A), Webcam (W), and DSLR (D), forming six
bidirectional adaptation tasks. Office-Home contains approx-
imately 15,500 images from 65 categories across Art (Ar),
Clipart (Cl), Product (Pr), and Real-World (Rw), forming
12 adaptation tasks. VisDA contains over 152,000 synthetic
and 55,000 real images from 12 categories, with Synthetic
(S) →Real (R) as the adaptation task. DomainNet contains
approximately 600,000 images from 345 categories across six
domains; following previous studies, we use Painting (P), Real
(R), and Sketch (S) for evaluation.
We consider four adaptation scenarios with different source-
target label-space relationships: open-partial domain adapta-
tion (OPDA), open-set domain adaptation (OSDA), partial
domain adaptation (PDA), and closed-set domain adaptation
(CDA). Table I summarizes the corresponding class splits.

UniDA methods include UAN [16], DANCE [27], DCC [46],
OVANet [28], and CCCOP [29]; the SF-UniDA methods in-
clude UMAD [32], LEAD [18], GLC++ [19], EGRS [34], and
ESBN [47]; and the B2-UniDA methods include UB2DA [20],
ADU [22], and GSS [21]. The reported results are collected
from the original publications or, when applicable, from
LEAD [18], GLC++ [19], and ADU [22]. Note that ADU
considers only OSDA and OPDA rather than the general B2-
UniDA setting. Since existing B2-UniDA studies generally do
not report reproducible PDA and CDA results, we additionally
use Source-Only (SO), which directly applies the pretrained
source model to target samples without adaptation, as the B2-
UniDA baseline for these settings.
Implementation Details : All experiments are implemented
in PyTorch [53]. Following previous B2-UniDA studies [21],
we use an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 [54], [55] as the
backbone. The source model is trained with cross-entropy loss
and subsequently accessed only through black-box queries
during adaptation. Both source-model training and target-
model adaptation use SGD with a learning rate of 10−2,
momentum of 0.9, weight decay of 5×10−4, and batch size of
64. We adopt CLIP ViT-B/32 and Stable Diffusion v1.5 [26] as
the foundation models. The refinement starting epoch t is set to
5. The hyperparameters β and γ in Eq. (14) are set to 0.3 and
0.1, respectively, except for DomainNet, where they are set to
0.025 and 0.01. We set ϵ = 10−6 in Eq. (6) and the uncertainty
threshold ω = 0.55 in Eq. (18), following GLC [45].

2) Ablation on Reliability-aware Pseudo-label Generation:
We compare PReS with three alternative pseudo-label genera-
tion strategies: using only the black-box source predictions
(only Src), using only CLIP predictions (only CLIP), and
equally averaging the two predictions (average). As shown
in Fig. 2, the two predictors exhibit complementary strengths
across transfer tasks, while simple averaging improves the
average H-score to 77.2%, compared with 77.0% and 76.5%
for only Src and only CLIP, respectively. By further accounting
for their sample-wise reliability, PReS achieves the highest
average H-score of 77.6%. These results validate the comple-
mentarity of the two knowledge sources and the effectiveness
of reliability-aware fusion.
3) Ablation on Asymmetric Target Learning: To evaluate
the learning objectives in Asymmetric Target Learning, we
individually remove each loss term under OPDA. As shown
in Table VIII, removing Lkd causes the largest performance
degradation, confirming the importance of transferring the
constructed label-level supervision to the target model. Re-
moving Lim reduces the H-score by 13.8, 10.3, and 6.0 points
on Office-31, Office-Home, and VisDA, respectively, demon-
strating the benefit of jointly promoting prediction confidence
and class diversity. Both Lem and Lmix also contribute to
unknown-sample learning, with the latter yielding a particu-
larly large gain of 12.5 points on VisDA. Overall, these results
support the asymmetric learning principle of encouraging
confident and discriminative predictions for reliable known
samples while maintaining uncertain yet locally consistent
predictions for potential unknown samples.
4) Ablation on Progressive Supervision Refinement: We
evaluate Pseudo-label Refinement (PLR) and Sample Partition
Refinement (SPR) using three variants: w/o SPR, w/o PLR, and
w/o PSR, where the last disables both refinement mechanisms.
As shown in Fig. 3, enabling PLR alone improves the average
H-score from 75.8% to 76.1%, while enabling SPR alone
improves it to 77.3%. Jointly employing PLR and SPR further
increases the H-score to 77.6%. These results demonstrate
that both mechanisms contribute to progressive supervision
refinement and are complementary when used jointly.
D. Visualization
1) Feature Visualization: Fig. 4 presents t-SNE [56] visu-

model and PReS under OPDA. Compared with SO, PReS
produces more structured feature distributions, with shared-
class samples forming more compact clusters and being more
clearly separated from target-private samples. This consistent
pattern across both transfer tasks indicates that PReS learns
more discriminative target representations under domain and
category shifts.
2) Discriminative Region Visualization: Fig. 5 presents
Grad-CAM [57] visualizations of PReS and three comparison
methods on VisDA target samples. Compared with the other
methods, PReS generally exhibits more concentrated activation
on object-relevant regions and less attention to irrelevant back-
grounds, indicating that the adapted target model relies more
strongly on semantically relevant visual cues for classification.
E. Parameter Sensitivity
PReS introduces β and γ in Eq. (14) to balance Lem and
Lmix, respectively. We vary β from 0.15 to 0.40 and γ from
0.02 to 0.18 on Office-31 under OPDA, while fixing the other
hyperparameter to its default value. As shown in Fig. 6, de-
spite task-wise variations, the average H-score remains stable,
ranging from 90.6% to 91.2% for β and from 90.8% to 91.2%
for γ. We therefore set β = 0.3 and γ = 0.1 by default.
We further evaluate the refinement starting epoch t, which
determines when target-domain knowledge begins to partici-
pate in Progressive Supervision Refinement. With 15 training
epochs, we vary t over {1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13}. As shown in
Fig. 7, the average H-score varies only from 91.0% to 91.2%,
demonstrating low sensitivity to the refinement starting point.
We set t = 5 by default.

target-model optimization, PReS introduces linear overhead in
Nt, except for the O(NtC log C) Gini reliability computation.
V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we proposed Progressive Reliable Supervi-
sion (PReS) for B2-UniDA to address the lack of reliable
target supervision under restricted source-side access and
unknown label-space relationships. PReS coordinates comple-
mentary knowledge from the black-box source model, foun-


11
dation models, and the evolving target model to progressively
construct and refine reliable target supervision. Specifically,
reliable sample- and label-level supervision is first estab-
lished from complementary external knowledge and exploited
through asymmetric target learning. The emerging target-
domain knowledge is then fed back to refine both pseudo
labels and the known/unknown sample partition, forming a
closed-loop adaptation process. Extensive experiments across
multiple benchmarks and diverse label-space relationships
demonstrate the effectiveness of PReS under the restrictive
B2-UniDA setting.
