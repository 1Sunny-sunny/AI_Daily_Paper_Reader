## 摘要
经验可以引导模糊选择，或在当前证据强烈时仍使选择产生偏倚。我们探究丘脑皮层回路如何将历史内容向前传递，并调控其在连续决策中的影响。在已发表的成年雄性小鼠数据中，当感觉历史与刺激相冲突时，抑制后顶叶皮层（PPC）或枕核（PUL）对当前判断的改善最为明显，这反映了历史权重的降低。在训练好的丘脑皮层回路模型中，将记录的PUL输入重放至PPC，并在不同历史条件下均衡完整接收状态后，重建了一条历史轨迹。重放相反历史会重定向后续选择，即便该回路已正确判断了中间强刺激。这一历史信息占据丘脑输出中的一个低维方向，该方向在放电率界限下仍可被广泛到达；在匹配能量下重新分配到达电流表明，除总输入强度外，精细的空间组织选择性地塑造后续历史使用，而不干扰当前选择。强感觉证据抑制历史输出，同时保留内部状态差异；而模糊性则调用神经元整流阈值和读出对齐来表达历史。皮层工作点的微小偏移调节后续历史影响，对循环驱动的部分衰减则在所有回路中降低该影响，同时保留当前承诺；相应地，循环动力学对于预测留出历史中的刺激后轨迹至关重要。总之，这些发现区分了历史引导选择中的三种操作：返回的丘脑皮层输入决定重建哪段过去，循环动力学将其影响经过中间需求向前传递，而当前感觉证据则门控其进入决策的强度。

## Abstract
Experience can guide an ambiguous choice or bias a choice despite strong current evidence. We asked how a thalamocortical circuit carries historical content forward and regulates its influence across successive decisions. In published data from adult male mice, suppressing posterior parietal cortex (PPC) or pulvinar (PUL) improved current judgments most when sensory history conflicted with the stimulus, reflecting reduced historical weighting. In trained thalamocortical circuit models, replaying recorded PUL input to PPC reconstructed a historical trace after equalizing complete recipient states across histories. Replaying the opposite history redirected the later choice, even after the circuit correctly judged an intervening strong stimulus. This historical message occupied a low-dimensional direction in thalamic output that remained broadly reachable under firing-rate bounds; redistributing the arriving current at matched energy showed that fine spatial organization, beyond total input strength, selectively shapes later history use without disrupting current choices. Strong sensory evidence suppressed historical output while preserving internal state differences, whereas ambiguity recruited neuronal rectification thresholds and readout alignment to express history. Small shifts in the cortical operating point modulated later historical influence, and partial attenuation of recurrent drive reduced it across all circuits while preserving current commitments; correspondingly, recurrent dynamics were essential for predicting post-stimulus trajectories in held-out histories. Together, these findings distinguish three operations in history-guided choice: returning thalamocortical input determines which past is reconstructed, recurrent dynamics carry its influence forward across intervening demands, and current sensory evidence gates how strongly it enters the decision.
