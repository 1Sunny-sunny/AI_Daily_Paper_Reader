Abstract 12 

On the basis of  verbal instructions, humans can accomplish novel and diverse demands  on 13 

the very first try. This complex phenomenon recruits structured brain activity across the 14 

frontoparietal Multiple Demand Network (MDN), which is thought to encode upcoming task 15 

parameters and guide behavior.  However , it is uncertain how novel instructions are 16 

translated into efficient neural task representations.  To address this , we collected functional 17 

magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data while participants followed a rich set of novel verbal 18 

instructions. These varied along three core dimensions: the overarching task demand (to 19 

select or to integrate stimuli information), the relevant target category (animate or inanimate 20 

items), and the visual feature that participants responded to (color or shape). We  used 21 

multivariate pattern  analysis (MVPA)  to  examine whether and how each of these dimensions 22 

was reflected  in MDN  activity. We contrasted two alternative representational geometries 23 

that may underpin novel task coding: low -dimensional spaces based on abstract and 24 

generalizable representations, and high -dimensional architectures hosting context -unique, 25 

conjunctive neural codes. Our results  showed  that anticipatory MDN activity was sensitive 26 

to the content of instructions. While selection vs. integration task demands were broadly 27 

encoded  across the MDN , coding of the relevant categories and features  was restricted to 28 

lateral MDN regions, namely, the intraparietal sulcus and the inferior frontal  junction. 29 

Critically, the  representational  spaces across the MDN displayed a mixt ure of geometrical 30 

motifs , partially supporting our two alternative hypotheses.  On the one hand,  Cross -31 

Condition  Generalization  Performance revealed the presence of abstract and transferable 32 

neural codes for  task  demand information.  On the other  hand , Shattering Dimensionality 33 

showed complex, high dimensional coding spaces across the MDN, structured around both 34 

task -informative and non -informative axes.  Despite this , no evidence of conjunctive neural 35 

codes was observed. Overall, these findings highlight that novel instructed behavior may 36 

re cruit both abstraction and high dimensionality to  promote generalization while maximizing 37 

the expressivity of MDN coding spaces. More broadly, they  emphasize  the  importance of 38 

considering  encoding geometry for a computational understanding of cognitive control 39 

processes. 40

conjunctive coding, fMRI, multivariate analyses, attention 42

Introduction 44 

Compared with other animal species, humans show remarkable success in navigating new 45 

and versatile cognitive task environments. This adaptability is tightly linked  to our 46 

employment of verbal instructions to acquire complex task procedures (Brass et al., 2017; 47 

Cole, Laurent, et al., 2013). Past research has linked this phenomenon to frontoparietal 48 

regions that assemble models or  representations  of the instructed demands (e.g., González -49 

García et al., 2021; Muhle -Karbe et al., 2017; Palenciano et al., 2019a ; Woolgar et al., 2015; 50 

Zheng et al., 2024). Building on recent research unraveling the geometry of neural 51 

representation (Badre et al., 2021), this work aims to advance our knowledge of the 52 

principles governing task coding in novel scenarios. 53 

To effectively translate new instructions into actions, we must encode and assemble all the 54 

task parameters into a compact representation able to guide upcoming behavior (Brass et 55 

al., 2017; Cole, Laurent, et al., 2013). This process, based on cognitive control mechanisms 56 

(Norman & Shallice, 1986), has been linked to preparatory brain activity across a set of 57 

prefrontal and parietal regions (Cole et al., 2010, Hartstra et al., 2011, Palenciano et al., 58 

2019b) that belong to the Multiple Demand Network (MDN;  Assem et al., 2020, Duncan, 59 

2010; Fedorenko et al., 2013). Using multivariate pattern analyses (MVPA), sensitive to fine -60 

grained information coded in distributed brain activity (e.g., Haxby et al., 2014; Haynes & 61 

Rees, 2006), recent research has started to characterize the nature of novel task 62 

representations. These results have shown that different dimensions of the instructed task 63 

are encoded in advance (i.e., before stimulus presentation) w ithin the MDN, such as 64 

targeted stimulus categories (González -García et al., 2017; Muhle -Karbe et al., 2017), 65 

relevant action plans (Palenciano et al., 2019a, 2024), or rules binding both (Cole et al., 66 

2011; Cole, Reynolds, et al., 2013; González -García et al., 2021). The strength of these 67 

neural codes is further modulated by overarching contextual demands  (González -García et 68 

al., 2021; Sobrado et al., 2022) and impacts behavioral indexes (Cole et al., 2016; González -69 

García et al., 2021), emphasizing their role in guiding novel performance. Nonetheless, the 70 

computations that enable the fast assembly of neural task representations across the MDN 71 

remain largely unknown. 72 

Recent models of cognitive control have stressed the role of the representational geometry 73 

for goal -oriented information coding (Badre et al., 2021; Freund et al., 2021; Musslick  &74 

Cohen, 2021). This term refers to the structure or organization that shapes how a set of 75 

features (e.g., task parameters) are encoded in a brain region’s distributed activity 76

(Kriegeskorte & Kievit, 2013). Current proposals state that coding architectures at the 77 

service of cognitive control are fine -tuned to current demands (Badre et al., 2021; Bhandari 78 

et al., 2024; Musslick & Cohen, 2021), with two opposite geometrical m otifs potentially 79 

supporting flexible task coding. On one hand, MDN regions can implement compressed 80 

representational geometries organized around a low number of task -relevant dimensions 81 

while abstracting over irrelevant information (Bernardi et al., 2020; Brincat et al., 2018). This 82 

mechanism leads to abstract and common neural codes that can easily generalize and be 83 

recombined across task contexts, a principle also known as compositional coding 84 

(Frankland & Greene, 2020; Reverberi et al., 2012; Tafazoli  et al., 2026). On the other hand, 85 

some of these frontoparietal areas also display expansive, high -dimensional coding 86 

architectures, which can host segregated neural codes for each task instance (Fusi et al., 87 

2016; Kikumoto et al., 2024; Kikumoto, Mayr, et al., 2022a, 2022b; Kikumoto, Sameshima, 88 

et al., 2022; Kikumoto & Mayr, 2020a; Rigotti et al., 2013). These geometries are thought  to 89 

rely on conjunctive task coding (Hommel, 2009), which non -linearly binds all the relevant 90 

task parameters into unique activity patterns (Rigotti et al., 2013). This coding mechanism 91 

also contributes to flexible behavior by maximizing the expressivity of the encoding spaces, 92 

i.e., the number of tasks that can be differentially represented (Badre et al., 20 21). 93 

Nonetheless, from a theoretical perspective, both mechanisms constitute suitable solutions 94 

for the computational challenges imposed by novel tasks, which could be coded either by 95 

abstracting and re -using a small set of common task dimensions (Cole, Laurent, et al., 2013; 96 

Cole, Reynolds, et al., 2013) or by exploiting high -dimensional coding spaces that can host 97 

a vast diversity of segregated neural task codes (Rigotti et al., 2013). 98 

The scarce evidence to date appears to support the abstraction -based account. Instruction -99 

driven representational geometries found in MDN regions can be decomposed into a few 100 

task dimensions (Cole, Reynolds, et al., 2013; Palenciano et al., 2019a) that generalize 101 

across contexts (Cole et al., 2011; Sobrado et al., 2022).  Moreover, compositional 102 

signatures seem to be  particularly relevant during the first encounters with new tasks, since 103 

experience promotes a shift toward context -specific neural patterns (Mill et al., 2025). 104 

However, the degree of abstraction might depend on the type of information being encoded. 105 

Past experiments show that generalization seems predominantly tied  to higher -level task 106 

dimensions rather than lower -level features, both in novel (Pena et al., 2025) and practiced 107 

task settings (Shashidhara et al., 2024). Studies manipulating sensory modalities have 108 

further suggested that stimulus -response rule codes may not generalise between auditory 109

unexplored how new task environments recruit the two geometrical features predicted by 111 

the above -mentioned models: the abstraction of the neural codes carrying task information, 112 

and the dimensionality of the underlying encoding space. Hence, a direct comparison 113 

between compositional and conjunctive novel task representation is still lacking. 114 

To fill this gap, we employed fMRI while participants followed a rich set of novel verbal 115 

instructions. These sought to impact task processing at different hierarchical levels, 116 

manipulating the more abstract and overarching task demand (either to select a target while 117 

ignoring the other or to integrate the two targets ), the allocation of attention to specific 118 

stimulus categories (either animate or inanimate targets) and the more concrete features 119 

that participants had to respond to (either the color or the shape of the frame surrounding 120 

the target).  First, we used MVPA classifiers to characterize the informational content that 121 

structured activity patterns across different regions from the MDN. Second, we characterized 122 

the instruction -driven encoding spaces, focusing on two core features: abstraction and 123 

dimensionality. Lastly, motivated by recent results in the field, we examined the presence of 124 

conjunctive task representations through Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA). By 125 

doing so, we aimed to juxtapose  the two main coding architectures identified in the cognitive 126 

control literature: whether novel tasks induce low -dimensional and abstract coding spaces 127 

or, alternatively, high -dimensional spaces conformed by context -specific activity patterns. 128 

These two architectures are often presented as alternatives but, as our data also show, may 129 

not necessarily be mutually exclusive. 130

2.2.  Apparatus, stimuli, and protocol 145 

Each trial of our paradigm entailed a unique verbal instruction stating an “ If … then … ”146 

conditional that referred to features of an upcoming pair of stimuli. All our instructions were 147 

in Spanish, our participants’ native language. The instructions were created  by manipulating 148 

three task components that aimed to affect different information processing levels. First, the 149 

instructions indicated the trial’s overarching demand: either to select one stimulus while 150 

ignoring the other, or to integrate information from both. Second, they indicated the identities 151 

of the stimuli that should be attended to , which could be either a pair of animate (land 152 

animals and sea animals) or inanimate (tools and musical instruments) items. All instructions 153 

referred to either animate or inanimate targets, including a stimulus from each subcategory 154 

(e.g., in animate trials, a land and a sea animal were always mentioned). Third, we specified 155 

the relevant stimulus feature that participants should respond to, being either the shape or 156 

the color of the frame(s) that surrounded the target stimulus/stimuli. In selection tria ls, only 157 

the frame from the cued stimulus should be attended to, while in integration trials, both 158 

stimuli frames should be processed. The full set of instructions was the result of orthogonally 159 

crossing task demand (selection, integration), stimulus category (animate, inanimate), and 160 

relevant feature (color, shape), yielding eight experimental conditions.  For instance, the 161 

instruction “ Atiende al martillo y a la guitarra.  Si están rodeados por un rombo, pulsa L. Si 162 

no, pulsa A”,  in English: “ Pay attention to both the hammer and the guitar. If they are both 163 

surrounded by a rhombus, press L  (right finger) . Otherwise, press A  (left finger)” belonged 164 

to the [integration, inanimate, shape] condition (see  Fig. 1  for more example instructions). 165

experimental conditions. Specifically, we manipulated the identity of the target stimuli (with 168

stimuli and task demands (e.g., in the pairing [cat, jellyfi sh], both stimuli were equally 172

2.3.  Experimental design and behavioral analyses 241 

Our paradigm followed a within -subject design, with three factors of interest: (1) task demand 242 

(to select or to integrate probe information), (2) target category (animate and inanimate 243 

probes), and (3) relevant feature to respond to (shape or color of the frame). These three 244 

variables were fully crossed, resulting in eight experimental conditions. Overall, we collected 245 

32  observations per cell of this design (four per fMRI run). 246 

For the behavioral analyses, we discarded data from catch trials and those with RT above 247 

and below 2 standard deviations from the participant’s mean. For the reaction times (RT) 248 

analysis, we further excluded error trials. Then, we addressed the effect of our three factors 249 

on accuracy rate and RT in two independent repeated measures ANOVAs (rmANOVAs). 250 

2.4.  MRI data acquisition and preprocessing 251 

fMRI data was acquired with a 3T Siemens Magnetom  Prisma Fit scanner located at the 252 

Mind, Brain, and Behavior Research Center (University of Granada, Spain), using a 64 -253 

channel head coil. The following sequences were acquired, in order: a T2 -weighted 254 

anatomical image, a phase and magnitude field map images, and nine T2* EPI functional 255

deidentified (i.e., defaced) the T1 anatomical images, co -registered them to the functional 267 

volumes, and  carried out a segmentation. To avoid distorting the fine -grained multivoxel 268

2.5.  fMRI data analyses 274 

We estimated a series of first -level GLMs on each participant’s data. These included 275 

regressors covering the trials’ instruction, preparation and target stages, separately for the 276 

eight experimental conditions (i.e., combinations of Task Demand, Target Category and 277 

Relevant feature). All regressors were modeled using a  stick function (i.e., 0s duration) 278 

convoluted with the SPM’s canonical  hemodynamic response function (HRF). An 279 

assessment of the collinearity among the three task events regressors using Pearson’s 280 

correlation confirmed that the GLM’s coefficients were estimable ( rInstruction -Preparation  = 0.70, 281 

rInstruction -Target  = 0.13,  rPreparation -Target  = 0.52). Additional nuisance regressors included error trials 282 

(locked to the instruction onset and covering the full trial duration) and the six movement 283 

parameters computed during the realignment. The estimation followed a traditional LSU 284 

(Least -Squares Unitary) approach (i.e., beta per run). These GLMs were first estimated with 285 

smoothed and normalized volumes to identify areas univariately engaged by our 286 

experimental paradigm. This was done with one -sample  t-tests that contrasted each trial 287 

event against the implicit baseline. Then, we estimated the GLMs using unnormalized and 288 

unsmoothed images to perform the multivariate pattern analyses. This provided a beta 289 

weight brain map per event, condition, and MRI run. Finally, we also contrasted these 290 

images against the implicit baseline (using one -sample  t-tests), obtaining brain -wise  T maps 291 

where the noise was normalized in a univariate fashion  (Misaki et al., 2010) . The se were 292 

used for  the main multivariate analyses. 293 

In addition, we estimated a series of trial -wise GLM models to obtain separate  T maps for 294 

each instruction employed in our task. This allowed us to explore finer -grained information 295 

provided by the instruction pool (see section 2.5.5). To reduce collinearity, we employed an 296 

LSS (Least -Squares Separate) approach  (Arco et al., 2018; Mumford et al., 2012, 2014), 297 

computing an individual GLM per trial with the following regressors: (1) the preparation 298 

interval of the targeted trial, (2) the preparation interval from the remaining trials, (3) the 299 

instruction stage from all trials, and (4) the probe stage from all trials. Additional nuisance 300 

regressors also modeled error trials and the six movement parameters. Only unnormalized 301

2.5.2. Decoding analysis 318 

First, machine learning classifiers were used  to detect information coded in MDN during 319 

novel instruction preparation. Focusing on the activity patterns  from  the  preparation  inter val, 320 

we  applied standard decoding analyses to classify th em  according to the three task 321 

components manipulated  in  the instructions: the relevant task demand, the targeted stimulus 322 

category , and the feature to respond to. To do so, linear Support Vector Machine (SVM) 323 

algorithms were trained and tested to  classify the two levels of each variable (i.e., selection 324 

vs. integration, animate vs. inanimate, shape vs. color, respectively) using data from each 325 

ROI  (Fig. 2B ).  The ROIs’ activity patterns consisted of  T-value vectors obtained from the 326

2.5.4. Shattering Dimensionality 407 

We also aimed to estimate the dimensionality of the overarching representational space of 408 

each MDN ROI. To do so, we computed the Shattering Dimensionality (SD), a measurement 409 

based on binary classifiers that approximates the minimum number of axes structuring a 410 

region’s distributed activity (Bernardi et al., 2020; Rigotti et al., 2013). This minimum 411 

dimensionality is identified, from this approach, with the maximum number of significant, 412 

above -chance binary classifications that can be performed in a given  dataset  (Fig. 2D ).413 

Hence, this index informs on whether the MDN ROIs display low -dimensional coding spaces 414 

constrained by the three task dimensions manipulated or show, instead, more expansive 415 

high -dimensional structures. 416 

To compute the SD, we first grouped the eight experimental conditions into all possible 417 

balanced dichotomies of four vs. four conditions, regardless of their interpretability (Bernardi 418 

et al., 2020; Rigotti et al., 2013). Our design provided an overall of 35 dichotomies (i.e.,  (8 4)419 

= 70,  which divided by 2 to omit duplicates led to the 35 dichotomies), with three of them 420 

corresponding to the manipulated task components and the remaining 32 being non -421 

informative regarding our paradigm. With this method, 35 was the dimensionality ceiling that 422 

could be estimated. Separately for each ROI, we trained and tested linear cross -validated 423 

classifiers to decode each one of these dichotomies. In our results, we reported the total 424 

number of significant above -chance classifications per ROI as the SD -est imated 425 

dimensionality. 426 

Last, to explore in greater detail the nature of the coding dimensions identified through the 427 

SD protocol, we additionally computed the CCGP score of the full set of 35 dichotomies. To 428 

do so, we extended the procedure described in the previous section to the non -informative 429 

binary dichotomies identified in the SD analysis (see Bernardi et al., 2020 for a similar 430 

approach). 431 

2.5.5. Model -based Representational Similarity Analysis 432 

The analyses described so far treat each task component independently, leaving 433 

unaddressed the relationship among them during their assembly into coherent control 434 

representations. In this regard, recent studies have emphasized that brain responses to 435 

different task parameters are non -linearly combined into conjunctive neural codes  (Kikumoto 436 

et al., 2024; Kikumoto, Mayr, et al., 2022; Kikumoto, Sameshima, et al., 2022; Kikumoto & 437 

Mayr, 2020) . Following this research, we  employed model -based Representational Similarity 438

Analysis (RSA) to  address whether the three instructed task components were jointly coded 439 

in such a conjunctive fashion. 440 

RSA ( Fig. 2E ) estimates the ROIs’ encoding space by computing the similarity between 441 

activity patterns from different experimental conditions, conveying these measurements into 442 

representational similarity matrices (RSM). We employed  the cross -validated Pearson 443 

coefficient as a similarity metric, a robust index that is insensitive to differences in signal 444 

mean or variance between conditions, avoids positive biases, and provides interpretable 445 

values  (Ritchie et al., 2021; Walther et al., 2016) . This measurement was computed  with a 446 

leave -one -run -out cross -validation procedure. Specifically, in each iteration we averaged the 447 

condition -wise activity patterns from seven out of the eight fMRI runs, providing a single 448 

mean pattern per condition, which was Pearson -correlated with the left -out run’s activity 449 

patterns. That provided eight -by -eight RSMs capturing the correlation between all the 450 

pairwise combinations of our eight experimental conditions. The cross -validation led to eight 451 

RSMs, which were then averaged  into a single matrix per ROI. These cross -validated RSMs 452 

were non -symmetrical, and their diagonal contained interpretable values, allowing us to 453 

include the full matrices in the analyses. 454 

Next, we decomposed the neural RSMs using a series of theoretical models, which 455 

expressed predicted between -condition similarity. Specifically, we used four categorical 456 

models: (1) task demand , (2) target category, (3) relevant feature, and (4) their conjunction. 457 

The first three were matrices where pairs of conditions sharing the same level of the 458 

corresponding component were labeled with ones (i.e., maximum similarity) and those 459 

featuring different levels, with zeros (i.e., minimum similarity). For instance, in the task 460 

demand RSM, the conditions [selection, animate, shape] and [selection, inanimate, color] 461 

were expected to be maximally similar and coded with a one, while the pair [selection, 462 

animate, shape] and [integration, animate, shape] was expected to be maximally dissimilar 463 

and coded with a zero. Replicating the strategy followed in previous studies (e.g., Kikumoto 464 

& Mayr, 2020), the conjunction model was generated after multiplying the first three, which 465 

resulted in an RSM that contained a diagonal of ones and zeros elsewhere, predicting 466 

condition -unique activity patterns.  Pearson’s correlation assessment of collinearity among 467 

the theoretical RDM models revealed full orthogonality between the task demand, target 468 

category, and relevant feature RDMs, and a moderate correlation of r = 0.38 between each 469 

of these models and the conjunctive RDM. Last, we fitted linear multiple regressions 470 

predicting the ROIs’ empirical RSMs with the four theoretical RSMs as predictors. The 471

Finally, and in an exploratory fashion, we ran an additional model -based RSA focusing on 474

participants had to detect, the response set required in each trial, or their conjunction. After 481

3.2.4. Conjunctive task representations 667 

Finally, we addressed the presence of conjunctive neural representations integrating the 668 

instructed task parameters into unique patterns. Following past studies (e.g.,  Kikumoto & 669 

Mayr, 2020) , we employed model -based RSA to quantify how well the ROIs encoding 670 

spaces could be explained  with theoretical models predicting pattern similarity only for 671 

same -context tasks, i.e., instructions sharing the same combination of the three task 672 

components. 673 

After computing each ROI’s cross -validated RSM, we estimated multiple regressions using, 674 

as predictors, models based on the three task components and their conjunction. Results, 675 

shown in  Table 3 , replicated the overarching strong effect of  task demand in shaping the 676 

encoding geometry, with this model linked to systematically above -zero beta weight in all 677 

the ROIs explored. Conversely, we did not detect a significant effect of the target category 678 

or the relevant feature with this analytical approach (see  Supp. Fig. 4A ). More critically,  the 679 

conjunctive model did not explain task coding in any of the ROIs explored (see  Fig 5E ). 680 

Further Bayes Factors provided moderate to strong evidence  supporting the null hypothesis 681 

in all ROIs except the preSMA/ACC, where inconclusive results were found (see  Table 3 ). 682

the other components or their conjunction, we conducted a follow -up exploratory analysis. 684 

We repeated the RSA as described in the Methods (Section 3.5.5), but separated the 685 

selection and integration conditions. This yielded isolated four -by -four RSMs, which were 686 

fitted  with regressors for the target category, the relevant feature, and their conjunction. This 687

4.  Discussion 709 

In this work, we aimed to characterize the neural coding mechanisms supporting novel task 710 

assembly across the MDN. To do so, we collected fMRI data while participants followed new 711 

verbal instructions and applied MVPA to address the informational content and format 712 

reflected in MDN activity. Addressing our first question, we identified distinct coding profiles 713 

across the network depending on the type of information  manipulated : the overarching task 714 

demand, the relevant target category, and the visual features participants had to respond 715 

to. Next, focusing on the instruction -driven representational spaces, generalization and 716 

dimensionality analysis revealed both abstract and high -dimensional neural codes. Finally, 717 

we examined whether conjunctive instruction codes were present in MDN activity. However, 718 

model -based RSA did not provide evidence of such context -specific coding architecture. 719 

Overall, our findings suggest that combinat orial coding geometries within the MDN may play 720

a role in novel, adaptive behavior, potentially exploiting both task generalization and 721 

expressivity, challenging the notion of an inherent trade -off between them. 722 

In line with past research, our findings highlight that MDN activity is adaptively configured to 723 

encode task -relevant information (for a review, see Zheng, Lu & Woolgar, 2024 & Duncan, 724 

2025). That is also the case in novel contexts, where these parameters are extracted  from 725 

complex verbal commands (Cole et al., 2011; González -García et al., 2017, 2021; 726 

Palenciano et al., 2019a; Sobrado et al., 2022). The analyses showed different coding 727 

profiles within this network, depending on the specific task content investigated. On one 728 

hand, the overarching task demand component, indicating current selection vs. integration 729 

goals, was widely represented acro ss the MDN. On the other hand, the encoding of the two 730 

stimulus -oriented parameters, the relevant target category and feature, was restricted to the 731 

IFJ and the  IPS . Although this pattern is consistent with a functional specialization of these 732 

regions, their larger size (i.e., greater number of voxels) may also have increased decoding 733 

sensitivity, potentially contributing to the higher decoding accuracy observed. These findings 734 

are consistent with the idea that the MDN informational profile reflected the hierarchical task 735 

structure, with higher -level parameters implemented through widespread neural codes, and 736 

lower -level information being coded more  locally . Supporting this view, recent EEG data 737 

obtained with the same paradigm revealed complementary temporal dynamics: while task 738 

demand was associated with temporally sustained and stable activity, target category and 739 

relevant feature were coded only transiently (Pena et al., 2025). More generally, similar 740 

hierarchical accounts have been formulated in the field of cognitive control (Badre, 2008, 741 

2024; Koechlin et al., 2003), supported by both fMRI (Badre & Nee, 2018; Nee, 2021; Nee 742 

& D’Esposito, 2016) and elect rophysiological (Cellier et al., 2022) evidence. Moreover, and 743 

resonating with the dual MDN model (Dosenbach et al., 2007, 2008), our findings highlighted 744 

that the lateral portion of this network (in comparison with the more medial, or cingulo -745 

opercular one) conveyed more diverse information (Crittenden et al., 2016) from different 746 

hierarchical levels. While early research pointed towards the lateral prefrontal cortex as the 747 

key hub for representing multiplexed task information (Duncan, 2001; Miller & Cohen , 2001; 748 

Sakai, 2008), our work showed a similar scenario for the IPS, where the three task 749 

parameters were jointly encoded, in line with more recent network -based conceptualisations 750 

of cognitive  control (Duncan et al., 2020; Cole et al., 2016;  Karimi -Rouzbahani et al., 2026 ). 751 

Future research with paradigms spanning wider hierarchical information may be needed to 752 

further describe the coding profiles displayed across different parts of the MDN. 753

Our main  goal  was to address the geometry or structure governing novel task coding within 754 

the MDN by focusing on two core features identified in the literature: abstraction and 755 

dimensionality. The CCGP scores robustly evidenced coding abstraction, particularly for the 756 

task demand component, whose activity patterns systematically generalized across all 757 

possible context combinations throughout the  MDN. This encoding format has been 758 

interpreted  as the main signature of compositional coding, i.e., the recombination of common 759 

neural representations to build broader task models (Cole, Laurent, et al., 2013; Frankland 760 

& Greene, 2020; Reverberi et al., 2012; Tafazoli et al., 2026). Hence, it has been proposed 761 

as a key mechanism for novel instructed performance (Cole, Laurent, et al., 2013; Ito et al., 762 

2022), with recent data showing its involvement during the first trials with a task (Mill et al., 763 

2025).  Through an exhaustive cross -decoding protocol, our work provided compelling 764 

evidence supporting this proposal, showing that patterns coding higher -order task 765 

information could be compositionally reused across new instructions. However, the same 766 

was not true for codes representing the lower -level target category or the relevant feature 767 

components. Although the two variables could be significantly  decoded  from IPS and IFJ 768 

activity patterns with the standard classification approach, we did not detect significant 769 

CCGP scores for either of them, nor above -chance accuracies in the individual cross -770 

decoding iterations (only one significant result across 16 iterations and 9 ROIs,  Fig. 3C ). 771 

Interestingly, despite their transient nature, these lower -level task features showed evidence 772 

of abstraction in an EEG study with the same paradigm, albeit with a weaker effect than task 773 

demands (Pena et al., 2025). One  possible explanation for these contrasting results is that 774 

the lower baseline decoding performance for these variables may have inherently reduced 775 

our statistical power to detect significant generalization effects. Additionally, transient task 776 

components may be difficult to detect with fMRI because of its relatively low temporal 777 

resolution. While this null finding should be treated with caution, similar results where 778 

generalizable and non -generalizable task dimensions coexist have been found  in past 779 

studies (e.g., Sobrado et al., 2022). These findings suggest that compositional coding could 780 

be selectively recruited by specific sources of task content or contextual demands. 781 

Critically, by examining both coding abstraction and dimensionality, we sought to understand 782 

the interplay between two possible geometries that could scaffold novel instruction following: 783 

either abstract and compressed (i.e., low -dimensional) architectures promoting 784 

generalization to novel contexts, or context -specific and high -dimensional ones, linked to 785 

greater read -out expressivity (Badre et al., 2021; Musslick & Cohen, 2021). Our data 786

suggested a more complex picture, one in which abstract and generalizable neural codes 787 

coexisted alongside expansive, high -dimensional representational spaces. We predicted 788 

that neural code abstraction observed for overall task demand would be reflected  in a 789 

compressed coding architecture. On the contrary, SD results showed that the distributed 790 

activity in the MDN was systematically structured around a greater number of axes than 791 

those required by the task, incorporating multiple arbitrary (or non -task -informative) 792 

dimensions. Numerically, this effect was rather generalized across the MDN. It is worth 793 

noting that, because the SD method provided a single dimensionality value per ROI (and 794 

not subject -wise estimates), these scores could not be  statistically compared; thus, this 795 

finding should be interpreted with caution. Moreover, these high -dimensional estimates 796 

should be interpreted in light of the broad pattern of task demands in our data, which may 797 

have particularly affected some of the arbitrary dimensions. Nonetheless, such high SD 798 

estimates resonate with a wide body of literature showing expansive dimensionality in 799 

prefrontal and/or parietal activity patterns  (Fusi et al., 2016) , as has been shown in 800 

intracranial recordings  (Bernardi et al., 2020; Rigotti et al., 2013)  and fMRI  (Bhandari et al., 801 

2024).  It has been thus considered an intrinsic property of these regions that maximizes the 802 

readout of their activity patterns and, hence, their expressivity (i.e., the number of 803 

distinguishable pieces of information that are encoded;  Badre et al., 2021; Fusi et al., 2016; 804 

Rigotti et al., 2013) . Furthermore, recent EEG results have suggested that encoding 805 

dimensionality is progressively expanded  over the time course of task preparation to 806 

implement new, complex instructions (Pena et al., 2025). Taken together, novel task 807 

scenarios could recruit this geometrical feature across the MDN, leveraging greater 808 

dimensionalities to host diverse repertoires of instructed  demands. Finally, in most ROIs, a 809 

subset of these arbitrary coding dimensions also displayed an abstract, generalizable format 810 

(Fig. 4D ). Previous theoretical proposals have posited that abstract codes should be low 811 

dimensional (Badre et al., 2021; Musslick & Cohen, 2021), as a lower -dimensional structure 812 

would offer the most parsimonious solution for generalization. Our results, however, 813 

challenge this account by raising the possibility that more demanding coding organizations 814 

may also support  abstraction . Consistent with this view, recent evidence suggests that 815 

generalization can emerge under associative inference learning, where latent task 816 

reinstatement has been observed (Lee et al, 2026). Although the interpretation remains 817 

uncertain, converging evidence from earlier work (Bernardi et al., 2020), points to a 818 

promising path for future research on coding  geometry. 819

Our third question was whether conjunctive codes driven by novel instructions manifested 820 

in our paradigm, since recent  studies have stressed the relevance of this coding architecture 821 

for adaptive task control (Kikumoto et al., 2024; Kikumoto, Mayr, et al., 2022; Kikumoto, 822 

Sameshima, et al., 2022; Kikumoto & Mayr, 2020; Chai et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026). 823 

Conjunctive coding states that different task components are bound or mixed into unique 824 

representations (Hommel, 2009). This mechanism is tightly linked with high -dimensional 825 

spaces, where the dimensions are thought  to implement these context -unique and 826 

segregated neural task codes (Badre et al., 2021). Nonetheless, we did not detect evidence 827 

supporting conjunctive novel task representations. In this regard, although we employed the 828 

standard approach used in the past to identify the presence of conjunctive coding at the 829 

macroscopic scale (model -based RS A and a similar theoretical RSM;  e.g., Kikumoto & Mayr, 830 

2020), our paradigm imposed a key difference. Specifically, previous studies have employed 831 

simpler tasks where a limited set of individual conjunctions (or task combinations) were 832 

widely repeated. The complexity of our paradigm, instead, led to different levels of 833 

conjunctions: from broader combinations of the three main task parameters to lower -level 834 

associations between specific features and response sets, but also finer -grained trial -wise 835 

combinatio ns of instructed content. Our methodology allowed us to interrogate the two 836 

former possibilities (high - and low -level con junction), not finding significant results for any. 837 

Still, we could not address whether individual instructions (shown only once in our paradigm) 838 

were conjunctively encoded  into unique activity patterns. Moreover, past studies supporting 839 

conjunctive neural codes relied on temporally resolved EEG recordings. In contrast, the fMRI 840 

BOLD signal collected in our work integrates neural events at a substantially slower 841 

timescale, which could have hindered the detection of this phenomenon. Therefore, i t842 

remains uncertain whether our null results indicate a lack of conjunctive process for novel 843 

tasks or, instead, whether our design was not sensitive to the task content or the temporal 844 

scale at which these conjunctions are formed. 845 

Several limitations of the current study should be acknowledged. Clearly, the slow timescale 846 

of fMRI BOLD signal constitutes a general constraint, as this technique is largely blind to 847 

fast changes in representational dynamics.  Nevertheless, a previous EEG study using the 848 

same paradigm replicates several of our main findings, particularly the stability of higher -849 

level task demands relative to the more transient effects of stimulus -bound variables (Pena 850 

et al., 2025). Related to the fMRI temporal precision, a further limitation concerns the 851 

proximity between epoch events: our design does not allow for a full dissociation between 852

time windows i n which these effects emerge. However, given that our primary analyses were 854 

not focused on contrasting these events, we do not consider this a critical issue (see 855

paradigm that could have overshadowed lower -level variables,  although we implemented 861 

multiple controls during both task design and  analysis to prevent potential confounds. To 862 

test  whether effects for lower -level variables might emerge when accounting for task 863 

demand, we exploratorily performed RSA separately within each task demand condition. 864 

The null results persisted for target category, relevant feature, and their conjunction across 865 

all ROIs (see  Supp. Fig. 5  and  Supp. Table 5 ). This control analysis therefore, does not 866

during complex behavior (International Brain Laboratory, 2025; Mill et al., 2025), suggesting 874 

that the difficulty imposed by our current paradigm may underlie such broad representations 875 

of task demand. Moreover, EEG results with the same paradigm also corroborate strong 876 

and stable task coding (Pena et al., 2025). 877 

Overall, our findings suggest a complex interplay of representational  features underpinning 878 

novel task assembly. Although most of the current theoretical models state that the two 879 

coding geometries we explored (abstract and compact vs. context -unique and high -880 

dimensional) constitute the two extremes of a continuum (Badre et al., 2021; Musslick & 881 

Cohen, 2021; but also see Egner, 2023), recent studies have also reported the presence of 882 

blended architectures that combine abstraction with high -dimensionality (Bernardi et al., 883 

2020; Pena et al., 2025). Hence, independently combining both coding principles could 884 

effectively tailor the coding geometry to current challenges, achieving a balance between 885
