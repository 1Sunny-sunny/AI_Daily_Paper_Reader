## Abstract 24 

25 

In  rodents,  hippocampal  activity  is  classically  described  in  terms  of  position -anchored  place 26 

cells,  whereas  nonhuman  primate  work  has  emphasized  spatial  view  cells,  in  which  activity 27 

depends  on  the  region  of  space  being  viewed.  This  contrast  has  fueled  a longstanding  debate 28 

over  whether  primate  and  rodent  spatial  codes  are  biologically  distinct.  We  recorded  single 29 

units  in  the  hippocampus  of  macaques  as  they  navigated  linear  tracks  in  two  visually  distinct 30 

contexts,  intermittently  making  context -dependent  object  choices.  Using  a generalized  linear 31 

model  to  dissociate  position  coding  from  spatial  view  coding,  we  identified  three  coexisting 32 

populations  in  the  macaque  hippocampus:  Position  cells,  View  cells,  and  Conjunctive  cells 33 

encoding  both  variables.  Position  cells  were  prevalent  and  showed  rodent -like  properties —34 

localized  firing , directional  tuning , and  context ual  remapping —even  after  accounting  for 35 

visual  sampling,  whereas  View  cells  stably  encoded  allocentric  visual  space.  Population 36 

decoding  confirmed  this  dissociation  at  the  ensemble  level.  Robust  position  codes  thus 37 

coexist  with,  yet  remain  dissociable  from,  view  codes  in  the  primate  hippocampus, 38 

suggesting  that  the  apparent  scarcity  of  place -like  responses  in  earlier  primate  work  reflects 39 

methodological  and  task -related  differences  rather  than  a fundamental  species -level 40 

divergence. 41

## Introduction 42 

43 

The  hippocampus  is  fundamentally  involved  in  episodic  memory  and  spatial  navigation 1,2 .44 

Rodent  hippocampal  place  cells,  which  fire  selectively  as  an  animal  traverses  specific 45 

locations,  led  to  the  influential  view  that  the  hippocampus  constructs  an  internal  cognitive 46 

map  of  space.  Building  on  this  framework,  subsequent  work  extended  cognitive -map -based 47 

models  of  hippocampal  function  from  rodents  to  nonhuman  primates  and  humans 1,3 –6,48 

shaping  a broad  research  field  centered  on  spatial  coding  and  navigation.  As  this  line  of 49 

research  has  developed,  studies  in  primates  and  humans 7–12 , often  using  virtual  navigation 50 

and  single -unit  recordings  or  neuroimaging,  have  substantially  advanced  our  understanding 51 

of  how  the  hippocampus  and  its  connected  networks  support  spatial  cognition  across  species. 52 

Despite  th is  wide ly  adopted  framework,  the  nature  of  spatial  coding  in  primates 53 

remains  unresolved.  In  rodents,  hippocampal  activity  is  typically  described  in  terms  of 54 

position -anchored  place  coding,  whereas  studies  in  nonhuman  primates  have  often 55 

emphasized  spatial  view  coding 11 –18 , in  which  neurons  fire  according  to  the  part  of  the 56 

environment  being  looked  at  rather  than  the  animal’s  physical  location.  Repeated  reports  of 57 

view -dominant  coding,  together  with  the  importance  of  active  visual  sampling  in  primates 58 

with  advanced  foveal  vision 19 –24 , have  led  to  the  proposal  that  spatial  representations  in  the 59 

primate  hippocampus  may  be  organized  differently  from  those  classically  described  in 60 

rodents.  Interestingly,  previous  nonhuman  primate  studies  have  often  failed  to  demonstrate 61 

place  cells  with  the  same  robustness  and  spatial  precision  characterized  in  rodents,  along  with 62 

their  defining  features  such  as  directional  selectivity 25 –28  and  contextual  remapping 29 –33 ,63 

across  settings  ranging  from  free  navigation  in  open  cages 15,16,34,35  to  linear  tracks 36,37  and 64 

virtual  space 11,12,38 . These  discrepancies  have  fueled  an  ongoing  controversy:  is  the  primate 65 

hippocampal  code  fundamentally  distinct  from  the  location -based  representations  found  in 66 

rodents? 19,20,22,24 

67 

Recent  physiological  evidence  suggests  that  the  primate  hippocampus  may  instead  be 68 

governed  by  mixed  selectivity,  in  which  individual  neurons  encode  multiple  variables 69 

concurrently  rather  than  a single  spatial  quantity  in  isolation 11,15 –17 . In  this  coding  scheme, 70 

individual  neurons  encode  multiple  variables  concurrently  rather  than  a single  spatial  quantity 71 

in  isolation.  Studies  employing  multivariate  models  to  account  for  diverse  variables , such  as 72 

location,  spatial  view , and  facing  direction  have  found  that  a larger  proportion  of 73

direction  or  other  behavioral  variables,  precluding  the  identification  of  pure  position -76

the  hypothesis  that  the  primate  hippocampal  code  is  fundamentally  distinct  from  the 78

Despite  these  discrepancies,  we  hypothesized  that  the  primate  hippocampus  can 80 

express  robust,  rodent -like  spatial  representations  when  specific  experimental  and  analytical 81 

constraints  are  met.  We  proposed  that  such  representations  emerge  under  a combination  of 82

variables . Collectively,  the se  results  suggest  that  the  long -debated  differences  between  rodent 93 

and  primate  hippocampal  codes  may  reflect  methodological  and  task -related  factors  at  least 94 

as  much  as  fundamental  biological  differences,  helping  to  bridge  a decades -old  gap  in 95 

comparative  neuroscience. 96

Position  and  view  codes  coexist  but  are  dissociable  in  the  primate  hippocampus 122 

To  determine  whether  hippocampal  neurons  encoded  position,  spatial  view,  or  both,  Poisson 123 

generalized  linear  models  (GLM s) were  fit  separately  in  each  of  the  four  task  conditions 124 

defined  by  context  and  travel  direction:  Forest -Outbound  (F -O),  Forest -Inbound  (F -I),  City -125 

Outbound  (C -O),  and  City -Inbound  (C -I)  (Fig.  2A).  For  each  neuron,  position,  view,  and 126 

conjunctive  models  were  compared  by  cross -validated  encoding  performance,  and  neurons 127 

were  classified  according  to  the  model  that  best  explained  their  activity.  Representative 128 

examples  showed  that  each  class  was  well  captured  by  its  corresponding  model,  with 129 

reconstructed  model  rate  maps  closely  matching  the  actual  firing  patterns  (Fig.  2B -D).  Across 130 

all  recorded  single  units  (n  = 113),  87.7%  were  classified  as  spatially  modulated  in  at  least 131 

one  task  condition,  whereas  the  remainder  were  non -spatial  (Null)  in  all  conditions  (Fig.  2E). 132 

All  three  classes  were  present  in  every  condition  (Fig.  2F),  and  their  relative  proportions  did 133 

not  differ  significantly  across  conditions  (χ² (9)  = 9.13,  p = 0.43,  chi -square  test),  indicating 134 

that  the  three  coding  schemes  were  consistently  expressed  across  contexts  and  directions. 135

Position  cells  show  stable  and  localized  spatial  tuning 147 

We  next  characterized  the  firing  properties  of  Position  cells  identified  by  the  GLM s. Cells 148 

with  high  SI  (>  0.15)  showed  sharply  localized  tuning,  with  spikes  concentrated  at 149 

circumscribed  locations  along  the  track  across  laps  (Fig.  3A).  Others  with  lower  SI  exhibited 150 

broader  or  multi -peaked  tuning  (Fig.  3B).  Importantly,  these  cells  were  still  classified  by  the 151 

GLM s as  Position  cells,  indicating  that  low  values  of  a marginal  information  metric  did  not 152 

preclude  robust  position -dependent  coding. 153 

The  proportion  of  Position  cells  did  not  differ  significantly  across  task  conditions 154 

(χ² (3)  = 4.09,  p = 0.25,  chi -square  test),  corresponding  to  11%  to  20%  of  neurons  per 155 

condition  and  41.6%  of  all  neurons  in  at  least  one  condition  (Fig.  3C).  SI  scores  spanned  a156 

broad  range  (Fig.  3D),  consistent  with  the  coexistence  of  sharply  tuned  single -field  responses 157 

and  broader  or  multi -peaked  tuning  patterns  within  the  same  population.  When  we  examined 158 

the  tuning  stability  by  correlating  each  cell's  rate  maps  between  odd  and  even  laps,  71.2%  of 159 

Position  cells  showed  significant  stability  (Fig.  3E),  suggesting  that  position  tuning  was 160 

reliable  within  a session. 161 

At  the  population  level,  Position -cell  rate  maps  collectively  tiled  the  linear  track  in 162 

every  condition  (Fig.  3F).  Fields  were  overrepresented  near  the  lap  start/end  region, 163 

corresponding  to  the  point  at  which  the  animal  entered  a context  at  the  beginning  of  the 164 

outbound  run  and  left  it  at  the  end  of  the  inbound  run.  Because  context  identity  could  change 165 

from  lap  to  lap,  this  concentration  near  the  context -entry  region  may  reflect  elevated  demands 166 

on  contextual  identification  upon  entering  each  context . Taken  together,  these  results  indicate 167 

that  the  primate  hippocampus  contains  a substantial  population  of  neurons  with  stable  and 168 

spatially  structured  position  tuning  during  virtual  navigation. 169

decoded  accurately  from  the  recorded  population,  with  decoded  location  closely  tracking  the 243 

animal's  actual  location  along  the  track  (Fig.  8A).  To  test  whether  position -coding  cells 244 

specifically  supported  this  performance,  decoding  accuracy  was  compared  across  two 245 

ensembles:  a position  ensemble  composed  of  Position  and  Conjunctive  cells  and  a non -246 

position  ensemble  composed  of  View  and  non -spatial  cells.  The  position  ensemble 247 

outperformed  size -matched  non -position  ensembles  in  every  condition,  with  error  advantages 248 

of  11.17  (F -O;  95%  CI  [7.47,  14.52]),  7.01  (F -I;  [4.96,  9.17]),  5.65  (C -O;  [2.91,  8.94]),  and 249 

7.46  (C -I;  [5.17,  9.77])  bins  (Fig.  8B) . Consistent  with  this,  spike -train  shuffling  in  the 250 

position  ensemble  strongly  degraded  decoding  (red  in  Fig.  8C),  whereas  shuffling  non -251 

position  cells  had  a much  smaller  effect  (blue  in  Fig.  8C),  indicating  that  accurate  position 252 

decoding  depended  primarily  on  the  activity  of  position -coding  neurons. 253 

An  analogous  result  was  obtained  for  spatial  view.  View  could  be  decoded  accurately 254 

across  conditions  from  the  full  recorded  population  (Fig.  8D).  A view  ensemble  composed  of 255 

View  and  Conjunctive  cells  outperformed  size -matched  non -view  ensembles  in  all 256 

conditions,  with  error  advantages  of  2.99  (F -O;  95%  CI  [2.61,  3.94]),  1.81  (F -I;  [0.96,  2.73]), 257 

2.41  (C -O;  [1.70,  3.09]),  and  1.88  (C -I;  [0.78,  3.01])  surface  steps  (Fig.  8E).  As  in  the 258 

position  analysis,  shuffling  spikes  in  the  view  ensemble  impaired  decoding  much  more 259 

strongly  than  shuffling  non -view  cells  (Fig.  8F).  These  population  analyses  demonstrate  that 260 

the  dissociation  between  position  and  view  coding  extends  beyond  single -cell  classifications. 261 

Rather,  the  two  forms  of  spatial  information  are  carried  preferentially  by  partially  distinct 262 

ensembles  that  operate  concurrently  within  the  same  hippocampal  population. 263

The  present  findings  show  that  the  primate  hippocampus  contains  coexisting  but  dissociable 268 

neural  codes  for  self -location  and  spatial  view  during  virtual  navigation.  Combining  a field -269

View  cells  forming  stable  representations  of  allocentric  visual  space,  and  a relatively  small 272 

population  of  Conjunctive  cells  encoding  both.  Population  decoding  confirmed  that  this 273 

dissociation  extends  to  the  ensemble  level,  with  position  and  view  information  carried 274 

preferentially  by  their  respective  ensembles.  The  fact  that  robust  position  codes  coexist  with 275 

spatial  view  codes  during  visually  enriched,  task -demanding  virtual  navigation  suggests  that 276 

the  view -dominant  description  of  primate  hippocampal  spatial  coding  may  reflect,  at  least  in 277 

part,  methodological  and  task  factors  rather  than  an  intrinsic  constraint  of  the  system. 278 

One  central  implication  is  that  spatial  coding  in  the  primate  hippocampus  cannot  be 279 

reduced  to  a single  dominant  reference  frame.  Earlier  primate  studies  either  reported  location -280 

specific  firing  without  fully  dissociating  it  from  gaze 35 –37,40 –42 , or  emphasized  view -based  or 281 

mixed  selectivity 11,12,15,16 . Our  data  extend  these  accounts  by  showing  that  a substantial 282 

population  of  neurons  is  better  explained  by  position  even  after  view -dependent  sampling  is 283 

explicitly  modeled.  During  movement  along  a linear  track,  an  animal's  location  and  the 284 

portion  of  space  it  views  are  intrinsically  coupled,  so  position -related  firing  can  be  mistaken 285 

for  view  coding  and  vice  versa;  separating  the  two  requires  a model  that  estimates  each 286 

contribution  while  controlling  for  the  other.  The  current  modeling  framework  addressed  this 287 

problem  by  forcing  position,  view,  and  conjunctive  explanations  to  compete  against  one 288 

another  within  each  task  condition.  Under  this  approach,  a distinct  population  remained  best 289 

explained  by  position  alone,  indicating  that  view‑independent  location  coding  can  be 290 

recovered  in  the  primate  hippocampus  when  the  relevant  variables  are  explicitly  dissociated. 291 

The  primate  hippocampus  therefore  appeared  to  maintain  parallel  representations  of  where 292 

the  animal  is  and  what  part  of  the  environment  it  is  sampling  visually. 293

properties  with  classical  place  cells.  First,  positional  firing  was  spatially  localized  and  reliable 295

signature  of  a place -cell -like  map 43,44 . Second,  tuning  was  strongly  directional , with  most 297 

cells  preferentially  encoding  a single  travel  direction , as  reported  in  rodent  studies 25 –28,45 .298 

Third,  a substantial  fraction  remapped  between  the  Forest  and  City  contexts,  consistent  with 299 

the  hallmark  global  remapping  across  distinct  contexts  in  rodent  work 29 –33 . Together,  these 300

neuronal  signal  was  detected,  recordings  commenced  after  an  additional  stabilization  period 478 

of  at  least  15  minutes. 479 

Neural  activity  was  recorded  using  a Digital  Lynx  acquisition  system  (Neuralynx). 480 

Signals  were  amplified  1,000 –10,000 -fold  and  band -pass  filtered  between  300  and  6,000  Hz 481 

for  spike  detection.  Spike  waveforms  exceeding  a manually  adjusted  threshold  (typically  30 –482 

100  μV)  were  digitized  at  32  kHz  and  time -stamped. 483 

At  the  end  of  each  recording  session,  the  electrode  was  retracted  at  a speed  of  15 484 

μm/s.  The  electrode  and  recording  grid  were  then  removed,  the  recording  chamber  was 485 

thoroughly  cleaned  according  to  established  protocols,  and  the  chamber  cover  was  reinstalled 486 

to  seal  the  recording  chamber. 487 

All  data  were  collected  after  completion  of  behavioral  training  (i.e.,  completed  more 488 

than  100  laps  within  60  minutes  and  achieved  over  80%  correctness  for  each  object  across 489 

two  consecutive  days) . Neuronal  and  behavioral  data —including  trial  events,  the  monkey’s 490 

position  within  the  virtual  environment,  and  joystick  manipulation —were  recorded  in 491

single  electrode  or  a tetrode  (MEM  pencil,  Thomas  RECORDING  GmbH). 493

GLM -based  cell  classification 599 

To  dissociate  position  and  spatial  view  at  the  single -neuron  level,  we  fit  Poisson  GLMs  to 600 

each  neuron's  binned  spike  train,  separately  for  each  of  the  four  conditions,  comparing  three 601 

encoding  models:  a position  model  (location  parameterized  by  Gaussian  basis  functions  along 602 

the  track),  a view  model  (gaze  location  parameterized  as  the  bins  of  the  unfolded  view  box), 603 

and  a conjunctive  model  combining  both. 604 

Cross -validation  was  lap -based  to  avoid  within -lap  leakage:  laps  were  split  into  9 outer  folds, 605 

and  a nested  3-fold  cross -validation  on  the  training  laps  selecte d the  ridge -penalty  strength  λ606 

(from  a logarithmically  spaced  grid)  by  minimizing  held -out  Poisson  deviance.  Sparsely 607 

sampled  view  bins  (low  cumulative  occupancy)  were  masked  to  prevent  unstable  fits.  For 608 

each  test  fold,  encoding  performance  was  quantified  as  the  cross -validated  log -likelihood 609 

gain  per  bin  over  a mean -rate  null  model  (ΔLLH/bin). 610 

Each  neuron  was  classified  by  forward  selection  on  the  fold -wise  ΔLLH/bin.  The  better 611 

single -variable  model  (position  or  view)  was  first  tested  against  the  null  (one -tailed  Wilcoxon 612 

signed -rank  across  folds;  α = 0.05);  neurons  not  exceeding  the  null  were  classified  as  non -613 

spatial  (Null).  Otherwise,  the  conjunctive  model  was  tested  against  the  best  single  model  and 614 

the  neuron  classified  as  Conjunctive  if  it  significantly  improved  on  it,  or  as  a Position  or 615 

View  cell  otherwise.  Classification  was  performed  independently  in  each  condition. 616 

Each  model  was  also  refit  on  all  of  a condition's  data  for  the  rate  map  reconstructions  shown 617 

in  the  figures;  the  significance  of  each  fit  was  assessed  by  circularly  shifting  the  spike  train 618 

relative  to  the  predictors  (1,000  shuffles)  and  comparing  the  observed  log -likelihood  to  the 619
