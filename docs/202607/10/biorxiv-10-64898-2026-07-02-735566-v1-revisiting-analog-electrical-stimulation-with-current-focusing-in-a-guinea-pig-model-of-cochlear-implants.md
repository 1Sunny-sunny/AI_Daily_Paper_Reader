## 摘要
现代耳蜗植入体主要使用脉冲刺激，在安静环境下能提供稳健的言语识别，但对时间精细结构的表征较差。模拟刺激保留了连续的声学波形，可能更好地编码时间信息，但尚未结合现代电流聚焦策略进行系统评估。我们在乌拉坦麻醉的豚鼠中，比较了通过单极、双极和三极电极配置传递的每秒100个脉冲的双相脉冲串与每秒100个周期的正弦模拟刺激所诱发的神经反应。耳蜗内植入后，使用16通道硅探针从下丘中央核的音调拓扑轴上记录多单元活动。量化了检测阈值、兴奋扩散、矢量强度、持续反应百分比和时间反应特性。与脉冲刺激相比，模拟刺激始终产生显著更低的激活阈值，同时在不同刺激模式下保持相当的空间选择性；双极模拟刺激还产生了更窄的兴奋扩散。相反，模拟刺激产生了较低的矢量强度、更大的持续反应成分以及显著的强度依赖性极性效应。在低刺激强度下，反应以正弦波形的阴极相为主，而增加刺激强度则募集了波形的两个相，产生两倍于刺激频率的同步。这些发现表明，刺激波形强烈影响时间编码，而对神经激活的空间分布影响相对较小。将模拟刺激与电流聚焦相结合，在不牺牲空间选择性的情况下降低了激活阈值，并产生了与传统脉冲串诱发的基本不同的时间反应特性。这些结果为重新审视模拟刺激作为耳蜗植入声音编码的替代策略提供了生理学基础。

## Abstract
Modern cochlear implants (CIs) primarily use pulsatile stimulation, which provides robust speech recognition in quiet but poorly represents temporal fine structure (TFS). Analog stimulation preserves the continuous acoustic waveform and may better encode temporal information, yet it has not been systematically evaluated in combination with modern current-focusing strategies. We compared neural responses evoked by 100 pulses/s biphasic pulse trains and 100 cycles/s sinusoidal analog stimulation delivered through monopolar, bipolar, and tripolar electrode configurations in urethane-anesthetized guinea pigs. Following intracochlear implantation, multiunit activity was recorded from the tonotopic axis of the central nucleus of the inferior colliculus using 16-channel silicon probes. Detection thresholds, spread of excitation, vector strength, sustained response percentage, and temporal response properties were quantified. Analog stimulation consistently evoked significantly lower activation thresholds than pulsatile stimulation while maintaining comparable spatial selectivity across stimulation modes; bipolar analog stimulation additionally produced a narrower spread of excitation. In contrast, analog stimulation generated lower vector strength, larger sustained response components, and a pronounced level-dependent polarity effect. At low stimulus levels, responses were dominated by the cathodic phase of the sinusoidal waveform, whereas increasing stimulus level recruited both waveform phases, producing synchronization at twice the stimulus frequency. These findings demonstrate that stimulation waveform strongly influences temporal coding while having relatively little effect on the spatial distribution of neural activation. Combining analog stimulation with current focusing reduced activation thresholds without sacrificing spatial selectivity and produced temporal response properties that differed fundamentally from those evoked by conventional pulse trains. These results provide a physiological basis for reexamining analog stimulation as an alternative strategy for cochlear implant sound coding.
