## 摘要
运动是动态环境的一个决定性特征，也是感觉系统编码的一个关键变量。在视觉中，提取运动方向的神经机制已在不同物种中得到广泛研究，揭示了跨时空比较信号的回路。气味羽流同样在其时空结构中携带运动信息，行走的果蝇会检测并利用这些信息来改进导航，而不依赖于风感。然而，嗅觉回路如何计算气味运动仍然未知。在此，我们确定了果蝇触角叶中将对侧嗅觉输入转化为方向选择性信号并传递至高级脑区的回路。这些方向选择性信号广泛存在于大多数测量到的嗅觉通道中，涵盖信息素、食物气味和厌恶线索。方向性反应由具有自然时间统计特性的气味轨迹引发，并调谐至约40毫秒的触角间延迟。连接组学、生理学和行为学分析确定触角叶中的一个GABA能抑制性神经元是方向选择性的关键介导者。对方向选择性神经元动态的测量揭示了同侧兴奋和延迟的对侧抑制。在一个最小数据驱动的模型中，这一机制足以产生观察到的方向选择性反应。这些结果将运动检测确立为回路水平的嗅觉计算，并揭示了一个早期感觉回路如何将时空化学信号转化为气味运动的神经表征，以引导导航。

## Abstract
Movement is a defining feature of dynamic environments and a key variable encoded by sensory systems. In vision, neural mechanisms that extract motion direction have been studied extensively across species, revealing circuits that compare signals across space and time. Odor plumes likewise carry motion information in their spatiotemporal structure, which walking fruit flies detect and exploit to improve navigation, independent of wind sensing. However, how olfactory circuits compute odor motion remains unknown. Here we identify circuitry in the \textit{Drosophila} antennal lobe that transforms bilateral odor inputs into direction-selective signals that are propagated to higher brain regions. These direction-selective signals were widespread across most measured olfactory channels, spanning pheromones, food odors, and aversive cues. Directional responses were elicited by odor traces with naturalistic temporal statistics and tuned to inter-antennal delays of $\approx$40~ms. Connectomic, physiological, and behavioral analyses identified one GABAergic inhibitory neuron in the antennal lobe as a key mediator of direction selectivity. Measurements of dynamics in direction-selective neurons revealed ipsilateral excitation and delayed contralateral inhibition. In a minimal data-driven model, this mechanism was sufficient to generate the observed direction-selective responses. These results establish motion detection as a circuit-level olfactory computation and reveal how an early sensory circuit transforms spatiotemporal chemical signals into a neural representation of odor motion to guide navigation.
