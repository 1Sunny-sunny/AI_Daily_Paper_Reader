Key Points 19 

• The fasciola cinereum (FC) is a small and poorly understood hippocampal subregion that 20 

has recently been implicated in hippocampal related function and seizure activity. 21 

• Here we show that neurons in the FC are morphologically and functionally distinct from 22 

cells in neighboring CA1 and CA2 hippocampal subregions, and find substantial 23 

physiological diversity of FC neurons, including differences in firing behavior, membrane 24 

properties, and intrinsic excitability. 25 

• Pharmacological analyses show that Kv2 and Kv7 potassium channels play a key role in 26 

shaping the intrinsic properties of FC neurons. 27 

• Less excitable FC neurons receive stronger excitatory synaptic input, revealing an 28 

inverse relationship between intrinsic membrane excitability and synaptic drive. 29 

• These findings identify the FC as a physiologically specialized hippocampal subregion 30 

and provide a foundation for understanding its role in hippocampal function and 31 

neurological disease. 32 

Abstract 33 

The fasciola cinereum (FC) is a small, conserved hippocampal subregion whose cellular properties 34 

remain poorly understood. Anatomically positioned between dorsal CA1 and the third ventricle, the 35 

FC receives diverse cortical and subcortical inputs but is often excluded from hippocampal circuit 36 

models. Here, we performed ex vivo whole-cell patch-clamp recordings in mouse hippocampal 37 

slices to define the intrinsic and synaptic properties of FC neurons. We find that FC neurons are 38 

electrophysiologically and morphologically distinct from neighboring CA1 and CA2 pyramidal cells, 39 

exhibiting reduced intrinsic excitability, delayed spike initiation, and enhanced 40 

afterhyperpolarizations. These properties are strongly shaped by potassium conductances, with 41 

Kv2 (KCNB) and Kv7 (KCNQ) channels contributing to the suppression of firing. We find that FC 42 

neurons span a continuum of intrinsic excitability which is structured by variability in potassium 43 

channel–mediated conductances and is inversely coordinated with excitatory synaptic input, such 44 

that less excitable neurons receive stronger synaptic drive. Together, these findings establish a 45 

mechanistic framework in which intrinsic membrane properties and synaptic input are co-organized 46 

across the FC population, providing new insight into how this understudied hippocampal region 47 

may contribute to the regulation of neural excitability. 48

Introduction 49 

50 

The hippocampus is classically organized into distinct subregions, including the dentate 51 

gyrus (DG), CA3, CA2, and CA1. At its dorsomedial boundary lies the fasciola cinereum (FC), a 52 

small and evolutionarily conserved subregion that has received comparatively little attention. The 53 

hippocampal formation is classically organized along a well-defined tri-synaptic pathway, with 54 

information flowing from the entorhinal cortex to the DG, then to CA3, and subsequently to CA1, 55 

before being relayed back to the entorhinal cortex or projected to other cortical and subcortical 56 

regions (1). This unidirectional circuit underlies key hippocampal functions such as spatial 57 

navigation (2, 3), learning (4, 5), and memory consolidation (6–9). Anatomically, FC is situated at 58 

the interface between the third ventricle and dorsal CA1 in rodents. It receives convergent inputs 59 

from diverse cortical and subcortical regions, including the entorhinal and perirhinal cortex, and has 60 

been found to project primarily to the DG, with additional projections to indusium griseum, 61 

retrosplenial cortex, intermediate lateral septum, distal CA1 and septal CA2 (10–16). However, the 62 

precise connectivity of the FC remains incompletely resolved, as recent work did not find clear 63 

evidence for a prominent projection to the dentate gyrus (11). These distinct and still evolving 64 

connectivity patterns point to a unique role of the FC in shaping network interactions within and 65 

beyond the hippocampus. Nevertheless, it has long been viewed as a vestigial or transitional zone 66 

and excluded from prevailing models of hippocampal circuitry. 67 

Recent work challenges this view, revealing that ablation of FC in rats impaired the 68 

acquisition of visual contextual memory (11, 12, 17). In addition, aberrant FC activity can play a 69 

causal role in seizure initiation and propagation, as supported by intracranial recordings in human 70 

patients showing seizure onset within the FC and optogenetic inhibition studies of FC in a mouse 71 

model of temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) (18). Furthermore, surgical ablation of the FC in a drug-72 

resistant temporal lobe epilepsy patient was associated with seizure relief, suggesting a conserved 73 

pathophysiological role across species (18–21). The FC shares several molecular markers with the 74 

CA2 hippocampal subregion, which has also been shown to regulate seizures in mouse models of 75

regions. Although a recent transcriptomic study highlighted molecular heterogeneity between the 77 

FC and CA2 (27), to date there has been no systematic comparison of the electrophysiological and 78 

morphological properties between FC and CA2. Understanding how FC neurons differ from their 79 

CA2 counterparts is thus essential to elucidate how these hippocampal structures contribute to 80 

network modulation and pathological hyperexcitability. 81 

Recent in vivo studies in rodent models have reported conflicting results regarding the 82 

electrophysiological properties of FC neurons (11, 12, 17). Juxtacellular recordings suggest that 83 

FC neurons exhibit firing properties comparable to dorsal CA1 neurons (11), whereas tetrode 84 

recordings indicate sparse and delayed firing, consistent with reduced excitability (12). These 85 

discrepancies leave unresolved fundamental questions regarding the intrinsic properties of FC 86 

neurons and the extent of their heterogeneity. In addition to intrinsic membrane properties, 87 

differences in synaptic input may also contribute to shaping neuronal activity across hippocampal 88 

subregions. However, whether FC neurons differ from neighboring regions in their excitatory 89 

synaptic drive remains unclear. Characterizing both intrinsic excitability and synaptic input is 90 

essential to define how FC neurons are positioned functionally within hippocampal circuits. 91 

Here, we performed whole-cell patch-clamp recordings and post hoc morphological 92 

analysis in acute mouse hippocampal slices to systematically characterize FC neurons and 93 

compare them with CA1 and CA2 pyramidal cells. We find that FC neurons exhibit reduced intrinsic 94 

excitability and distinct dendritic architecture relative to neighboring regions. Importantly, we found 95 

variation in FC intrinsic excitability which is strongly influenced by potassium channel–mediated 96 

conductances. In parallel, FC neurons receive overall enhanced excitatory synaptic input compared 97 

to neighboring CA1 pyramidal cells, which varies across the population and is inversely related to 98 

intrinsic excitability. Together, these findings identify distinct properties unique to the FC among 99 

hippocampal subregions and provide a cellular framework for understanding its potential 100 

contribution to hippocampal network dynamics. 101

FC neurons exhibit reduced intrinsic excitability and distinct dendritic architecture 104 

Whole-cell current-clamp recordings were performed in acute hippocampal slices to 105 

compare the intrinsic properties of neurons in CA1, CA2, and FC (Fig. 1 A). Representative firing 106 

patterns revealed markedly lower excitability in FC neurons compared with CA1 and CA2 pyramidal 107 

neurons (Fig. 1 B). Consistent with this observation, the input–output relationship showed a 108 

shallower increase in action potential (AP) frequency in FC neurons (Fig. 1 C). FC neurons required 109 

larger current injections to reach threshold (rheobase; Fig. 1 D) and exhibited longer interspike 110 

intervals (Fig. 1 E), indicating reduced firing capability. These differences were accompanied by 111 

distinct intrinsic membrane properties. FC neurons displayed more hyperpolarized resting 112 

membrane potentials (Fig. 1 G), higher AP thresholds (Fig. 1 H), and smaller AP amplitudes (Fig. 113 

1 I). Subthreshold and passive properties also differed across regions: FC neurons exhibited 114 

smaller hyperpolarization-activated sag responses (Fig. A1 B), larger medium 115 

afterhyperpolarization (mAHP) amplitudes (Fig. A1 C), and higher input resistance compared with 116 

CA1 neurons (Fig. A1 D). Although CA2 neurons showed higher input resistance than CA1 117 

neurons, this difference was not significant when compared with FC neurons. In addition to putative 118 

excitatory neurons, we identified a population of interneurons within the FC based on their distinct 119 

electrophysiological properties (Fig. A2). These cells were identified based on their fast-spiking 120 

firing patterns (Fig. A2 A) and unique electrophysiological signatures (Fig. A2 B–C), including 121 

depolarized resting membrane potential (RMP; Fig. A2 E), hyperpolarized action potential threshold 122 

(Fig. A2 H), prominent afterhyperpolarizations (AHPs; Fig. A2 I), and increased input resistance 123 

and sag (Fig. A2 J-K). 124 

To determine whether intrinsic physiological differences were associated with structural 125 

specialization, we performed reconstructions of biocytin-filled neurons from CA1, CA2, and the FC 126 

(Fig. 1 J). Quantitative morphological analysis revealed pronounced differences in dendritic 127 

architecture across regions. Sholl analysis demonstrated reduced dendritic complexity in FC 128

Kv7-mediated conductances contribute to variability in intrinsic excitability across FC 211 

neurons 212 

The hyperpolarized resting membrane potential of FC neurons suggests that potassium 213 

channels active at subthreshold potentials may also contribute to their intrinsic excitability. Among 214 

these, Kv7 (KCNQ) channels mediate the M-current, a non-inactivating subthreshold conductance 215 

that stabilizes membrane potential and suppresses repetitive firing. Kv7.2 and Kv7.3 subunits are 216 

widely expressed in the hippocampus and exert strong inhibitory control over neuronal excitability 217 

(36–38). 218 

To assess the contribution of Kv7 channels in FC neurons, we recorded M-currents under 219 

voltage-clamp conditions. Kv7-mediated currents exhibited substantial variability across FC 220 

neurons, paralleling the heterogeneity observed in intrinsic excitability (Fig. 6 B–C, F). Notably, 221 

neurons with higher intrinsic excitability tended to display smaller M-current amplitudes, suggesting 222 

a reduced contribution of subthreshold potassium conductance in these cells. Importantly, Pearson 223 

correlation analysis revealed a significant negative correlation between Kv7-mediated current 224 

amplitude and firing output (Fig. 6 I). 225

Discussion 236 

Our results identify the fasciola cinereum (FC) as a distinct hippocampal neuronal 237 

population characterized by reduced intrinsic excitability and unique structural features. FC 238 

neurons differ from neighboring CA1 and CA2 neurons in both electrophysiological and 239 

morphological properties, supporting the view that the FC is not a dorsal extension of these regions 240 

but a separate circuit element with specialized cellular characteristics. 241 

Although FC shares many molecular markers with CA2 such as PCP4 and AMIGO2, our 242 

findings demonstrate that its physiological profile is fundamentally distinct. CA2 pyramidal neurons 243 

exhibit depolarized resting membrane potentials, minimal spike-frequency adaptation, and high 244 

firing rates associated with rapid encoding functions (39–41). In contrast, FC neurons exhibited 245 

pronounced spike-frequency adaptation, large afterhyperpolarizations, and hyperpolarized resting 246 

membrane potentials, indicating substantially reduced intrinsic excitability. Importantly, input 247 

resistance in FC neurons was not lower than in CA2 or CA1 neurons, suggesting that their 248 

hypoexcitability stems from enhanced active potassium conductances rather than passive 249 

membrane differences. Together, these comparisons underscore that FC and CA2 are 250 

electrophysiologically distinct. In addition to their unique electrophysiological properties, FC 251 

neurons exhibited differences in dendritic organization compared to neighboring hippocampal 252 

pyramidal neurons, with reduced dendritic complexity at distal radii. These features may influence 253 

synaptic integration and contribute to the functional profile of FC neurons. However, these 254 

observations should be interpreted with caution, as reconstructions from single acute slices may 255 

underestimate dendritic extent due to truncation, and differences in slicing orientation between 256 

regions may affect dendritic preservation. Together these results suggest that the FC represents a 257 

functionally and anatomically distinct hippocampal subregion. 258 

FC neurons exhibit substantial heterogeneity in intrinsic electrophysiological properties. In 259 

our analysis we segregated FC neurons into low and high excitability groups, although we 260 

acknowledge that this grouping does not mean that these are distinct subtypes, and our data may 261

their contribution to intrinsic firing properties in this understudied subregion. Although voltage-282

contribution of Kv-mediated conductances. These findings underscore the functional importance of 285 

Kv2 conductances in shaping intrinsic excitability in FC neurons and suggest that they may 286 

represent potential targets in conditions characterized by altered neuronal excitability, such as 287 

epilepsy (45, 46). 288

In addition to Kv2, we identified a substantial M-current in FC neurons, mediated by Kv7 289 

channels. These channels provide tonic inhibition and support spike-frequency adaptation, 290 

stabilizing the resting membrane potential and suppressing repetitive firing (37, 47), both in 291 

hippocampal and cortical neurons (38, 48) . Kv7-mediated currents also varied across FC neurons 292 

and were associated with intrinsic excitability, with smaller M-currents observed in more excitable 293 

neurons. Given the direct association of Kv7 mutations with epileptic encephalopathies and the 294 

clinical efficacy of Kv7 channel openers such as retigabine, these channels may serve as critical 295 

targets for modulating FC function under pathological conditions (49–51). The positive correlation 296 

between Kv2 and Kv7 function in FC neurons suggests a coordinated expression or functional 297 

coupling between these two potassium channel families, which together shape the excitability 298 

landscape of FC neurons. 299 

Recent in vivo studies have reported divergent findings regarding FC neuron firing. 300 

Recordings in freely behaving animals indicate sparse and delayed firing consistent with reduced 301 

excitability (12), whereas juxtacellular recordings under anesthesia report firing properties more 302 

similar to CA1 (11). These discrepancies may reflect differences in network state. Under 303 

anesthesia, reduced synaptic drive may mask intrinsic differences in excitability, whereas awake 304 

behavioral conditions engage stronger network activity. Notably, we found that FC neurons receive 305 

substantial excitatory synaptic input, with significantly increased sEPSC frequencies compared to 306 

CA1, yet they do not exhibit correspondingly high firing rates either in vivo from previous studies or 307 

ex vivo from our results. Within the FC population, synaptic input was inversely related to intrinsic 308 

excitability, with lower-excitability neurons receiving higher-frequency excitatory input. Our 309 

additional PCA analyses further support this relationship by showing that FC neurons occupy a 310 

continuum of electrophysiological states, structured along an axis defined by intrinsic excitability 311 

and synaptic drive, suggesting coordinated variation in intrinsic and synaptic properties. Given that 312 

intrinsic excitability in FC neurons is strongly governed by potassium conductances, particularly 313 

Kv2- and Kv7-mediated currents, these findings suggest that variability in ion channel composition 314 

defines the position of individual neurons along this functional axis. Neurons with stronger 315

Recent work has identified the fasciola cinereum as a critical node in seizure initiation and 327 

propagation, with both human intracranial recordings and animal models demonstrating a causal 328 

role for FC activity in epilepsy (18). While these studies establish the functional importance of the 329 

FC at the circuit level, the cellular mechanisms underlying this role have remained unclear. Our 330 

findings provide a potential biophysical framework linking intrinsic membrane properties to this 331 

function. The inverse relationship we observe between intrinsic excitability and synaptic drive 332 

suggests a coordinated organization in which increased input is balanced by intrinsic suppression 333 

of firing. In this context, perturbations of potassium channel function or synaptic balance could shift 334 

FC neurons toward hyperexcitability, potentially facilitating seizure initiation or propagation. 335 

Although direct circuit-level validation will be required, these results identify intrinsic and synaptic 336 

properties of FC neurons which may contribute to its previously identified role in epilepsy. 337 

Altogether, our results indicate that the fasciola cinereum is not merely a transitional zone 338 

but a distinct hippocampal subregion with specialized intrinsic properties. The combination of 339

Materials and Methods 348 

Mice 349 

All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee 350 

(IACUC) at Vanderbilt University and were conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of 351 

Health guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. C57BL/6J mice of either sex (postnatal 352 

day 21–60) were used for all experiments. Animals were housed under standard conditions (12 h 353 

light/dark cycle, food and water ad libitum) and monitored daily for health and well-being. Every 354 

effort was made to minimize animal suffering and the number of animals used. 355 

Hippocampal Slice Preparation 356 

Coronal hippocampal slices (280 μm) were prepared from postnatal day (P) 21–60 from C57BL/6J 357 

mice of either sex. Mice were deeply anesthetized with isoflurane and euthanized by decapitation. 358 

Brains were rapidly removed and placed in ice-cold, oxygenated N-methyl-D-glucamine (NMDG)-359

7.4, 300–310 mOsm). Coronal slices were cut using a vibratome (Leica VT-1200S) and transferred 362 

to a holding chamber containing oxygenated artificial cerebrospinal fluid (ACSF) at room 363 

temperature (24–26°C) for at least 1 h before recording. ACSF contained (in mM): 125 NaCl, 2.5 364 

KCl, 0.8 NaH ₂PO ₄, 26 NaHCO ₃, 3 CaCl ₂, 2 MgCl ₂, and 10 glucose, continuously bubbled with 95% 365

cutting solution. Slices (300 μm) were prepared in the horizontal plane using a vibrating microtome, 370 

an orientation selected to preserve the anatomical and synaptic connections spanning CA2. After 371 

recovery in ACSF, slices were transferred to the recording chamber and visualized under IR-DIC 372

Electrophysiology 386 

Recordings were performed at room temperature (24-26 o C) in a recording chamber continuously 387 

perfused with oxygenated ACSF. 388 

Neurons were recorded from the fasciola cinereum (FC), a small hippocampal subregion located 389 

at the dorsomedial edge of the hippocampal formation. The FC was identified in acute coronal 390

1.67 mm to 2.03 mm), together with previously published descriptions (11, 12, 16, 18). Specifically, 392 

recordings were restricted to the region adjacent to the third ventricle and medial to the CA1 393 

pyramidal layer, consistent with established FC boundaries. 394 

To minimize potential contamination from neighboring regions, cells located within or near the CA2 395 

pyramidal layer were systematically excluded. Only neurons within the visually defined FC region 396

and the resting membrane potential was stable and hyperpolarized. 405 

Cells were initially recorded in current-clamp mode to assess intrinsic membrane properties. 406 

Apparent input resistance was calculated using hyperpolarizing current steps ( −80 pA, 800 ms). 407 

Series resistance was monitored throughout the experiment, and recordings were discarded if 408 

resistance changed by more than 10%. To assess excitability, action potentials (APs) were evoked 409 

using a series of incremental depolarizing current injections (10 pA steps, 1000 ms duration), and 410 

input–output curves were generated based on AP counts. Action potential threshold, amplitude, 411 

and afterhyperpolarization were extracted using automated routines based on first derivative 412 

thresholding. 413

Principal component analysis (PCA) of intrinsic excitability 432 

Principal component analysis (PCA) was applied to electrophysiological data derived from the 433 

input–output (I-O) current-clamp protocol in FC neurons (number of action potentials across 434 

depolarizing current steps). For each FC neuron, the number of action potentials generated at each 435 

depolarizing current step was used as a feature. Prior to PCA, all features were standardized by z-436 

scoring. Two principal components were used for visualization which overall accounted for 90.96% 437 

of the variance (PC1: 67.61% variance, PC2: 23.35% variance) of the distribution of neurons in 438 

reduced feature space. The 70-200 pA range contributed most to PC1, while the 20-60 pA range 439 

contributed most to PC2. The 0 pA and 10 pA features did not contribute to either component. To 440 

facilitate comparison across the range of excitability profiles, k-means clustering (k = 2) was applied 441 

to the two principal components. This grouping was used solely as an analytical framework to 442 

contrast neurons at the lower and higher ends of the excitability spectrum and does not imply the 443 

existence of discrete neuronal subtypes. 444 

Cluster assignments were visualized in the two-dimensional PCA space. Subsequent analyses of 445 

intrinsic membrane properties and firing behavior were performed using this excitability-based 446 

grouping to illustrate variability across the population. 447

Principal component analysis of intrinsic and synaptic properties 448 

To examine the relationship between intrinsic properties and synaptic input across FC neurons, we 449 

performed two PCAs on a combined set of electrophysiological parameters. Input resistance, 450 

rheobase, RMP, sag amplitude, AP amplitude, AP threshold, mAHP, and average I-O spike count 451 

were used as features to represent intrinsic electrophysiological properties (PC1: 29.05% variance, 452 

PC2: 20.97% variance). sEPSC frequency, inter episode interval length, amplitude, and decay time 453 

were used as features to represent synaptic properties (PC1: 41.23% variance, PC2: 26.99% 454 

variance). All features were z-score standardized and two principal components were used for 455 

visualization for each PCA. These analyses allowed identification of the dominant axes of variability 456 

across the FC population. No clustering algorithms were applied in this analysis. Instead, the 457 

distribution of neurons in principal component space was used to assess whether intrinsic and 458 

synaptic properties were organized into discrete groups or varied continuously across the 459 

population. For visualization, the same low and high excitability cells noted in the PCA of intrinsic 460 

excitability were also noted in these graphs. 461
