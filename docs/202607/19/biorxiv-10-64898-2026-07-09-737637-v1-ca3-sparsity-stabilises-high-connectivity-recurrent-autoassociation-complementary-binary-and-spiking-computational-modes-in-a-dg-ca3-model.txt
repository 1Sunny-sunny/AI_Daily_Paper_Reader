The dentate gyrus (DG) decorrelates entorhinal inputs (pattern separation); area CA3 completes 32 

partial cues via recurrent autoassociation. The density of CA3 recurrent connectivity is contested, 33 

with estimates from ∼0.9% (Guzman et al., 2016) to ∼9–11% (Sammons et al., 2024). We ask how 34 

completion depends on recurrent connectivity ( CRC ) and whether the answer is intrinsic to CA3 35 

dynamics or inherited from the DG front-end. Using a trisynaptic model that crosses two DG 36 

implementations (a point-LIF network with Santhakumar et al., 2005 topology; an abstract fixed-37 

in-degree spiking network validated size-invariant to N = 10 7) with two CA3 autoassociators 38 

(binary k-WTA; spiking excitatory/inhibitory attractor) via burst-gated mossy-fiber detonators, we 39 

find: (i) completion in the binary CA3 improves monotonically with CRC and is robust across DG 40 

implementation; (ii) the spiking CA3 exhibits a runaway transition whose boundary is set by the 41 

product (active fraction × CRC ), is not rescued by stronger feedback inhibition ( 8×), is insensitive 42 

to input overlap, and is size-invariant ( N = 10 4–10 5); (iii) the two CA3 types have opposite failure 43 

modes (binary under-completes at low CRC ; spiking runs away at high active-fraction × CRC ) and a 44 

capacity/stability trade-off. Adult neurogenesis flips sign by the same logic: excitability-only young 45 

cells densify the code and collapse the spiking attractor, but if they recruit feedback inhibition they 46 

instead sparsen it and preserve recall. Consistent with classical sparse-coding attractor theory 47 

(Tsodyks and Feigel’man, 1988), we propose that the contested CA3 connectivity is better read as 48 

an implementation-mode trade-off, and that the empirically sparse activity of CA3 ( a ≈ 0.02 –0.05 )49 

is the condition that lets a highly recurrent network perform stable autoassociation. 50

## 1 Introduction 61 

Episodic memory rests on complementary neural codes . Efficient-coding theory holds that suc-62 

cessive stages of a processing hierarchy should reduce the redundancy of their inputs (Barlow, 63 

1961), a goal the brain realises in part through sparse codes in which few neurons are active at 64 

once (Olshausen and Field, 1996). The hippocampal trisynaptic circuit separates these operations 65 

anatomically: the dentate gyrus (DG) performs pattern separation , decorrelating similar en-66 

torhinal inputs onto a sparse granule-cell code (Hainmüller and Bartos, 2020; McAvoy et al., 2015; 67 

Leutgeb et al., 2007), while area CA3 performs pattern completion , recurrent autoassociation 68 

that reconstructs a stored pattern from a partial cue (Marr, 1971; Hopfield, 1982; Treves and Rolls, 69 

1994; Guzman et al., 2021; Rolls and Kesner, 2016), a function supported in vivo by the recall deficit 70 

of CA3-NMDA-receptor knockouts under partial cues (Nakazawa et al., 2002). Separation is thus 71 

a redundancy-reduction operation — the view we adopt by scoring it with Υ∗

> R

, a correlation-based 72 

surrogate inspired by the redundancy-reduction index of Bird et al. (2024) — and completion is an 73 

attractor-retrieval operation; sparse activity is the currency both rely on and, we will argue, the 74 

variable that sets the stability of the CA3 autoassociator. 75 

Beyond static patterns, the hippocampus also codes the temporal order of experience. Granule 76 

cells integrate convergent perforant-path streams with pathway-specific short-term plasticity to 77 

become sensitive to input timing and history (Kamijo et al., 2014; Hayakawa et al., 2015; Nakajima 78 

et al., 2024; Kamijo et al., 2026), and a spatiotemporal learning rule confers strong sequence 79 

separation on feedforward synapses while Hebbian recurrence supports completion (Tsukada and 80 

Pan, 2005). Downstream, the Tsuda–Kuroda Cantor-coding hypothesis proposes that temporal 81 

sequences relayed from CA3 are encoded hierarchically in a fractal partition of the CA1 state space 82 

(Tsuda, 2001; Tsuda and Kuroda, 2001). Because such a representation requires the receiving 83 

dynamics to remain contracting rather than to run away, the stability of recurrent CA3 plausibly 84 

conditions temporal coding downstream — a link we return to in the Discussion. 85

3.3 Spiking-CA3 recall-collapse (“runaway”) phase boundary 169 

The spiking attractor undergoes a runaway transition (recall collapse) governed by the product of 170 

CA3 active fraction and recurrent in-degree (Fig. 1): with active fraction 0.026, recall stays 171 

∼0.9–0.96 across CRC = 18 –400 ; at fraction 0.11 it collapses for CRC ≥ 150 ; at fraction 0.34 it stays 172 

high at low CRC but collapses to chance for CRC ≥ 150 . Empirically the boundary is organized by 173 

the product, lying near frac × CRC ≈ 20 –40 , though the critical product is not strictly constant: 174 

it drifts modestly with active fraction (collapse begins near product ≈16 at fraction 0.11 but only 175 

by product ≈50 at fraction 0.335 ), so frac × CRC is an approximate organizer over the tested range 176 

rather than an exact invariant. The qualitative message holds — sparse coding prevents collapse 177 

even at high connectivity. Stronger feedback inhibition shifts recall monotonically but does not 178 

prevent runaway over an 8× range of winh (e.g. recall 0.71 → 0.82 at CRC = 150 , 0.24 → 0.28 179 

at CRC = 400 ), and the boundary is insensitive to input overlap (Extended Data Fig. 5); 180 

the collapse is thus an intrinsic property of the recurrent load that feedback inhibition cannot 181 

rescue. Importantly it is not a population-rate explosion: across the transition the CA3 active 182 

fraction stays clamped near its inhibition-set value ( ∼0.11 at fixed mossy-fiber drive, essentially 183 

constant from CRC = 18 to 400 ) while recall falls from 1.0 to 0.24 . The failure is therefore a loss 184 

of recall specificity — attractor crosstalk / pattern interference as additional recurrent links bind 185 

the stored patterns together — rather than runaway firing; we retain the term “runaway” only 186 

as a convenient label for this recall-collapse transition. The stable/collapse classification is 187 

size-invariant across three decades (NGC = 10 4–10 6; at CRC = 200 the collapse persists with 188 

recall 0.54 → 0.40 → 0.27 at 10 4/10 5/10 6, never recovering to the stable ∼1.0; Fig. 2), though we 189 

note this is a qualitative invariance: in the collapse regime recall is seed-dominated (SD ≈0.1–0.27 ,190 

straddling chance), so the boundary location, not the precise recall value, is what reproduces across 191 

size. 192

## 4 Discussion 231 

Two messages. First , CA3 sparse coding is the control variable that stabilises high-232 

connectivity recurrent autoassociation : the recall-collapse boundary is organized chiefly by 233 

active-fraction × CRC — approximately, over the range tested, with only a weak residual depen-234 

dence of the critical product on fraction — and not by the other factors we varied (not inhibition 235 

strength, not input similarity, not network size). A highly recurrent CA3 (Sammons-level c) is 236 

functional provided the code is sparse ; the empirically sparse activity of CA3 ( a ≈ 0.02 –0.05 ;237 

Rolls, 2013), consistent with the sparse, lognormally skewed in-vivo firing of hippocampal principal 238 

cells (Mizuseki and Buzsáki, 2013; Leutgeb et al., 2007), is exactly the regime that permits dense 239 

recurrence without collapse. We note one caveat that our sweep cannot settle: how the modelled 240 

CRC maps onto the biological recurrent in-degree is itself uncertain, so whether a Sammons-level 241 

in-degree would keep real CA3 below the boundary at a ≈ 0.026 remains an open question (the 242 

effective in-degree implied by our operating point is quantified in future work). That frac × CRC 243 

is the controlling product is expected on theoretical grounds — it is the mean number of active 244 

recurrent inputs per cell, the mean-field drive whose growth low activity is classically known to 245 

bound (Tsodyks and Feigel’man, 1988; Brunel, 2000). A back-of-envelope balance estimate even re-246 

covers the absolute scale: a cell crosses threshold when its mean recurrent drive, (frac × CRC ) we, rec ,247 

approaches the distance to threshold inflated by a leak/inhibition factor κ > 1, giving a critical 248 

product (frac ×CRC )c ≈ κ (Vth −EL)/w e, rec . With Vth −EL = 20 mV and we, rec = 2 mV this is ≈ 10 κ,249 

i.e. 20 –40 for κ ≈ 2–4 — matching the observed boundary, with the residual fraction-dependence 250 

(above) attributable to the fraction-dependence of recruited inhibition and effective gain. Our con-251

A biological corollary, now resolved by a control: adult hippocampal neurogenesis is implicated 273 

behaviourally in pattern separation (Clelland et al., 2009; Sahay et al., 2011). In an excitability-274 

only caricature, young granule cells densify DG activity, collapsing an E/I-attractor CA3 but not 275 

a hard-sparsity one (Fig. 3); when they instead recruit feedback inhibition — the dominant 276 

experimental account (McAvoy et al., 2015; Temprana et al., 2015) — the dentate code sparsens 277 

and recall is preserved (Fig. 4). The sign is thus set by one assumption, and the robust statement 278 

is the mode-level one: a CA3 in E/I-attractor mode is sensitive to whatever changes DG 279 

activity, whereas a k-WTA CA3 is buffered against it . Because the experimental literature 280 

favours net sparsening (McAvoy et al., 2015; Sahay et al., 2011), the biologically realistic prediction 281
